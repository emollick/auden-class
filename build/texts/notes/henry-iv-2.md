# henry-iv-2: Shakespeare, Henry IV, Part 2

- Source: Project Gutenberg no. 1518 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1518), cached as `.cache/texts/pg1518_henry_iv_2.txt` ("most recently updated September 19, 2025"; a fresh download on 2 Oct 2026 was byte for byte the same). Gutenberg's own modern-spelling text; it names no printed edition.
- meta: `title` "Henry IV, Part 2", `translator` null, `verse` false, `year` 1600: the year the play was first printed (the quarto). No first performance is recorded; it is usually thought to have been written about 1597-98.
- Dropped: the title and author lines, the Contents, the Dramatis Personæ, the line "SCENE: England.", and the marks of the plain-text file.
- Parts: `induction` (label `Induction`, no group), one part a scene `a1s1` to `a5s5`, `epilogue` (label `Epilogue`, no group). 5 acts and 19 scenes (3, 4, 2, 5, 5); the script throws on anything else.
- Act 4 has five scenes in this source (the forest in three, then the Jerusalem Chamber and "Another chamber"). Editions that divide Act 4 into two or three scenes will number differently; nothing was forced.
- The Induction has a place line under its heading, as the scenes have; it is the first `d` block. The Epilogue is three paragraphs of prose with no speaker's name printed over it (the list of persons calls him a Dancer), so the part has no `s` block.
- Counts: 21 parts, 27,354 words, 85 pages; 902 speeches, 1,640 lines of verse in 337 blocks, 607 paragraphs of prose, 171 stage directions. Largest part `a2s4`, 3,122 words.
- Speakers: 50 labels as printed (joint ones with an ampersand: GLOUCESTER & CLARENCE, PRINCE & POINS).
- Cut by the same code as Hamlet: see `notes/hamlet.md` for how lines, verse, prose and one-line speeches are read, and for the checks. The word-for-word check against the source passes; check-text has no problems and no warnings.
- One-line speeches or pieces of speeches: 484 (131 settled as verse, 353 as prose).

## Doubts

- The year (see above).
- One-line speeches where verse and prose meet. Against my own reading: `a2s1` 121 and 127 (Falstaff breaking in on the Chief Justice and Gower). Falstaff's cries to the new King in `a5s5` (45, 49, 55) are read as verse, with the verse round them.
- Pistol: his speeches are verse wherever the source sets them in lines, and his one-liners follow what is near them.
- Short prose speeches the source sets as two lines, kept as lines: `a1s2` 38, `a5s1` 3 and 42; `a2s4` 28 is the end of a ballad and a line of prose in one block.
