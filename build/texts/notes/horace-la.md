# horace-la

Source: Project Gutenberg no. 46938. The research file was named for Anthon, but it is not Anthon's edition. The title page in the file (first lines after the START marker) reads: "The Works of Horace, with English Notes, by the Rev. A. J. Macleane, M.A. ... revised and edited by Reginald H. Chase, A.M. Boston: Allyn and Bacon. 1895", entered 1856, "Twentieth edition". The cache copy is `pg46938_horace_latin.txt`.

Translator: none (`translator: null`, `lang: 'la'`). `year` is -23 (23 BC, Books I-III; the hymn is of 17 BC and Book IV of about 13 BC), after the pattern of the Italian Dante, whose year is the year of the work. The date of this edition, from the file's title page, is 1895.

It cuts clean, so The Latin Library was not needed. In this book the notes stand together at the end, after the whole text; the Odes run from "CARMINUM LIBER PRIMUS." to "EPODON LIBER" with nothing in them but headings and verse. Taken off: the margin line numbers (one on every fifth line, 582 of them; the script checks that each is the number of the line it stands on), and one pair of italic marks (IV.6 line 17). Double spaces after a full stop inside a line are closed. No brackets, no notes, no footnote marks in the Odes.

Dropped: everything else in the book (life of Horace, Epodes, Satires, Epistles, Ars Poetica, the notes).

Parts: one for each ode, with the same ids as `horace` (`o1-01` to `o4-15`, `cs`); labels `I.1` and so on, the hymn "Carmen Saeculare"; group `Liber I` to `Liber IV`. All 103 odes are here, so four parts (o1-25, o2-05, o3-20, o4-10) have no partner in Conington, and III.6 has 48 lines to his 36.

Counts: 38 + 20 + 30 + 15 = 103 odes and the hymn, 104 parts, 3,110 lines, 13,602 words. The script checks the number of lines of every ode against the table the editions agree on.

Stanzas: the book leaves no space between stanzas. Divided into fours (Meineke's rule), III.12 by threes, IV.8 eight fours and a couplet, so that the blocks answer Conington's block for block. Indenting is not kept.

Spelling is the edition's: j and v ("Jam", "juvat"), diaeresis on some vowels, and I.2 line 19 ends with a divided word carried to line 20, as the metre requires and editions print.

Check-text warnings: three parts under 40 words (I.30, I.38, III.22). They are odes of eight short lines.

Doubtful: nothing in the text. The choice of -23 for `year` and of Latin group names is mine.
