# gotterdammerung

Source: Project Gutenberg no. 49507, "Siegfried & The Twilight of the Gods". The file holds both works; only the second is taken.

Translator and date: both in the file. The Gutenberg header has "Translator: Margaret Armour"; the title page (the first lines after the START marker) reads "Siegfried & The Twilight of the Gods, by Richard Wagner, with illustrations by Arthur Rackham, translated by Margaret Armour, London William Heinemann, New York Doubleday Page & Co, 1911". So `translator: 'Margaret Armour'`, `year: 1911`. The script checks those lines.

Dropped: the title page and the list of illustrations; the whole of Siegfried; under the half-title of the Twilight, the list of characters, the "scenes of action" and two ornaments; the 20 illustration markers from the Prelude on, with their captions (the captions repeat lines of the text). Nothing stands after the last direction.

Parts: `prelude`, `act-1`, `act-2`, `act-3`, as the book divides it (PRELUDE, THE FIRST ACT, ...). The book marks no scenes; a change of scene is a stage direction. 4 parts, 13,587 words, 2,026 lines of verse, 58 pages (prelude 256 lines, act-1 577, act-2 635, act-3 558). 412 `s`, 481 `v`, 282 `d`.

Speakers: the book prints the names in black letter in the margin; the file gives them in capitals with a small "and" between two names, and so do I. Two forms the file does not capitalise throughout (4 places) are put like the rest. The script holds the table of names with the number of speeches of each and throws on any other.

Directions: in the file a direction opens with a bracket that is never closed and is in italic; the descriptions of the scene have no bracket. 223 stand alone, 59 beside a name (these come after the `s` block). Brackets and italic marks are taken off.

Verse: each line of the file is a line; indenting is not kept. A blank line inside a speech makes a new `v` block (9 places; at all nine the scan has a white line too, by the positions of its lines); where the blank was only the place of a dropped illustration the verse is joined again (5 places).

Checked against the book. The file was compared with a scan of the 1911 Heinemann edition (archive.org `siegfriedtwiligh00wagn`: its OCR for every verse line and every direction, the page images wherever the two disagreed) and the order of the speakers with the German libretto. The file departs from the book in the places below; the book's reading is restored, each through a table in the script that throws if the place is not found exactly once.
- Names, 4: one misspelt; one pair of names with the wrong second name; one speech of Brünnhilde's under Waltraute's name; one chorus without its "The".
- Stage directions, 7 words (six of them the long s of the book's italic taken for f, or the reverse; one word with a letter dropped) and a stray mark.
- Verse, 9: a word split in two, a hyphen added, and seven points of punctuation (among them two broken ellipses and a stray character).
- Six short speeches on one page of Act 3 that the file runs together like prose are divided into lines again as the book divides them (seen on the page images).
- One word turned over at the end of a long line (Act 2) is joined to its line.
- At the end of Act 1 a sentence of a direction that the book sets in the margin beside Siegfried's last speech had been tacked on to the direction that closes the act; it is put back.

Supplied: one name. Gutrune's speech at the opening of the last scene has no name over it in the book itself (seen on the page image); the direction before it says she comes in, and the German gives the speech to her. An `s` block GUTRUNE is added so that the speech has a speaker. It is the only word not in the book.

Left as the book has them: one phrase in the direction at the oath in Act 1 that looks like a misprint of the book ("pace", probably for "space"); the spacing of the ellipses; the quotation marks.

Doubtful:
- The supplied name, above.
- Punctuation inside the stage directions was compared with the scan only through its OCR, which reads about four fifths of their words (the italic is hard for it). Where the OCR's mark differed from the file's I looked at the page, and the file was right; once it ends a direction with a full stop that the book seems to lack, and that is left.
- The title page in the file misspells the American publisher (Pace for Page); it is not part of the text taken.

Check-text: no problems, no warnings.
