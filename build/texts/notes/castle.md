# castle: Kafka, The Castle, tr. Willa and Edwin Muir (1930)

- Source: the Standard Ebooks edition (https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/franz-kafka/the-castle/willa-muir_edwin-muir), first released 1 January 2026. Its colophon says it is the translation of 1930, made from digital scans at the Internet Archive. `meta.year` is 1930.
- The cached file, `.cache/texts/se_kafka_castle_muir.txt`, is the file that was supplied: the text of the edition's single-page view, a paragraph to a line. It carries no address and no imprint or colophon. Standard Ebooks publishes no plain-text file, so `fetchText` is given the address of that single-page view (`…/text/single-page`), which is XHTML; `source.url` in the index is the edition's own page. The script reads either form. Tried on 2 Oct 2026 with the cache emptied: the page was fetched (718,549 bytes) and gave output byte for byte the same as the supplied file gives. The supplied file was then put back.
- Parts: 22 for 18 chapters, no groups. The book numbers its chapters and gives them no titles: `c01` to `c18`, labelled `Chapter 1` to `Chapter 18`. Chapter 15 is about 24,000 words and the book itself divides it into an opening and four titled sections, so it is five parts: `c15` (the opening, labelled `Chapter 15`) and `c15-2` to `c15-5`, labelled `Chapter 15: Amalia’s Secret`, `… Amalia’s Punishment`, `… Petitions`, `… Olga’s Plans`. A reading of "chapter 15" has to name all five, or run from `c15` to `c15-5`. To have one part instead, the four titles would become `h` blocks.
- Counts: 96,799 words, 277 pages; 302 paragraphs and one quoted block. `c13` is 10,582 words and has no division in the book; everything else is under 8,000. The script throws unless it finds 18 chapters in order, the four section titles in chapter 15, and 303 paragraphs, one of them the letter set apart.
- Word totals compared: the source between the title and the Additional Note is 96,824 words; the parts hold 96,799. The 25 between them are the 18 chapter numbers and the four section titles. The script checks this sum. A separate comparison word by word (not part of the build) found the parts and the source the same and in the same order apart from those headings.
- Beyond the translation's text the supplied file holds: the reader's navigation and a list of contents at its head; Edwin Muir's Introductory Note (To the First American Edition); the title; then, after the novel, Max Brod's Additional Note and one endnote to it. All of that is dropped. The first block is the opening of chapter 1 and the last is the end of chapter 18, where this translation ends.
- Set apart: the edition sets one thing apart, Klamm's first letter in chapter 2 (`c02[8]`), which is a `q` block. The supplied file cannot show that, so there the letter is found by its opening words and by the paragraph that follows it; the page marks it as a quotation.
- Characters: Standard Ebooks puts an invisible word joiner before every dash and on either side of the hair space before an ellipsis (487 in all), a hair space before an ellipsis and between quotation marks that meet (5), and, in the page though not in the supplied file, a no-break space after "Mr." (7). None is in the book as printed. The first two are taken out and the third is made an ordinary space.
- check-text: no problems; nine warnings, all of long paragraphs (`c03[1]`, `c09[4]`, `c10[1]`, `c13[5]`, `c15[2]`, `c15-2[0]`, `c15-3[0]`, `c15-5[0]`, `c18[0]`; the longest 32,253 characters). Each is one paragraph in the source; Kafka's paragraphs run to many pages.
- Doubt: Standard Ebooks modernises some spelling and hyphenation as a rule of the house, and this text was not compared with the printing of 1930. Later English editions add chapters and passages translated by others; they are not in this one.
- Tools: check-text does not look for invisible characters (word joiner, hair space, no-break space, marks of direction). These came with two of the four sources here and are dealt with in the scripts.
