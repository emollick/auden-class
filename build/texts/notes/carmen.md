# carmen

Source: Internet Archive, item `carmen1910bize` (a scan from the Harold B. Lee Library, Brigham Young University), the plain OCR text `carmen1910bize_djvu.txt`. Not Project Gutenberg: the file is raw machine reading of the page images.

What it is: "Carmen. The Music by Georges Bizet. French-English Edition (with the music of the principal airs). Published by Fred Rullman, at the Theatre Ticket Office, No. 111 Broadway, New York." The cover reads "The original Italian, French or German libretto with a correct English translation" and names Andreas Dippel as general manager; the advertisements are of a Chicago firm. Nothing in the book says "Metropolitan Opera". French and English.

This is the opera with the sung recitatives (Guiraud's), not the original with spoken dialogue: there is no prose in it. The `note` in the index says so.

Translator: not named anywhere in the book.

Date: `year` is 1910, as I was told to set it. That is the date of the Archive's catalogue record ("New York : F. Rullman", 1910); I found no year printed on the cover or the title page.

The two languages: each page has the French in its left column and the English in its right; the file gives the columns one after another. The script tells the English lines from the French by their words (common words of each language, counted block by block; a block the words do not settle takes the language of the blocks round it), and throws if a block holds both or cannot be placed. Twenty-one places are settled by hand in the table `ASSIGN` (the trumpet's syllables and the like, which count as neither language, and names the scan read as a column of their own). While writing the script I compared its answer with the side of the page each line stands on: they agree on every line of text.

Dropped: the cover, the advertisements, the title page, the prose Argument, the list of persons, all the French, the running heads and page numbers, the numbers of the stanzas of the two songs of Act 2, "End of the Opera", and the printed music that follows it.

Parts: `act-1` to `act-4` (11, 5, 5 and 2 scenes; scene headings are `h` blocks). 4 parts, 9,147 words, 1,172 lines, 33 pages; 415 `s`, 435 `v`, 155 `d`, 23 `h`.

Verse: everything spoken or sung is set in lines in the book, so all of it is `v`. A line too long for the column is turned over; the turned part is joined to its line (it begins with a small letter; eight turned parts begin with a capital, table `TURNED`). In 28 places the printer tucked the last word or two of a long line into the end of the line above or below, after a bracket; they are put back (table `TUCKED`). A `v` block ends where a direction or a new speaker comes; the book's stanzas are not otherwise marked.

Speakers: the book cuts most names short; the script gives them in full, in capitals, from a table (`PERSONS`), the full names being those of the book's list of persons. Groups are as printed. "&" between two names is written "and". One name is supplied (table `UNNAMED`): the second stanza of Carmen's song in Act 2 follows a refrain sung by two others, and the book prints only the stanza's number over it. Twenty-nine names that the scan read apart from their lines are put back before them (table `MOVED`).

Stage directions: the book opens a direction with a bracket and never closes it; at the head of a scene directions stand without brackets (table `PLAIN`); after a name they stand in round brackets. All are `d` blocks without the brackets. A direction at the end of a line of verse follows that line. Where a bracketed direction ends is told by its sentences, with a table (`MORE`) for the twelve lines that go on after a full stop; I checked these against the indenting on the pages.

Marks: the old printer's space before ; : ! ? is taken out, dashes are closed up to the words as the book has them, and the quotation marks and apostrophes are curled. The dance song of Act 4 is printed with a quotation mark at the head of every line; only the first and the closing mark are kept.

Corrections: I read the whole English column against the page images, leaf by leaf, and then the built text from end to end. 350 misread places are mended by the table `FIXES` (line of the file, wrong, right); two lines the scan broke are joined (`JOIN`) and one line it gave out of order is put back (`AFTER`). 52 words broken at the end of a line are joined; two hyphens that belong to their words are kept. Every word not found in the other English texts of the course was looked at (list `KNOWN`).

Slips of the printer mended (table `MISPRINTS`, five): three misspelt words, one word that lost a letter, and one question mark that should be an exclamation mark at the end of a cheer. Other oddities are kept as printed, and I checked each on the page: an exclamation mark after two questions (one in Act 2, one in Act 3), "thou are" once and "thou will" twice, a full stop where a comma is wanted in a few places, a line with no stop at the end of a speech in three places, the last word of the list of persons at the head of Act 3 in the singular, and the Spanish and French words in the spelling the book gives them.

Doubtful:
- In Act 1, scene 9, the English has two speeches of the Officer running, with a gap between them; the answer that stands between them in the French is not in the English column. Kept as printed.
- The last word of Carmen's song in Act 1, scene 10: the page shows the word with one l and a mark after it that I read as an exclamation mark; it may be a broken letter, and then the line ends without any mark.
- Punctuation marks read from worn type, where the page allows more than one reading (file lines 542, 1939, 2114, 2961, 3857, 5116, 5627): I gave the mark the page seems to show.
- In Act 2, scene 3, two lines stand against a brace with the names of Mercedes and Frasquita; by the French the second of them belongs to another speaker. Kept as printed.
