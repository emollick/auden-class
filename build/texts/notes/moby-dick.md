# moby-dick: Melville, Moby-Dick; or, The Whale (1851)

- Source: Project Gutenberg no. 2701 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2701), cached as `.cache/texts/pg2701_moby_dick.txt`. It calls itself a combination of two earlier e-texts compared with a printed copy, which it does not name; `meta.year` 1851 is the first edition. A second transcription, no. 15 (also supplied; `.cache/texts/pg15_moby_dick.txt`, fetched from `https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15/pg15.txt` if the cache is empty), is used only to say where lines of verse end. Fresh downloads of both on 2 Oct 2026 were byte for byte the cached files.
- Parts: 138. `etymology`, `extracts`, `c001` to `c135`, `epilogue`; no groups. The script throws unless it finds 135 chapters in order between the Extracts and the Epilogue. Chapter titles keep the source's capitals ("The Pequod Meets The Rachel").
- Counts: 211,378 words, 604 pages; 2,444 paragraphs, 14 verse blocks, 36 quoted blocks, 17 stage directions, 7 headings. No part is over 8,000 words; the shortest chapter, `c122`, is 44 words in the book.
- Word totals compared: the source from the heading ETYMOLOGY to the end of the Gutenberg text is 212,058 words; the parts hold 211,378. The difference is 668 words of headings and 12 asterisks in two rows that mark breaks (`c032`, `c054`). The script checks this sum. A separate comparison word by word (not part of the build) found the parts and the source the same and in the same order apart from those and the brackets taken off eleven stage directions.
- Dropped: the Gutenberg header and licence, the title lines, the contents, the transcribers' note, the chapter headings (they are the labels), the heading EXTRACTS where it is printed a second time, the two rows of asterisks, and two invisible marks of direction round the Hebrew word in the Etymology.
- Melville's footnotes: the source sets each apart, as a paragraph beginning with an asterisk straight after the paragraph that carries the mark. They are kept there as `q` blocks, 22 notes in 27 paragraphs (two notes in chapter 42 and one in chapter 45 run to more than one paragraph), and the asterisk is left on the mark and on the note so that the two can be matched. There are 23 marks: chapter 24 marks its one note twice. Rows of asterisks inside a paragraph stay as the source has them (five places: quotations with words left out in the Etymology and chapters 32 and 45, and the end of one paragraph in chapter 54).
- Verse: no. 2701 runs the lines of the hymns and songs together. The 13 stanzas (55 lines: chapters 9, 22, 35, 39, 40, 119) have their words from no. 2701 and their line ends from no. 15, found by aligning the two; the script throws if a stanza cannot be found there or is found divided in two ways. The table of thirteen languages in the Etymology is a `v` block, a line to a language.
- Extracts: one `p` block to each paragraph of the source, with the attribution in the same block as the source gives it: 82 paragraphs for 80 quotations (Cowper and Scoresby each run to two). Verse among the extracts stays run together, as the source has it.
- Lines under a heading: seven lines that the source sets by themselves under a heading and that belong to it are `h` blocks of level 3, first in their parts: who supplied the Etymology and the Extracts, the scene of chapters 39 and 108, the cast of chapter 40, where the story of chapter 54 was told, and the second half of the title of chapter 100. They are not in the labels, which follow the source's own headings and contents.
- Other blocks: in the chapters written as scenes (36 to 40, 108, 119 to 122, 127, 129) a paragraph all in italics is a `d` block without its brackets; speakers' names stay at the head of their speeches, as the source prints them. Six displayed documents (a playbill, three tablets, a mark, a list of stores) and the epigraphs of chapters 90, 91 and the Epilogue are `q` blocks.
- check-text: no problems, no warnings.
- Doubt: no. 2701 has no dedication (to Hawthorne); no. 15 has it. It was not added, since the parts were named in the brief; it could go in as a `q` block or a part of its own, from the cached no. 15.
- Doubt: no. 15 marks a break with a row of asterisks in three more places (chapters 47, 50, 94) where no. 2701 has nothing. Breaks are not represented in either case; no words are concerned.
- Doubt: the source's spelling is kept even where the two transcriptions differ; the differences seen in the verse are of a letter or two.
