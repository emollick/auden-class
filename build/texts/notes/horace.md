# horace

Source: Project Gutenberg no. 5432, "The Odes and Carmen Saeculare of Horace", translated into English verse by John Conington.

Edition, translator, date: the title page in the file (the first lines after the START marker) reads "Translated into English verse by John Conington, M.A., Corpus Professor of Latin in the University of Oxford. Third edition." The file prints no date anywhere. The third edition is London, Bell and Daldy, 1865: that comes from a scan of the edition (archive.org, odesandcarmensc00flacgoog), not from the file. `year` is 1863, the year of the translation as the task gave it; the file does not say so either.

Dropped: the title page, the three prefaces (first, second and third editions), the notes at the end (from "NOTES." to "FINIS."). Nothing else stands in the file.

Parts: one for each ode, `o1-01` to `o4-15`, then `cs`. Label: book, number, and the Latin opening words that head the ode. The file gives those headings in capitals; the book prints them in italic with capitals only for the first word and proper names, and the labels follow the book (the names are listed in the script). Group `Book I` to `Book IV`; the hymn has no group and is labelled "The Secular Hymn" (the book heads it "Carmen Saeculare").

Counts: 99 odes and the hymn, 100 parts, 3,030 lines, 19,244 words. Conington left out one ode in each book, as his preface says: I.25, II.5, III.20, IV.10. So Book I has 37 (1-24, 26-38), Book II 19 (1-4, 6-20), Book III 29 (1-19, 21-30), Book IV 14 (1-9, 11-15). He also left three stanzas out of III.6 (36 lines against the Latin 48). Every other ode has exactly as many lines as the Latin, and the script checks each against the table of the Latin.

Stanzas: neither the file nor the book leaves a space between stanzas; the stanza shows only in the indenting. The script divides every ode into fours, which is how the Latin goes and how Conington's rhymes go (checked by rhyme on all odes). Exceptions: III.12 by threes; IV.8 (34 lines) eight fours and a closing couplet; III.9, the dialogue, which the file does divide, by its six speeches, each an `s` block (HORACE, LYDIA, then H and L) and a `v` block. Indenting is not kept. This division is mine, not the printer's.

Mended, each checked against the scan of the 1865 edition and scans of the 1870 and 1872 printings:
- 24 slips of the file in the verse (wrong or dropped words, wrong stops, an opening word left in capitals, three names typed "AE-"). They are listed in the script by ode and line (SLIPS). The largest is IV.11 line 2, where the file drops the last two words of the line.
- 2 slips in the headings (I.12 and I.28).
- Four lines of I.11 and I.18 that the file wraps are joined to the lines they belong to.

Left as the file has them:
- Nine words the file gives in capitals where the book has italics (listed in the script).
- Ligatures as "ae" and "oe", as the file has them.
- Two misprints that are in the 1865 book itself, not slips of the file: III.26 line 1 (a doubled possessive) and the missing full stop at the end of IV.14.
- The heading of III.21 as Conington prints it ("O nate mecum"; Horace wrote "nata").
- Where the 1865 text differs from later editions in a stop or a capital, the file's (1865) reading stands.

Doubtful: the stanza division (see above). The reader numbers verse every third line by default; for these odes `every: 4` in the index would suit better, but that field is not in the contract and I have not set it.
