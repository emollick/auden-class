# pascal: Pascal, Pensées, tr. W. F. Trotter (1904)

- Source: Project Gutenberg no. 18269 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18269), cached as `.cache/texts/pg18269_pascal.txt` (a fresh download on 2 Oct 2026 was byte for byte the same). The file is made from the 1958 Dutton paperback, which reprints the Everyman's Library text with T. S. Eliot's introduction. Neither the file nor the Gutenberg page names the translator.
- Date of the translation, `meta.year` 1904: Trotter's version first came out from J. M. Dent, London, in 1904 (Temple Classics); Everyman's Library reprinted it in 1931 with Eliot's introduction. Where found: the Online Books Page (University of Pennsylvania), in its list for "Trotter, W. F. (William Finlayson), 1871-1945", records Pascal's "Thoughts", J. M. Dent, 1904, from HathiTrust record 012454972; a web search also returned the catalogue of Marsh's Library, Dublin, with the same imprint and series. HathiTrust itself answered 403 and I did not try it again. The reprints I found on the Internet Archive are later (Harvard Classics, 1910; Modern Library, 1941). Nothing I found supports the 1908 of the research notes.
- 923 fragments, not 924. Trotter's own note before Section I says he moved fragment 514 of the French (Brunschvicg) edition into his Notes and renumbered all that follow. It is in his note 195, with his reasons for doubting that it belongs to the Pensées. So his numbers run 1 to 923 without a break (the number 924 occurs nowhere in the file); up to 513 they are Brunschvicg's, and after that one less. The script expects 923 and throws at any gap or repeat. As the notes are dropped, that one fragment is not in the site's text.
- Parts: the fourteen Sections, `s01` to `s14`, labelled `Section I: Thoughts on Mind and on Style` and so on. The titles are the book's (it prints them in capitals; the script checks each against the file). Fragments in each: 59, 124, 58, 49, 48, 86, 130, 33, 53, 51, 44, 66, 54, 68. Sections II (14,119 words), VII (14,054) and XI (9,519) are longer than the 8,000 words aimed at; the brief asked for one part to a Section.
- Blocks: each fragment opens with an `h` block, level 3, that is its number; its paragraphs follow as `p`. There are three `v` blocks: the three-line numbered list in 513, and two small diagrams (571 and 590) which the source draws with braces, rules and strokes. In the diagrams the words are kept, a row to a line, and the drawn lines are not (in 571 one brace stays after the total; in 590 the upright stroke stays between the two names). They no longer look like the printed page.
- Counts: 14 parts, 94,904 words, 269 pages; 923 headings, 2,017 paragraphs, 3 verse blocks.
- Word totals compared: the source body, from the line SECTION I to the line before NOTES, is 94,988 words; the parts hold 94,904. The 84 between them are the Section headings (81 words) and three drawn strokes in the diagrams. The script checks this sum; a separate comparison word by word found the two the same and in the same order.
- Dropped: Eliot's Introduction (about 5,060 words, in copyright) and its lettered footnotes; title pages; the Contents; the translator's NOTE before Section I; the 380 note markers; the NOTES (about 6,700 words); the INDEX; italics marks; and the plus signs with which the file marks transliterated Greek (in 70 and 631).
- Square brackets are part of the text and are kept. The translator's NOTE explains them: passages Pascal erased are in square brackets, and so are words the French editor added or corrected, those being also in italics. Since italics are not kept, the two kinds now look alike. One closing bracket in 154 has no partner in the source; kept as printed.
- Italics in the source sometimes open in one paragraph and close several later (the Latin lines in 513), so the lib's `clean` would leave a stray mark; the script removes every underscore instead.
- check-text: no problems, no warnings.
- Suggested line for the reader, if one is wanted (`index.note` is shown by `reader.js` but is not in the contract, so I did not set it): square brackets enclose what Pascal struck out or what his French editor supplied; the numbers are Brunschvicg's as far as 513 and one behind his after that.
