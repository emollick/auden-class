### 1. Works with no free public-domain English text

**Required reading**

- **T. S. Eliot, The Family Reunion** is the only required title with no public-domain text at all in the United States. It is protected through 2034 (renewal R386668). - **Kierkegaard, Fear and Trembling**: no complete English translation is free. ### 2. Things that may surprise

- The Castle in the Muirs' 1930 translation is already a finished Standard Ebooks edition. It is not on Project Gutenberg or Faded Page.
- A public-domain English version of both Baudelaire journals (Shipley, 1919) sits inside a Gutenberg anthology, eleven years before Isherwood's. Nine of its numbered entries are absent, and its numbering does not match the French text on Gutenberg.
- A Season in Hell was printed complete in English in The Dial for July 1920. It is unsigned there and is attributed to the magazine's co-owner, J. S. Watson Jr.
- Conington's Horace is not complete: four odes (I.25, II.5, III.20, IV.10) are omitted.
- Gutenberg #3023, catalogued with no translator, is Anna Swanwick's Faust (the Harvard Classics text). Gutenberg #63203 is Blackie's, not Swanwick's.
- The Gutenberg Pensees is the Everyman text with T. S. Eliot's introduction, which is the edition Auden's sheet names. The file never names the translator, and its last fragment (924) has no number heading.
- The Gutenberg text of Pusey's Augustine has book headings only and no chapter numbers.
- The openly readable Internet Archive scan of The Family Reunion is labelled 'out of copyright' by the contributing library. The label is wrong: the play was renewed as a drama.
- Many Internet Archive books that look borrowable are restricted to print-disabled readers. That is why Patterns of Culture has no usable copy and why the 1937 Plato To-day and the 1936 Allegory of Love had to be replaced by later printings.
- Two Internet Archive items catalogued as the Loeb Horace are Latin school editions with no translation.
- The Metropolitan Opera librettos are not what a modern reader expects: the Don Giovanni stops at the Don's damnation and omits the final scene; the Magic Flute is Italian and English, follows the old recitative version and is about half the length of the German original; the Carmen is the recitative version without the spoken dialogue. The Orfeo scan has a Metropolitan Opera programme of 29 November 1939 bound in.
- Lowrie's Fear and Trembling was registered for copyright on 10 December 1941, after the course had begun.
- Faust Part I and The Education of Henry Adams carry a handwritten 'Omit' on the sheet (from A_michigan_course.md).

### 3. How the numbers were made, and their limits

- Word counts are whitespace-separated tokens, as `wc -w` gives, over the line range stated for each file. The Project Gutenberg header and licence are always outside the range. Introductions, notes, glossaries, indexes and contents lists are excluded wherever the range says so. d_data.py recomputes every count from the local file each time it runs.
- Pages are words divided by 300 for every work. For verse that understates printed pages, so use the line counts. Line counts are non-blank lines in the range and include speaker names and stage directions unless the entry says otherwise. Only the Dante figure is a count of verse lines alone.
- 'Host reports' figures are Standard Ebooks' own counts and include front and back matter.
- OCR counts come from unproofed Internet Archive text and run about 1 to 2 per cent high.
- In the two-language librettos the English and the original were separated line by line by an automatic language test (langsplit.py). Treat those figures as accurate to about 5 per cent. The Flying Dutchman was split by indentation and is exact.
- French texts from Wikisource run a few per cent high, because French spacing before punctuation makes separate tokens.
- The complete Fear and Trembling at 45,000 words is an ESTIMATE, not a measurement. It rests on Hollander's statement that his text is a third of the book and on the size of the Danish text.
- The Family Reunion was counted from OCR text that was then deleted.
- The Castle figure covers the eighteen chapters only and is counted on the local text file. It replaces an earlier count of 97,145 made on the web page. With a two-word trim at the end of the Rilke range, the totals are 318 words lower than in the first version of this file.

### 4. Copyright reasoning and evidence

- Rules applied: anything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain in the United States as of 1 January 2026. US works of 1931 to 1963 are protected for 95 years only if the copyright was renewed. Foreign works that were still protected at home in 1996 had US copyright restored and run 95 years from publication whether renewed or not. Old opera-house librettos printed in the US without a copyright notice are public domain. This is a researcher's reading of the rules, not legal advice.
- Renewal evidence was read in Project Gutenberg's transcriptions of the Catalog of Copyright Entries renewal lists (ebooks #11821 to #11840, covering 1960 to 1969) and, for the one drama, in the Internet Archive scan of the 1966 Dramas volume. The entries found, with source and line number, are in `d_records/renewal_evidence.txt`, together with the titles searched for and not found. The lists themselves were deleted after use; they can be fetched again from the addresses in that file.
- The book renewal lists do not include plays. The Family Reunion renewal is in the Dramas volume, which is why a search of book renewals misses it.
- 'Not found' is weaker than 'not renewed': a renewal under another heading would be missed. Where that matters the entry says 'uncertain'.
- The Stanford renewal database and the Library of Congress site could not be reached, so no second source was used.
- Translator dates marked (PG) are from the Gutenberg catalogue record. Dates marked (recalled) were not re-checked. John Linton's dates were not found.

### 5. What 'lending copy' means

The Internet Archive lends scanned books to anyone with a free account, one reader at a time per copy, usually for an hour and sometimes for fourteen days. Every lending copy listed here was checked against the Archive's own availability service in this session and reported as lendable. Items the service reported as restricted to print-disabled readers are described that way and are not counted as free access. Lending status can change without notice, so it should be checked again before the course relies on it.

On the final pass all 70 Internet Archive items named in this file were put to the availability service again, and every one matched its description here: 20 lendable, 3 restricted to print-disabled readers, 47 openly readable. The rows are in `d_records/lending_check.json`; `verify_lending.py` repeats the check in about seven minutes.
