# Peer Gynt: the critic's record

Piece `peer-gynt`. Critic at work on 2026-10-03, after the examiner
(exam.md) and the artist (art.md). Words of the work are pointed at by
passage, tab, row and word number, never typed out. "Old" and "new"
below are the site's own sentences.

In short: 48 sentences touched (7 cut, 1 moved to notes.md, 40
reworded), 21 titles marked, no entry of the list taken out whole.
The checker ends with no problems, no warnings, and the printed notes
fit to print. For the lead: section 4 (nine things I doubted and left),
section 5 (the wording of the drawing's script and facts) and
section 6 (what got in the way of reading).

### Passage 1, the troll king's two sayings

4. Figure, caption. Old: "He answers it with two sayings". New: "He
   answers it himself with two sayings". The riddle is set to Peer,
   so "He" read as Peer.

   also turn two words round. That one word is the whole difference
   between the commands, and in his next speech the Old Man calls it
   **sundering**, dividing." New: "with one more after a dash, and
   that word is the whole difference between the two commands. The
   translators, William and Charles Archer, also add a *to* and turn
   two words round. In his next speech the Old Man calls the added
   word **sundering**, dividing." Three faults. "The Archers" had not
   been said to be the translators anywhere a reader meets them
   (section 3). "That one word" came after the clause about the
   English and could be taken for a word of theirs. And a reader who
   lays the two commands side by side finds a third difference in the

   passed over; the lens's first ground already has it.

## 4. Doubted and not changed (for the lead)

Nothing here is a doubt about a fact. The examiner's record holds, and
where I read Auden's pages again the paraphrases say no more than he
does.

1. **The head of the page says its first thing twice.** The first
   sentence of `view.says` ("For Auden the play puts one question
   again and again and gives it different answers: who am I.") is
   the quotation printed directly above it, in other words. The
   rule says never to touch `view`, so I marked its title and left
   the rest. If one of the two is to go, it is that sentence, and
   the next would begin "In February 1941, the year of the course,
   Auden wrote that every answer is provisional".
2. **Three `auden` grounds now back no sentence**, because the
   sentence they backed is cut (6, 11, 28):
   `plates[0].lenses[0].grounds[4]`, `plates[0].lenses[3].grounds[6]`
   and `plates[2].lenses[1].grounds[3]`. Grounds are not printed and
   not mine to touch. The lead can take the three out, or put a
   sentence back if he thinks the cut wrong.
3. **Entry 5 goes to the edge of what I may do.** It brings into the
   caption a fact that stood in the lens's first ground and not in
   the caption: the small word the English adds. I did it because
   the caption said "the whole difference" of two lines in which a
   reader can count three differences. If that is more than a
   critic's mend, take out "add a *to* and"; the rest stands.
4. **Two Beat captions give a reason the page cannot show.**
   Passage 1: "In Ibsen both line ends are rhyme words, and a
   handbook of verse says that rhyme emphasizes the end of a line.
   The added word is given that place." Passage 2: "In Ibsen each
   pair is a rhymed couplet, and a handbook of verse says the quick
   return of the rhyme makes a couplet one unit: a coat to a
   couplet." The English on the plate has no rhyme, as the first
   sentence of each caption says, and the Norwegian is not printed.
   The reader takes the rhymes on trust. In passage 1 the picture
   still bears out half the reason: the added word has the last
   beat and the line end. In passage 2 it bears out "two lines to a
   coat" and no more. Both sentences are true to their grounds, so
   I left them.
5. **The Return tab leans on a stage direction that is not on the
   plate.** The caption says "Peer steps back and says the one
   word", and the second remark says "by the stage direction, he
   steps back". The Act 5 passage shows rows 71.1, 75.0, 79.0 to
   79.1 and 82.0; the direction is block 81, between the last two,
   and is not shown. A reader looks for it. Either the block joins
   the rows shown, or the remark says where the direction is. Rows
   are marks and a new clause is a claim, so neither is mine.
6. **The Beat tab of passage 2 pays least of the twelve.** Its
   picture rules fourteen lines to show two things: that each coat
   takes two lines, which the Figure tab's marks show already, and
   that a stage direction splits the last line. Its first two
   sentences are about the measure of the whole play. It is not
   wrong and not empty; if a tab had to go, it is this one.
7. **The Layers caption has room and a gap.** It is 99 words and no
   longer says which coats the passage shows. The artist asked for
   that clause (art.md, section 4). It would be a new sentence, so
   it is the lead's. The red rule and the last row of the key carry
   the fact meanwhile.
8. **The first entry of the list ends on two sentences about
   editions**: which translation has which wording, and whose words
   Auden quotes in 1962. They explain why the page's words differ
   from his, and a note at the foot says the same. If the entry is
   to be shorter, its last sentence is the one to cut. I left it: it
   is a fact the examiner chose to print.
9. **"Catechism"**, in the second entry of the list, is left as
   Auden's word, unexplained. A reader who does not know it loses
   the tone and not the sense.

## 5. The words of the drawing (passage 2, tab "Layers")

I read every word the drawing prints, in the pictures at both widths
and in `site/js/pieces/peer-gynt.js`. The words of the rows come from
the lens's `facts`; the key and the line that speaks when a row is
touched come from the script. Neither is mine to change. Nothing in
either is untrue. What should change, in the order a reader meets it:

**The line over the drawing** (`what`, in reading.json).
Now: "The coats of the onion in the order Peer strips them, with what
he calls each one and where in the play that part is seen or told".
"That part" has no noun before it; the reader finds the noun a line
lower, at the head of the second column. Mine: "The coats in the
order Peer strips them: what he calls each, the part he played, and
where in the play that part is seen or told". It names the three
columns in their order and is no longer than the line it replaces.
This one is mine to change. I saw it after the second and last set of
pictures and left it, so that the pictures in shots/ show the page as
the reading has it (the same holds for two labels in section 6).

**The rows** (`plates[1].lenses[1].facts`).

- `facts[3]`, "the part he played": "fur hunter". It repeats two words
  of the line above it and tells nothing more; the place beside it
  already says the part is named nowhere else. Mine: an empty string.
  The script prints nothing for an empty part, as in the row "then
