### A note on titles in captions

CRITIC.md names `about`, `where`, `view.says`, the list and each
`source`. Captions are not in that list and cannot take asterisks. I
set the titles in three captions in italics all the same (changes 6,
17 and 20), with the tag the model piece uses for the plays it names
in its captions, because the same title would otherwise stand upright
in a caption and in italics in the list below it. The page looks for
an italic word of a caption in the passage and lights it there; none
of these titles is a word of the passages, so nothing lights. If the
lead wants the rule kept to its letter, take the tag off in those
three captions. The notes on single words and the numbered remarks
are printed as plain text, all in italics, so "the Paradiso" and
"Inferno 1" in the note on the word at row 28.1, word 5, cannot be
marked.

## 5. Sentences I doubt and did not change

1. **Rhyme, first passage, the last sentence.** "For Dante, Auden
   wrote, a single step leads from the creature who can love to the
   Creator who is Love." On p. 68 the sentence is about Beatrice: the
   creature is the beloved person, who in Auden's words can love and be
   loved, and the step is from the love of her to God. The caption
   drops "and be loved" and sets the sentence after lines whose
   creature is what the Maker became, and after the rhyme of maker
   with love. A reader will take Auden to be speaking of the prayer
   and of that rhyme. He is not. The head of the page gives his
   sentence its right sense; here it is bent toward the words of the
   passage. The page warns the reader that the remark on the number of
   lines is applied by the page; it gives no such warning here. For
   the lead or the examiner: either say whom Auden means, or let the
   caption end on the lines themselves (the next line, row 2.0, says
   that love was lit again in her womb, which is the text's own tie of
   maker to love).
2. **Figure, second passage, the last sentence.** "Auden wrote that in
   Dante the flow of images never runs dry." True to p. 269, and the
   only place the page ties the "images" of the view to a passage. But
   it is general, and it follows the caption's own reason without
   joining it. If one Auden sentence is to go, it is this one; then
   "Auden: *Forewords and Afterwords*, p. 269." comes out of the
   `stands` too (the `why` does not name him).
3. **Rhyme, second passage, the last two sentences** (the word for
   light inside the word for volume; the same small word before
   each). They show two things and give no reason for either; the
   poet's notes say this was deliberate. Under rule two that half of
   the tab is unfinished, and under rule three I could not finish it.
   The examiner's record has a fact that could carry it (exam.md,
   section 7, last item): the word for volume ends a line six times
   in the poem, and each time the word for light rhymes with it. To
   add it is to add a claim, so it is not mine to do.
4. **Rhyme, third passage.** "The rhyme word before, mossa, moved,
   returns inside the last line as move, moves." Also a bare
   observation. The facts of the Whole tab hold an unused one that
   bears on it (`facts.move`: the first line of the *Paradiso* ends on
   the same verb). Again a claim to add, for the lead.
5. **Beat, third passage.** The reason given for the two stresses is a
   general remark applied by the page to a line its source does not
   cite. The caption says whose remark it is and a caveat says the
   rest, so I left it; the examiner has already put the choice to the
   lead (exam.md, section 11).
6. **`caveats[0]`.** "A few syllables in lines 140 to 144 can be heard
   or counted another way." The examiner's table and the poet's notes
   both give a second hearing for line 139 as well (its first stress
   on the second syllable or the third). A number, so not mine to
   change: "139 to 144" would be exact.
7. **Figure, first passage.** "By the measure of nature" is a little
   stiff. Left, because it is the commentator's own hedge, and the
   poet's notes say why a plainer phrase would claim too much of the
   third pair.
8. **`where`.** "The Divine Comedy, Paradiso, Canto 33" says again
   what the line over the title says, and adds the canto. The Inferno
   page has the shorter form. Left as it is, with its titles marked;
   see section 7.

No tab pays nothing. The two Rhyme tabs named in 3 and 4 pay in their
first half and only point in their second.

## 6. The words the drawing prints (third passage, Whole)

Read in `site/js/pieces/dante-paradiso.js` and in the pictures; the
script was not edited. What it prints, and where each word comes from:

- From the facts: the sum at the head ("100 cantos: 1 + 33 + 33 + 33",
  from `cantos` and `plan`), "14,233 lines", the three names and their
  lines, the last word of each part and what its last line says
  (`lastWord`, `lastLineSays`), "Paradiso 33, 145 lines: 48 × 3 + 1",
  the red "lines 139 to 145", and the sentence under the strip
  (`cantoShape`). When the pointer is on the first bar it also prints
  what `plan` says after its colon.
- Typed in the script: "canto", "lines", "groups of", "closing line",
  "line 1", "this passage:", "in all", the pointer's sentences ("line
  139 ends the Inferno on stelle"; "Line 145, the closing line"), and
  the first row of the key.

Two of them should change:

1. **`facts.cantoShape`**, printed under the strip: "each of the 100
   cantos is a run of three-line groups followed by one single line,
   so each has a multiple of three lines, plus one". It is the fourth
   place on one tab that says how a canto is built: the caption's
   second sentence, the strip's heading ("48 × 3 + 1") and its two
   labels ("48 groups of 3 lines", "1 closing line") say it already.
   The one thing it adds is that all hundred are built like the one
   drawn, and that is lost in the middle of it. "One single line"
   says one twice. The tab also has three names for one thing: "a
   run of three-line groups" here, "a chain of three-line groups" in
   the caption, "groups of 3 lines" on the strip. Something like
   "all 100 cantos are built so: groups of three lines, then one
   closing line" would say only what is new. It is a fact key, so the
   examiner or the artist should set it; the drawing follows by
   itself.
2. **The first row of the key**, typed at line 265 of the script: "a
   canto, as tall as its lines". Lines have no height; what is meant
   is that a bar's height is the number of lines in its canto.
   Something like "a canto: the more lines, the taller the bar". The
   comment at the head of the script says no word is typed in it;
   this one is, with the small words listed above, which are fine.

The rest reads well and should stay: the three `lastLineSays`, the
sum, the labels of the strip, the pointer's sentences, and the title
over the drawing (`what`), which is long but is built as every other
piece builds its own. `stelle`, `velle`, `move` and `purgatorioEnd`
are not printed by this drawing.

- The kit was being mended while I worked: `plate.js` and the style
