### The head of the page

1. `about`, fourth sentence. Old: "In the second circle Francesca ...
   tells him that love led them to one death, and far lower the Greek
   hero Ulysses, speaking from inside a flame, tells how ..." New: "...
   led them to one death. Far lower, the Greek hero Ulysses, speaking
   from inside a flame, tells how ..." Fault: one sentence of 46 words
   carried two of the three passages; now each passage has a sentence.
   The comma keeps "lower" from being read as a verb.
2. `view.says`, second sentence. Old: "The phrase, he writes, describes
   no mood in God." New: "That phrase, he writes, ..." Fault: unclear
   pointer; "the phrase" could be taken for the writing over the gate.

### Passage 1, the gate

   <i>terza rima</i>, for the poem, on the number three, which his
   prose ties to the Trinity." New: "... for the poem and built it on
   the number three, which ..." Fault: four phrases stacked on commas
   with no verb for "on the number three". "Built it on three" is the
   wording of `stands`.
4. Figure, caption, sentences 2 to 6 put in another order and two of
   them joined. Old order: "Beginning line after line alike is called

   claim changed; 110 words.

### Passage 2, Francesca

## 6. Sentences doubted and not changed

No sentence was found that says more than its grounds. The nearest
are the first two below, where the order of the sentences says a
little more than their words.

1. Francesca, Sentence, last sentence. Landor's remark comes straight
   after "the second time it is the love", so it reads as said of
   her. Auden says it of the people of the *Inferno* at large, and
   `open` records that he does not apply it to Francesca himself. The
   caption says no more than he said. Moving or cutting it is a
   judgment on the reading, not on the words. For the lead.
2. Gate, Figure, the sentence of 1939. It is about Hell's gates in
   general (the examiner's note; the second caveat tells the reader).
   After change 4 it follows the sentence on city gates, which makes
   the join easier and does not make it a sentence about Dante's
   gate. Left as the examiner left it.
3. Ulysses: that the temptation may have been Dante's own is told in
   the Word caption, in the last sentence of the Poem caption, and in
   `auden[5]`. Two captions of one passage rest on one remark. Each
   tab has to stand alone, so both were left. For the lead.
4. Tense of his verbs where the grammar holds. Gate, Compare: "he
   wrote" and then "he says", of one review, in two sentences
   running. Gate, Rhyme: "Auden wrote", with no date, where the list
   has "he writes" of the same review. A rule that would settle it:
   the past where the caption gives a year or an occasion, the
   present elsewhere. By it Rhyme would read "Auden writes" and
   Compare "English has few rhymes, he writes, ...". Not changed: it
   is a rule for the whole site, and the lead's to set.
5. `auden[4]`: "the other love". It follows the three lines he
   quotes, which say no more than that. The fourth caption on
   Francesca calls it the mind's love, from the line before them. A
   reader of the list alone is not told what the other love is, and
   telling him would bring in a line Auden does not quote.
6. `auden[1]`: "Its teaching aim" is plain and a little odd. The
   entry's last sentence, on Hitler, closes with force; it is his
   point and stands as his.
7. Short sentences kept, each a plain fact the next sentence needs:
   "Letters mark the rhymes." "The <i>me</i> is a gate." "The two
   rhyme." "It does not rhyme." "The figure is called polyptoton."

8. Notes that say part of their caption again, kept because each adds

   Ulysses, Word, `notes[1]` (the Latin) and `notes[4]`, where "a
