## 3. The terms

- Parallelism (third passage, Frame): Silva Rhetoricae defines it as
  likeness of structure in a pair or series of related words, phrases or
  clauses. Rows 34.21 to 34.24 keep one order of words. The name fits.
- Zeugma (Yoke): Silva Rhetoricae gives the Greek as "a yoking", one
  part of speech (most often the main verb) governing two or more parts
  of a sentence; prozeugma is the kind where the verb stands in the
  first clause and is understood in the others. Row 34.20 has its verb
  once, in the first half. The name fits, and the caption's plain gloss
  (one verb governs two clauses) is right.
- Chiasmus (Cross): Silva Rhetoricae has two senses, of which the first
  is the repetition of ideas in inverted order. Row 34.25: grief, mirth,
  mirth, grief (w2, w3, w6, w7). It crosses in ideas while the grammar
  of the two halves runs the same way, which is what the caption says.
