## 4. The two things the lead asked about

### The label of the third color (second passage, Sentence)

The label was true and did not say what the rule shows.

A reader comes to the gold rule from three blue ones. Each blue rule
stands under one sentence, and the three are labelled "three whole
sentences": the picture has just taught that one rule is one thing.
The gold rule is one rule, under eleven words, and its label said
"pieces of one to four words": a plural for a single mark, and a
length the mark does not have. The reader has to look for the stops
inside the rule to make the label true. What the rule shows is a
stretch, the end of the speech.

So the label now names the stretch and then says how it is made: "the
end, in pieces of one to four words". These are the caption's own
words ("he ends in pieces of one to four words"), so key and caption
now say one thing. The key reads: "Iago: eight words", "three whole
sentences", "the end, in pieces of one to four words". Seen in the
picture of 11:39 at both widths.

One choice is the lead's, because it is a mark. The artist writes that
a rule for each piece cannot be had: a span begins and ends at a
token, and four of the six pieces lie inside or across token 65, which
is four words joined by dashes. The kit may allow it all the same. Its
function for the words of a row takes a number that names one part of
a word joined by dashes (`site/js/kit/plate.js`, lines 432 to 448; its
own note says "6.1 before the dash, 6.2 after it"), and the Sentence
drawing hands a span's two numbers straight to that function
(`site/js/kit/lens-sentence.js`, lines 24 to 26). Six spans would be:
row 49.0, words 59 to 59; 60 to 63; 64 to 65.1; 65.2 to 65.2; 65.3 to
65.3; 65.4 to 66. That is one, four, two, one, one and two words. I
have not tried it: marks are not mine to set, and I do not know that
the checker takes a part number in a span. If the lead does rule each
piece, the old label is exact again and should come back.

   caption does not say that it does, but the place of the sentence

   cause he will not name, with Auden (p. 268). The second half glosses
