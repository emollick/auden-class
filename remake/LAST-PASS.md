# For the last reading across the pieces

Things that one piece cannot settle, gathered as the finishers and the
critics pass them up. The critic who reads across all the pieces at the
end takes these with it.

1. **The name of the measure.** Pieces say it three ways: "rising", a
   plain description (each beat follows a lighter syllable), and the
   foot's name. One wording for the site. A plain description must not
   say of every beat what a line that begins on a beat contradicts
   (othello, rows 110.0 and 112.0).
2. **A dictionary's headword after "under"**, in `stands` and in the
   sources at the foot: bare with a capital as the dictionary heads it
   (othello), bare in lower case (karamazov), or marked as a word named
   as a word. One rule.
3. **A sense said three times on a Word tab** (under the word, in the
   caption, in the note). It is how the tab is built. Decide whether a
   note may be dropped where it only repeats its gloss.
4. **Turns of phrase that recur from piece to piece**: "first example",
   "Auden writes that", "the lexicon of Shakespeare's words", and what
   the critics list under "what recurs".
5. **Figures for cantos, acts and books.** The Purgatorio page has Roman
   figures for cantos (in `where` and throughout); the Inferno and
   Paradiso pages have Arabic. One way for the three, and the same
   question for acts and scenes elsewhere.
6. **The dictionary's name.** *A New English Dictionary* (the name on
   the volumes cited), "the Oxford dictionary" in captions, and once
   "the first edition of the OED" in a source line. One form in the
   sources and one in captions.
7. **"As long as its words"** in the keys of drawings (othello,
   kierkegaard and others) can be read as "provided that". One wording:
   for example "each mark as long as the words it stands for".
8. **The word over the English column** on a plate of two versions:
   "Longfellow" bare on the Inferno page, "Longfellow, 1867" on the
   Paradiso page, "Okey's prose, 1901" on the Purgatorio page.
