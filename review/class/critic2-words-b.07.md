## The worst faults of the whole share, worst first

This replaces the list at the head of part .01.

1. Who is speaking. Six sentences give a general judgment with no speaker, inside paragraphs that otherwise report Auden: marks 10, 19, 30, 34, 39 and 91. Each steps from the play to a claim about a society, about life or about Shakespeare, and at that step the site seems to speak for itself. Two small lines of "The work on record" slide another way: in 64 this site's caution reads as Chapman's, and in 66 Miller is cited for what only Pearce says.
2. "He" and "his" with two owners, in about twenty places: marks 3, 4, 8, 23, 25, 26, 29, 32, 35, 38, 44, 47, 48, 58, 67, 68, 74, 76, 77 and 79. Seven of them are a scene or a part that opens on "he" or "him" under a line of witnesses' names (8, 38, 44, 47, 67, 74, and 68 after Miller). This is the most frequent fault of the share.
3. In "The work on record," links set straight under a student's name read as what the student worked on (59); two link labels say nothing where they stand (62); and "scansion" is never said in plain words (65).
4. One build over and over: "X, he said, is Y" eleven times in scene 8 (87), and runs of short sentences that open on "He" (6, 27, 53).
5. The "Lycidas" project told three times in the short scene 13 (69).

Not found anywhere in the share: a dash inside a sentence, "we," a rhetorical question, a sentence that tells the reader what to feel, a scene that closes on a moral, a fault of tense (the past is kept for 1941 and the present for what a source says now). "Not X but Y" comes once, in two sentences (mark 34). There is one stock word of the house list ("testament," mark 33). British turns are two: "Some weeks on" (mark 50) and "set" for "assigned" (marks 56 and 93).
