## 6. The way in from the front page (first, because a visitor meets it first)

At 1280 (`home/home-1280-0.png`):

- The band sits directly under the opening plate and above the works. Small
  label "THE CLASS", then a large head, "In room 2215, late in the afternoon",
  with an arrow, an italic deck of two sentences, and to the right the fourteen
  scene titles in two columns. It is handsome and it is the same hand as the
  rest of the front page. The head is the best invitation on the site so far: a
  room number and a time of day, no adjective.
- It says what is there. The deck ends "with the blackboards, the records he
  played and the work he asked for", and the list of titles lets me see the
  shape before I enter ("Othello on the blackboard", "A chart the width of the
  room", "The examination", "At 1223 Pontiac Trail"). I wanted to press
  "A chart the width of the room" at once.
- One weakness. On a laptop screen about 800 px high, the first screen is the
  quotation and the plate; the band begins near 1085 px, so it is wholly below
  the fold. The only sign of the class in the first screen is the word
  "THE CLASS" in the navigation, in the same small grey capitals as the other
  three entries. A visitor who does not scroll sees a site about lines of
  verse, as before. Change, in a sentence: either bring the band's top rule and
  head into the first screen at 1280 (the plate has room to give: there is
  about 80 px of air above the quotation and 80 below the plate), or accept it
  knowingly; I would not move the plate out of first place.
- Small: the sentence under the band, "The reading list of that course names
  thirty-three works", leans on the band for "that course". It works, and it
  ties the band to the works below. Keep it.

At 390 (`home/home-390-0.png`):

- The band keeps the label, the head, the deck. The list of fourteen titles is
  gone. That is the right cut for a phone (fourteen rows would push the works a
  screen and a half down), but it means that at 390 the band no longer says
  what is there beyond the deck. The deck carries it well enough.
- The whole band reads as a heading and a paragraph; only the small arrow after
  "afternoon" says it can be pressed. The arrow is about 12 px wide and grey. I
  cannot tell from a picture how much of the band is the link. If only the
  head is, say so to the builder: on a phone the whole band (head and deck)
  should be one target.
- The head breaks "In room 2215, late / in the afternoon" at both widths, which
  is the right break.

Verdict on 6: yes, it makes me want to enter, and it says what is there. Fault:
below the fold at laptop height, and at 390 the affordance is one small arrow.

## The top of the class page (head, lede, list of scenes) at 1280

- Head: small capitals "UNIVERSITY OF MICHIGAN · FALL 1941", then "The class",
  then an italic line, "English 135, Fate and the Individual in European
  Literature, as the students in the room recorded it". Quiet and exact. The
  last seven words of that line already answer question 4 (whose words these
  are) before the page begins. Keep.
- Lede: two paragraphs. The first gives the room, the hour, and Pearce writing
  down what he could remember within the hour, in a classroom near by. That one
  detail makes every later "Pearce says" trustworthy and human. The second
  paragraph tells me what a scene will hold (what he said, the blackboard, the
  records, what he asked for by the next meeting) and that the passages
  under each are what the class was reading. It is the only place the page
  explains itself, and a newcomer needs it. Keep both; do not lengthen.
- List of scenes: fourteen titles, three columns, hairlines. Clear. It is the
  same list as on the front page, which a visitor has just read ten seconds
  ago: on arrival from the band it is a repeat. Not a fault worth a change at
  1280 (the page is 23,000 px tall and wants a list), only noted.
- The lede is set in a narrow column on the left with the right half of the
  screen empty; then the list runs full width. I have not yet seen the whole
  first screen together (the tool cuts it into head, lede, toc), so I cannot
  say whether the three parts sit well together. If the lede is left-aligned
  under a centered head, look at it once by eye.
