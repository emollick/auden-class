# The class page, critic by word, second reading. Share A: head, lede, way in, scenes 1 to 5

Part 01. Saved early, after reading as far as the end of scene 2. The ranking
below is what stood out so far. The ranking for the whole share is given again
at the top of the last part.

**Worst faults so far, worst first**

1. "He" and "his" with two or three possible owners. Scenes 1 and 2 have Auden,
   Pearce, Miller and Chapman in them, and the pronoun changes owner from one
   sentence to the next with no name to mark the change.
2. People who act before a word says who they are: Miller in scene 1 (he is
   introduced in scene 2), George Lefferts, and a Millar beside a Miller.
3. The page says what it holds, and says it twice: [lede[1]] repeats
   [home.say], and its "Under each" is not true of the first scenes.
4. Sentences that have to be read twice: "as the examination", "with every book
   to read", "For the eleven in black a class".

Each mark gives the place, the opening words, the fault, and what to put.

## The head, the lede, the way in

**W1 [lede[0]]** "One of them, Donald Pearce, took…"
Fault: forty-three words in one sentence, and "most of what is known of what
Auden said" sets one "what" on top of another.
Put: "One of them, Donald Pearce, took to writing down what he could remember
within an hour or so of a lecture, in a classroom nearby. Most of what Auden is
known to have said in class comes from those notes."

**W2 [lede[1]]** "What follows are the afternoons they…"
Fault: the page explains what it holds where the scenes show it (their labels
say "On the blackboard", "On the phonograph", "For Tuesday"), and a reader who
came by the front page has read the same list a moment ago in [home.say].
Put: cut the paragraph. If one line must stay: "Below are the afternoons they
recorded, with the passages on this site that the class was reading."

**W3 [lede[1]]** "Under each are the passages on…"
Fault: it is not so of each. Scenes 1 and 2 have no passages under them.
Put: cut with W2. The one line offered there says "with", not "under each".

**W4 [contents]** "The afternoons in order"
Fault (small): the list it opens also holds an interview, the Friday evenings,
the examination, the papers and the next course, and "in order" does not say
in what order. The plainest label that is true is "On this page".
Put: "On this page". The lead may keep the present label for its sound: the
fault is small.

**W5 [home.say]** "These are the afternoons his students…"
Fault: on a narrow screen "These" points at nothing, since the scenes' titles
stand beside the block only on a wide one. "The blackboards" stands for what was
written on them.
Put: "The afternoons his students recorded, with what he wrote on the
blackboard, the records he played and the work he asked for."

## Scene 1, "A card on an office door"

**W6 [scenes[0].paras[0]]** "The course was not in the…" and "Miller found
that one had…"
Fault: Miller is cited, and then acts, before a word says who he is. He is
introduced one scene later, in [scenes[1].paras[0]].
Put, here: "Charles Miller, who shared Auden's house that fall, found that one
had been kept for him (p. 7)."
Put, in [scenes[1].paras[0]]: "Miller puts the first class in early October."

**W7 [scenes[0].paras[0]]** "The heading of the mimeographed reading…"
Fault: four things in one sentence. "It" may be the course or the list just
named. "The mimeographed reading list" comes with "the" before the reader has
heard of a list. "Of the department" names no department. And the reader cannot
tell whether written permission was asked of all three kinds of student or of
the graduates only.
Put: "The reading list was mimeographed, and its heading opened the course to
juniors, seniors and graduate students who had permission in writing from
Professor Arno Bader of the English department."
Missing: what the heading says about permission. If it asks it of graduates
only, put "to juniors and seniors, and to graduate students who had permission
in writing from…". Check also that Bader's department was English.

**W8 [scenes[0].paras[0]]** "Auden also chose for himself."
Fault: chose what? The reader has to wait for the next sentence to learn it.
Put: "Auden also chose his students himself."

**W9 [scenes[0].paras[1]]** "Auden said that they must talk…" and "He named
some of the…"
Fault: "whether he was prepared" may be either man, and the "He" that opens the
next sentence has changed owner (Pearce, then Auden).
Put, as one sentence: "Auden said that they must talk first, to see whether
Pearce was prepared, and named some of the authors: Horace, Dante, Shakespeare,
Goethe, Melville, Kafka, Rimbaud, Valéry."

**W10 [scenes[0].paras[1]]** "There would be opera scores and…"
Fault (small): "named" for the second time in two sentences, and "libretti" is
a word of the trade with no plain word beside it. It comes again in the note
under the bars of scene 2.
Put: "There would be operas as well, the scores and the printed words, and he
mentioned *Carmen*, *Don Giovanni* and *Tristan*."

**W11 [scenes[0].parts[0].after[0]]** "Then he admitted him."
Fault (small): two pronouns for two men in four words.
Put: "Then he admitted Pearce."
