# The class page, critic by word, second reading. Share A, part 07 (the last)

**Worst faults of the whole share, worst first** (this replaces the ranking of
part 01)

1. Auden's judgments printed flat, as if they were this site's: W58 (what *The
   Castle* is to its century), W55, W41, W79. Four places, and W58 is the worst
   sentence of the share.
2. "He", "his" and "it" with two or three possible owners: twenty marks,
   thickest in scene 2 (W9, W11 to W14, W17, W18, W20 to W22, W26 to W28, W31,
   W65, W66, W68, W69).
3. People and terms that arrive before a word says what they are: Miller,
   Lefferts, "Auden's two courses", King Mark, Bunyan's pilgrim, Virgil, "the
   famous formula", ontology and teleology, Agape and Eros.
4. The page talking about itself, and things told twice: [lede[1]] against the
   front page, two scenes that open on their source (W39, W53), the pages blown
   off the table in two scenes running (W45).

## Scene 5, the cards and the fold

**W82 [scenes[4].parts[1].doors[1].why]** "Carmen's own account of love…"
(small)
Fault: the first two of its three things are not in the two lines the card
shows, and the reader looks for them. The card glosses its first line in three
words of its own.
Put: keep "a thing that knows no law", and in place of the rebel and the bird
use the card's own gloss of its first line.

**W83 [scenes[4].parts[1].folds[0].paras[0]]** "Summing up its argument, he
wrote…"
Fault: "Agape" here, and "Eros" in the last sentence, are words of the trade
with no plain word beside them.
Put, for the last clause: "…and that what Christianity teaches about Agape,
unselfish love, stands opposed to it."

**W84 [scenes[4].parts[1].folds[0].paras[0]]** "It sometimes writes, he said,
as…"
Fault: a book does not write. "He said" stands where the paragraph has had "he
wrote". "The two loves" comes before Eros has been named. "For him" may be de
Rougemont.
Put: "De Rougemont, he wrote, sometimes treats Eros, the love that desires, and
Agape as simply divided. For Auden, Agape is Eros changed by grace."

## Four more, on parts already covered

**W85 [lede[0]]** "In the fall of 1941 Auden…" (small)
Fault: the line over the title has just said "University of Michigan · Fall
1941", so the first sentence tells the place and the term a second time. Auden
has no initials at his first mention on the page.
Put: "W. H. Auden taught one course that fall."

**W86 [scenes[0].paras[0]]** the paragraph, as the opening of the first scene
Fault: the scene is called "A card on an office door", and it opens on a
catalog, a mimeographed heading and a professor's permission: five sentences
from three witnesses. The card and the knock are in the second paragraph. It is
the driest paragraph of the share, at the place where a reader decides whether
to go on.
Put: no new words. Open the scene on [scenes[0].paras[1]] ("Donald Pearce was
twenty-three…") and set [scenes[0].paras[0]] after the questions and their
answer, as a note. W6 to W8 apply to it there.

**W87 [scenes[3].parts[1].recordNote]** "A recording of this concerto came…"
(small)
Fault: the reader has heard of one recording, Schnabel's, and takes this for
it.
Put: "Another recording of the concerto came out in 1925, and no copy of it has
been found."

**A tic: "kept".** Six times in scenes 1 to 4, in five senses: a place kept for
Miller, "kept to the ideas", "kept coming back", "Pearce kept six" with the
label over the list, and "the first notes Pearce kept in order". W16 and W53
take out two. The rest can stay.

**Outside this file.** The link at the foot of the page, "The list, and the
books to read" (site/js/class.js, in site.md), reads two ways, as W32 does.

## Not found in this share

No dash inside a sentence, no "we", no rhetorical question of the site's own,
and none of the stock words the brief lists except "famous" (W57). Three
British forms (W49, W60, W75). No sentence tells the reader what to feel: where
a feeling is reported it is Pearce's, and the page says so.
