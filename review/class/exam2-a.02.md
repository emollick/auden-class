# Second examination of the class page, share A: part 02

Findings 2 to 6. The count and the leading findings are at the top of the
last part. A, E, B, C are the research notes named in part 01; line

## Finding 2 (doubtful)

- Place: [scenes[1].paras[2]], the last sentence.
- Claim: "The rule frightened the class at first, he says (pp. 508–09)."
  "He" is Chapman, the subject of the sentence before.
- Fault: (c) voice, and with it (d) stronger.
- The record: A, section 4.4, line 546, gives the fright inside the entry
  for the reason against notes "as Frances Aaronson Lewis remembered it",
  joined to her recollection by a semicolon and told in the first person
  plural. The record nowhere says that these are Chapman's own words.
  `exam-others.md`, lines 676 to 679 (Left out, item 11), gives the fright
  with the pages and names no speaker; its list of the page's sentences
  as they stood at 07:43 (lines 419 to 423) has no such sentence, so the
  sentence and its "he says" came in after that examination. If the
  fright is part of what Frances Lewis remembered, two things follow: it
  is her word and not Chapman's, and by the page's own sentence just
  before ("he does not say it of hers") and A line 552 it is not known to
  be about this course at all, so "the class" is more than the record has.
- Smallest change: "The same recollection has the rule put the class in a
  panic at first (pp. 508–09)." If Chapman's page shows the words to be
  his own, the sentence can stay as it is; the record here cannot show it.

## Finding 3 (small)

- Place: [scenes[5].parts[2].asks[0]]
- Claim: "For the next day: how does the mind of a saint work, and the
  mind of a villain or a criminal? Write down where the two are alike."
- Fault: (b) different, in one word, and (f) inside the page.
- The record: A, section 3.2, line 401, quotes Pearce, p. 143, with the
  phrase standing without the article. E line 264 reads it as work for
  the next class. `exam-pearce.md`, line 78, proposed the sentence
  without the article, and line 563 takes the phrase together with the
  work set for the following Tuesday as the ground for placing the
  afternoon before a weekend. With the article the page says the day
  after. The scene's own heading is "An afternoon before a weekend", the
  part's label is "For next time", and [caveats[3]] gives the class two
  meetings a week by Pearce and one by the list; on neither did it meet
  on the day after.
- Smallest change: drop "the", as A line 401 has the phrase, or use the
  part's own label: "For next time: …".

## Finding 4 (small)

- Place: [scenes[5].paras[0]], the last sentence.
- Claim: "For the verse he told the class to read the critic Wilson
  Knight."
- Fault: (d) stronger: it gives Auden a purpose the record does not give.
- The record: E line 231 (Pearce, p. 139): Auden hardly ever touched
  technique, which Pearce spells out as the music of the verse, rhetoric,
  style and ambiguity, though he did tell the class to read Wilson
  Knight. A line 402 has the same and no more. The record joins the two
  by a concession. It does not say that Knight was assigned for the
  verse, or for anything named. "The critic" is the site's own gloss.
- Smallest change: "…and hardly touched the verse, though he did tell
  the class to read Wilson Knight."

## Finding 5 (small)

- Place: [scenes[4].paras[0]], the second sentence.
- Claim: "Pearce says Auden lectured on it often that semester." "It" is
  the romantic love of the sentence before.
- Fault: (d) stronger, in one word.
- The record: E line 213 and B line 140 (Pearce, p. 137): he lectured
  often on love that semester, with no qualifier. E line 215 has romantic
  love as the subject after the break, on that afternoon. In the kept
  scan the words for lecturing often on love stand before the list of the
  reading, and the word for romantic first stands after the break is
  over (positions only: 14054 to 14097 against 14558, all on p. 137).
- Smallest change: "Pearce says Auden lectured on love often that
  semester."

## Finding 6 (small)

- Place: [scenes[3].parts[1].recordNote], the last sentence.
- Claim: "A recording of this concerto came out in 1925, and no copy of
  it has been found."
- Fault: (d) stronger.
- The record: `records.md`, lines 24 to 27, 482 to 504 and 543: an early

  [scenes[4].parts[0].recordNote], keeps to what the search can bear ("No

- Smallest change: "…and no transfer of it that can be given here has
  been found."
