# The class page, critic by word, second reading. Share A, part 05

Scene 4, "Augustine, Descartes, Kafka": the paragraphs, "For Tuesday", the
cards and the two folds. The phonograph and the note are in part 06.

## The paragraphs

**W53 [scenes[3].paras[0]]** "These are the first notes Pearce…" and "They
pick up late…"
Fault: "These" points at nothing on the page. The scene is this site's telling,
not the notes. "Kept in order" reads two ways: kept in sequence, or kept tidy.
It is also the second scene in a row to open on its source (see W39).
Put, for the two sentences: "The earliest notes Pearce has in any order pick up
late in the hour, as Auden was winding up before the break."

**W54 [scenes[3].paras[0]]** "He called Augustine…" (small)
Fault: "Augustine's account" names no book. The *Confessions* is on the list
and is linked under the cards, but the paragraph never says that it is meant.
Missing: Pearce's notes do not name the book here. If the lead will name it:
"…and the *Confessions*, his account of the conflict between self and ego, the
first classic of self-analysis." If not, leave the sentence.

**W55 [scenes[3].paras[0]]** "Since Freud, he went on, the…" to "So Augustine
now matters less…"
Fault: who is speaking. "So Augustine now matters less as psychology than as
ethics" stands alone, with no "he said", and "now" can be taken for today. It is
Auden's conclusion in 1941. The paragraph also ends on three short sentences in
a row.
Put, as one: "Since Freud, he went on, the ego has looked like a cluster of
impulses in competition and no longer like one steady thing, so that Augustine
now matters less as psychology than as ethics."

**W56 [scenes[3].paras[1]]** "He crossed the room, in what…"
Fault: "who fared the same" does not say the same as what, or at whose hands.
In the notes Auden says that what holds for Augustine holds for Descartes, and
then crosses the room.
Put: "He crossed the room, in what Pearce took for bedroom slippers, touched the
handwritten pages on his table without looking at them, and went on to
Descartes, of whom he had just said as much."

**W57 [scenes[3].paras[1]]** "Auden asked which of a man's…"
Fault: "the famous formula" is never given, and "famous" tells the reader that
he ought to know it. The six selves in parentheses hang on "formula" and belong
to "selves". In Pearce's notes they are Descartes's own, which "a man's" hides.
Put: "Auden asked which of Descartes's selves (the soldier, the mathematician,
the lover, the Latinist, the philosopher, the horseman) does the thinking in "I
think, therefore I am," and which kind of thinking is meant: doubting,
reasoning, remembering or dreaming."
Missing: the examiner should confirm "Descartes's" against Pearce, p. 135.

**W58 [scenes[3].paras[2]]** "The book looks like a satire…" and "Underneath
it is a…"
Fault: who is speaking. The "he said" is in the sentence before. These two are
printed flat, and the second carries the largest claim of the scene as if it
were this site's. "Underneath it is" also reads first as "under the book is".
Put, as one: "On its face, he said, the book is a satire on bureaucracy, and
underneath a parable, theological and psychological, which is to the twentieth
century what the *Divine Comedy* was to the fourteenth."

## "For Tuesday" and the cards

**W59 [scenes[3].parts[0].asks[1]]** "Would Virgil be any use to…" (small)
Fault: Virgil comes with no word of who he is. The cards below supply K. and
Ahab. Nothing supplies Virgil.
Put: "Would Virgil, Dante's guide, be any use to K.?"

**W60 [scenes[3].parts[0].asks[2]]** "Keep Melville in mind too. What…"
Fault: "What have Ahab and K. in common" is the British form.
Put: "Keep Melville in mind too. What do Ahab and K. have in common, and how are
their quests alike?"

**W61 [scenes[3].parts[0].doors[1].why]** "Ahab, alone, on the iron way…"
Fault: a fragment with a poetic turn, "the iron way of his own purpose", beside
two cards that speak plainly. The card's own mark says "a railroad".
Put: "Ahab, alone, speaks of his purpose as a railroad."

## The fold "What Auden wrote"

**W62 [scenes[3].parts[0].folds[1].paras[0]]** "Asked which artist stands to
the…"
Fault: "Asked" seems to belong to "he" (Auden was asked) until "one" arrives
twenty words later. Read twice.
Put: "He wrote that anyone asked which artist stands to the present age much as
Dante, Shakespeare and Goethe stood to their own would think first of Kafka,
whose three novels use the oldest device in literature, the Quest."

**W63 [scenes[3].parts[0].folds[1].paras[0]]** "In a footnote he set…"
Fault: "*Moby Dick*" here, "Moby-Dick" on the card above and over the bars of
scene 2. "No saving is in view" is not plain: the plain word is "salvation".
Put: "In a footnote he set *Moby-Dick* apart from every other quest: no
salvation is in view, and the whale is both the thing sought and its hostile
keeper (p. 111)."

**W64 [scenes[3].parts[0].folds[1].paras[1]]** "In 1946, in an essay called…"
and "Bunyan's pilgrim must gain…"
Fault: "Dante's dreamer" comes three sentences before the fold says the poem is
a dream. Bunyan's pilgrim then arrives with no word of who he is, and he is
neither of the two the sentence before has promised.
Put: "In 1946, in an essay called "K.'s Quest," he said what K. and Dante are
after." Cut the sentence on Bunyan: the next one stands without it.

**W65 [scenes[3].parts[0].folds[1].paras[1]]** "He called the *Divine Comedy*
a…" to the end
Fault: "He" follows "all he can do", where "he" is K. "It is made to see" reads
two ways: made in order to see, or forced to see. Four short sentences of one
build close the fold.
Put: "Auden called the *Divine Comedy* a dream quest, whose journey seeks no
object. The dreamer makes it to see what is real behind what appears and, awake
again, can go on with his earthly life."
