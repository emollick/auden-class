# The class page, critic by word, second reading. Share A, part 04

Scene 3, "Kierkegaard's three". E is remake/research/E_pd_pearce.md.

## The paragraphs

**W39 [scenes[2].paras[0]]** "Pearce's account opens here."
Fault: the reader has just had two scenes that cite Pearce, so "opens here"
looks wrong. What is meant is that his essay begins with this afternoon, and
[caveats[1]] says that already. It is also the page talking about its source
where the scene could simply begin.
Put: cut. The paragraph then opens on the classroom, which is its best
sentence.

**W40 [scenes[2].paras[0]]** "He stood half turned to the…"
Fault: "with long pauses and no break". A pause is a break, and the reader
stops to square the two.
Put: "He stood half turned to the windows and talked slowly and steadily, with
long pauses."

**W41 [scenes[2].paras[1]]** "A tragic hero is a superior…"
Fault: who is speaking. Seven definitions follow in the present tense, and only
the fifth has "he said". Until then a reader can take them for this site's own
account of Kierkegaard.
Put: "A tragic hero, he said, is a superior person who meets a force stronger
than himself, chooses to fight it and is crushed."

**W42 [scenes[2].paras[1]]** "If he turns death down and…"
Fault: the two sentences on the hero do not lead to the five on the attitudes.
Nothing says what a hero has to do with an attitude. Pearce gives the link (E,
line 192): the tragic hero is the esthetic hero.
Put: "If he turns death down and settles for a compromise, he stops being a
tragic hero, or esthetic hero, and becomes an ethical one."

**W43 [scenes[2].paras[1]]** "Esthetics, he said, has an ontology…"
Fault: two terms of philosophy with no plain word beside them. A reader without
them gets nothing from the sentence, and the blackboard just below says the
plain version.
Put: cut the sentence. With W41 the paragraph keeps its "he said". If it is
kept: "Esthetics, he said, has an ontology and no teleology (an account of what
is, and none of what it is for), and ethics the reverse."

## The table and the note

**W44 [scenes[2].parts[0].src]** "The first column is the blackboard…"
Fault: on the page there are four columns (the name, the line, and the two
cases), so "the first column" is only the three names. "The cases are in this
site's words" does not say whose the answers are.
Put: "Pearce, pp. 130–31. On the left is the blackboard as Pearce copied it. The
answers are Auden's, in this site's words."

**W45 [scenes[2].parts[1].paras[0]]** "He was still on the second…"
Fault: told twice, or it reads so. Scene 2 has just had a gust take Auden's
pages off the table. Here "the loose pages" come with "the" though this scene
has named no pages, and no word says it is a second time.
Put: "He was still on the second case when someone opened the door, and again a
draft took the loose pages off his table."
Missing: that these are two events. The examiner has it open (exam2-a.01.md,
inquiry 1). If they are one, one telling goes.

## The card and the fold

**W46 [scenes[2].parts[2].doors[0].why]** "Here is its man of faith…"
Fault: "who looks like anyone else at his window" can be read as "anyone else
who is at his window". The three lines on the card show no window, so the
reader looks for one.
Put: "Here is its man of faith, who looks like anyone else."

**W47 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].h]** "Where the sedative came back"
Fault (small): every other fold of this kind is headed "What Auden wrote", and
a reader learns that label. This is a second label for the same thing. It is
honest and exact, so the lead may keep it.
Put, if one label is wanted: "What Auden wrote".

**W48 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]]** "In 1944, reviewing
Kierkegaard's *Either/Or*…"
Fault: "the three" is a number with no noun. The paragraph above calls two of
them "attitudes", and the fold goes on to call them "views". The title of the
scene has the same bare number. It is short and the scene supplies the rest, so
the title can stay.
Put: "In 1944, reviewing Kierkegaard's *Either/Or*, Auden tried the three views
on Tristan and Isolde."

**W49 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]]** "The aesthetic view wants all
three…"
Fault: "aesthetic" here, and "esthetic" in the paragraphs above, on the
blackboard and in scene 6. One spelling.
Put: "The esthetic view wants all three people of the story to suffer, because
that makes the situation interesting."

**W50 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]]** "The ethical view would have
the…"
Fault: "keep faith" with whom? With each other, or with the husband? The two
readings are opposite.
Missing: the sense of the review (*Prose*, vol. II, p. 215). If it is the
husband, put: "The ethical view would have the lovers keep faith with King Mark,
her husband, and would gladly exchange the love potion for a strong sedative."
The next sentence then says "Mark" and needs no gloss.

**W51 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]]** "The religious view blesses
the lovers…"
Fault: "blesses King Mark most, who suffers" cuts the "who" off from its noun,
and King Mark comes with no word of who he is.
Put: "The religious view blesses the lovers only so far as they truly suffer
their guilt, and blesses most King Mark, the husband, who suffers more
innocently and in the end forgives."

**W52 [scenes[2].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]]** "Had the lovers taken a
sedative…"
Fault: "either way" has no two ways to point at.
Put: "Had the lovers taken a sedative and stopped being tempted, he added, their
keeping faith would have meant nothing to religion."
