## Scene 4, the fold "What two of the class wrote"

**W66 [scenes[3].parts[0].folds[0].paras[0]]** "One of his papers for the…"
Fault: "his" follows "Pearce's right", so the paper is Pearce's until the next
sentence says "Pearce's own essay". The fold is also four short sentences of
one build.
Put, for the first two sentences: "Kenneth Millar, a teaching assistant, sat on
Pearce's right, and one of Millar's papers for the course compared the *Divine
Comedy* with *The Castle*."

## Scene 4, "On the phonograph"

**W67 [scenes[3].parts[1].says[0]]** "That afternoon he played the second…"
(small)
Fault: told twice. The record's own label, beside the paragraph, gives Schnabel
and the London Philharmonic and says that Pearce names them.
Put: "That afternoon he played the second movement of Beethoven's Fourth Piano
Concerto."

**W68 [scenes[3].parts[1].says[0]]** "As far as music can show…"
Fault: "it shows" has no noun. It may be music or the movement.
Put: "As far as music can show such a thing, he said, this movement shows how
one person stands to his fate: Ahab to the whale, or K. to the Castle."

**W69 [scenes[3].parts[1].says[2]]** "Pearce had not heard the record…" and
"He heard the two…"
Fault: "heard" ends one sentence and begins the next. "He calls it" has no noun
for "it": the record, the close, or what Auden did.
Put: "Pearce was hearing the record for the first time: the two drawing apart,
the piano's frenzy, its tired return and the quiet close. He calls what Auden
did with it the best demonstration he ever saw in a classroom of how music and
literature can explain each other."

**W70 [scenes[3].parts[1].says[3]]** "No statement by Auden of what…"
Fault: the note opens on what has not been found and comes second to what is
there. "This passage of the notes" is three paragraphs back. "Of what the title
of the course meant" sets "of" on "of".
Put: "In introducing the record Auden names the two terms of the course's title
together: one person and his fate. He is not on record as saying what the title
meant. Miller has him call it [the two quoted words, as they stand] at the first
class (pp. 26–27)."

**W71 [scenes[3].parts[1].recordNote]** "Only records published before 1926
can…"
Fault: "given" is not the plain word for a record, and the sentence before it
says "played". Two words for one thing. The same in scene 5 (W80).
Put: "Only records published before 1926 can be played on this site."

## Scene 4, the note

**W72 [scenes[3].parts[2].paras[0]]** "At a recess he was easy…" and
"Otherwise he slipped out…"
Fault: "Otherwise" does not say other than what.
Put, as one: "At a recess he was easy to stop with a question, unless he had
slipped out for a smoke or a short walk to think."

**W73 [scenes[3].parts[2].paras[0]]** "Often, Pearce says, the recess went…"
Fault: "the recess went on" reads first as "the recess continued".
Put: "Often, Pearce says, the recess was spent on the phonograph: Auden searched
a record for the spot where the next hour's music started, and the students
gathered around the machine to help (pp. 136–37)."

## Scene 5, "Romantic love": the paragraphs

**W74 [scenes[4].paras[0]]** "He added *The Allegory of Love*…" (small)
Fault: "and called it a good book" gives the reader nothing to see. It can go.
Put: "He added *The Allegory of Love*, by C. S. Lewis, which the list also
recommends."

**W75 [scenes[4].paras[1]]** "He made two points, much on…" (small)
Fault: "on de Rougemont's lines" is the British idiom.
Put: "He made two points, and both follow de Rougemont."

**W76 [scenes[4].paras[2]]** "For that to last, the two…"
Fault: "that" has to reach back over a sentence and a paragraph break to the
affair. Standing after "the barrier", it reads as "for the barrier to last".
Put: "For the affair to last, the two must not become friends or settle into
marriage."

**W77 [scenes[4].paras[2]]** "Something has to keep them apart…"
Fault: "a marriage" is an obstacle here, one sentence after "settle into
marriage" was the thing to avoid. One word in two opposite parts. Pearce means
that one of the two is married already (E, line 220).
Put: "Something has to keep them apart: an existing marriage, a family feud, a
duty, a difference of religion or race."

**W78 [scenes[4].paras[2]]** "The best obstacle cannot be overcome…" (small)
Fault: it reads first as a statement about the best of the obstacles just
named.
Put: "The best obstacle is one that cannot be overcome, so that the lovers can
be joined only in death."

## Scene 5, "On the phonograph"

**W79 [scenes[4].parts[0].says[0]]** "So it would be with the…"
Fault: who is speaking. The sentence stands alone at the close with no "he
said", and reads as this site pointing the moral. It is Auden's remark (E, line
225). The "he" of the sentence before is Wagner, one sentence after a "he" that
is Auden.
Put, for the last three sentences: "Wagner, he said, understood this psychology
perfectly: all through the duet he holds the two voices apart, and lets them
join only as it closes. So it would be with the lovers' lives, Auden said."

**W80 [scenes[4].parts[0].recordNote]** "No record of the close that…"
Fault: "the close that can be given here" runs together, and "given" is the
word marked in W71.
Put: "The record ends well before the close of the duet. No record of the close
has been found that can be played here."

**W81 [scenes[4].parts[0].says[1]]** "Pearce remembered the fuss with the…"
Fault: "remembered the fuss…, the two voices…, and that…" yokes two nouns to a
clause, as in W12. "Remembered" and then "he says" for one witness in adjoining
sentences.
Put: "Pearce remembered the fuss with the needle, the two voices circling each
other up to their union, and how nobody moved when the record ended. He left the
room feeling that he knew everything."
