# The class page, read by a first visitor: part 02 (scenes 1 to 5 at 1280)

A correction to part 01: I have now seen the first screen whole
(`shots-reader/screens/screen-1280-0.png`). The lede is centered under the head
in a narrower column and the three parts sit well together. At 390 the list of
scenes is folded behind one row, "The afternoons in order", which is right.

Each scene below: the room (1), pleasure and drag (2), the work set (3), whose
words (4), the look (5). I name a card by its scene and author and do not copy it.

## 1. A card on an office door (`door`)

- Room: yes, from the second paragraph on: the card, the knock, the talk first.
  The five questions set large in the right column, with Pearce's answer and
  Auden's reply in small type beneath, are the first "what he asked" on the
  page and a delight. The fourth question is the one a visitor repeats to
  someone else.
- Drag: the scene opens on its driest paragraph (the catalog, the mimeographed
  heading, permission in writing, a professor's name, three citations) while
  the title promises a card and a door. Change: put the paragraph of the knock
  first and the paragraph on how students got in second.
- Whose: clear. "WHAT HE ASKED", with Pearce's pages at the right of the rule.
- Look: clean. Nothing to change.

## 2. The first afternoon (`first`)

- Room: strong. What he wore, the pacing, the looks out at the street, the
  gust and his two words, the coal shaft. Three witnesses and it still reads as
  one afternoon.
- Report: the paragraph on the rule against notes gives three reasons from
  three witnesses, which is worth having, and then spends two sentences on
  Chapman's questionnaire and on which of two courses an answer belongs to.
  **This is the first place I wanted to skip**: for the length of those two
  sentences I was reading about a file and not standing in a room. Change: keep
  the three reasons, and move the caveat about which course to the fold at the
  foot ("Where this page may be wrong"), or cut it to a clause.
- The six definitions: a beautiful figure (large type, small numerals,
  hairlines). Whose words: two of the six carry quotation marks and four do
  not, and nothing says why. Change: one short line at the right of the rule,
  as the Kierkegaard table has, saying which are word for word and that the
  rest are in this site's words.
- The work set: the first assignment (cantos by heart, and the allowance for
  those who could not manage) is in running text near the end, with a link on
  to the examination. Findable only by reading, but it has its own scene later
  and the link is there. Leave.
- The reading list drawn to scale: one of the three best things (see part 05).
  Thirty-three bars, each as wide as the work is long; press one and its name,
  its pages and its page on this site come up (`bar/first-1280.01.png`). It
  shows why the course was called the hardest without one adjective. But the
  two numbers that make it land, "about 4,800 pages" and "about seventeen
  weeks", are the last sentences of a grey small-type note in two columns,
  after libretti, a broken outline and one student's ticks. I skipped that
  note at first and found the numbers only on a second pass. Change: put the
  pages and the weeks on the line directly under the bars, where "Thirty-three
  works." now stands, and leave the rest below.
- Look: the labels over the bars mix two authors in roman (Dante, Henry Adams)
  with five titles in italic; consistent enough once seen. The nine opera bars
  at the right are a few pixels wide each; the arrows make them reachable.

## 3. Kierkegaard's three (`three`)

- Room: the best opening on the page. Low sun, students sitting apart, nobody
  writing, the teacher half turned to the window. I was there in six sentences.
- The table of the board and his two cases is clear at a glance, and its note
  ("The first column is the blackboard as Pearce copied it. The cases are in
  this site's words.") is the model for question 4. The cell with no answer
  says so plainly and the table loses nothing by it.
- A doubt a visitor will have: pages go off his table here (a door, a draft)
  and also in the scene before (a gust, at the first meeting). Two scenes
  running, the same accident. I could not tell whether one event is told twice
  or it happened twice. For the examiners; if twice, one word here would
  settle it.
- "Where the sedative came back" is a pleasure: a small word from the table
  returns three years later in his own prose. It stands open at 1280. Keep.
- Look: the paragraph after the table sits alone in the left half with the
  right half empty. Slightly bare; not worth a change.

## 4. Augustine, Descartes, Kafka (`ego`)

- Room: the slippers, the pages touched without a look, the recess, the class
  round the phonograph hunting for the spot. Good.
- "FOR TUESDAY": the first set of questions, large, numbered, in a panel of
  its own with Pearce's page. I understood at once what the class had been
  asked to do. The three cards directly beneath are the three books the
  questions name (Kafka, Dante, Melville), each with one line saying what the
  passage is and a way into its page. That is the exploring the owner asked
  for, and nothing is asked of me.
- Missing, and a visitor needs it: what happened on Tuesday. The two folds
  give what two of the class wrote and what Auden wrote, both good, but the
  first thing I wanted was the next meeting. The Othello scene does this with
  two rows pointing forward. If the record has nothing for that Tuesday, say
  so here in a few words, as the Kierkegaard table does.
- The row "Augustine examines one theft ..." looks like the two folds under it
  but leaves the page. The arrow against the plus signs is the only sign.
  Small; set it apart by a little space or put it with the cards.
- Phonograph: the left column is the room and is very good (what to listen
  for; the question he left open). The right column is the site talking about
  itself: two sentences on why the record cannot be played. One would do.
- Lower in the same right column is the one place the page says what the
  title of the course meant, and it begins with what has not been found.
  Change: lead with what the notes give (the two terms named together) and let
  the "not found" follow.
- Bare: at 1280 the right half of the phonograph block is mostly empty in two
  places, and the last paragraph (the recess) has an empty right half.
- Length: the tallest scene (2,656 px). It is two things, the questions and
  the record, and it holds; I did not want to skip.

## 5. Romantic love (`love`)

- Room: yes. The fuss with the needle, and nobody moving when the record ended.
- The record plays: one round button and a hairline. No ornament, no game. The
  note is honest that this record stops before the close of the duet, which is
  the part his point is about, and the Wagner card below shows the closing
  lines and says who sings them. A good recovery; keep the two together.
- The best-proportioned scene: text, record, two cards, one fold. A pleasure
  from end to end. Nothing to change.
