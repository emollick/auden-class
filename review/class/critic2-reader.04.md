# The class page, read by a first visitor: part 04 (the phone, then the whole)

## At 390 (read: door, first, three, ego, love, othello, falstaff, tempest, chart, exam, papers, friday, after, since, end)

- Nothing is cut off and nothing is too small to read. Body type is large; the panels, cards and boards all hold; the list of scenes folds to one row; the Kierkegaard table is re-set for the phone (the board first, then each case). Careful work.
- **`othello`, the one real failure:** the text for a followed name opens under the sixth scheme, more than a screen and a half below the row of names (`pick/othello-390.01.png`). I pressed, saw a name turn red, and had to hunt for the words; to follow a second name I went back up. Change: at 390 put that text directly under the row of names, or repeat the names beside it.
- **`papers`:** the small links under an entry stack about 24 px apart (the stylesheet gives `.set-ways` a 2 px row gap): too close for a thumb. Give each row about 40 px.
- **`chart`:** it scrolls sideways with three and a half of nine pairs in view, and a small note says it runs on to the right. It suits the title and I would accept it. The pair cut off at the edge is the real signal; keep one cut there.
- **`first`:** the nine opera bars are two to four px wide and cannot be pressed one by one; the arrows reach them and have a fair target (about 32 by 40 px by the stylesheet). The names over the bars are dropped, so until a bar is pressed the strip is black and white stripes. Acceptable.
- **`three`:** the note still says "The first column is the blackboard", but on the phone there is no column: the board is the first block. Word it so that it is true at both widths.
- **`friday`:** the first paragraph breaks short twice, after "the coffee." and after the newspaper's name, as if words were tied together. It looks like a stray line break.
- **`ego`:** the record that cannot be played falls between two paragraphs of the account, and its title in display type reads as a subheading. Tolerable.
- **Length:** 34,700 px, about forty-six screens. The navigation does not stay in view and no scene offers a way back to the list, so from the middle a visitor who wants the examination has only the scroll. A small link to the list by each scene's small head would be enough. Low priority.
- **Front page:** the prose file says the whole band is one link, which settles my doubt in part 01.

## The whole

1. **In the room?** Yes, in most scenes, and by small things seen: what he wore, where he looked, where he stood, the needle, the dark outside. Strongest in Kierkegaard's three, Romantic love, the end of *Othello*, the chart, the examination, the Friday evenings and the next course. It turns into a report on sources in four short places: the first paragraph of scene 1; the questionnaire sentences and the small note under the bars in scene 2; the right-hand column of the phonograph in scene 4; the three paragraphs that open Papers. Each needs a move or a cut, not a rewrite.
2. **Pleasure and drag.** A pleasure wherever there is something to look at with the words: the table, the boards, the two panels of questions, the record, the strokes. First place I wanted to skip: scene 2, the two sentences on the questionnaire, because the page stops there to weigh a source. Where it drags most: from the fifth paragraph of Falstaff through the sixth of *The Tempest*. Too long for what they give: *The Tempest* before its board, Papers before its list. The order is clear.
3. **The work that survives.** A visitor can find it: two panels of questions in the flow, the cantos, "The work on record" gathering all eight with links, and the project of the next course. Can understand it: the questions are plain, the cantos are shown at their size, the dictionary project is shown by one word. Can explore it: the passages under each panel, "Read it here" for four cantos, a poem in each of three languages, a song with its measure marked. **Nothing is asked of the visitor:** no test, no box, no score, no "now you try". The imperatives in the panels are 1941's and their small heads say so. One thing is missing: what happened on the Tuesday of the first panel (part 02).
4. **Whose words.** Clear nearly everywhere, and by one steady means: a small head, the witness's page at the right, and phrases such as "as Pearce set it down", "in this site's words", "as Miller tells it". Three soft spots. The six definitions mix quotation marks and none. The two panels of questions, set large in italic without marks, will be read as Auden's own sentences. And the rule that settles both ("outside quotation marks it is this site's") stands only inside the shut fold at the foot. Change: bring that one sentence up into the lede.
5. **The look.** A finely made book from end to end: paper, ink, hairlines, one red. No chalk, no green board, no classroom, no game; the one thing that makes a sound is a plain round button. Nothing ugly or crowded. Faults at 1280 are small: bare right halves (the phonograph in scene 4; opened folds), the hanging line in *The Tempest*, grey small notes that carry the page's best numbers, and in the first *Othello* scheme one name standing alone at the right, which invites a meaning the foot of the page says is only this site's arrangement. At 390: the list above.
6. **The way in.** Part 01: it invites and says what is there. It sits below the fold at laptop height, and at 390 the only visible sign that it opens is a small arrow.
7. **Three best things, not to be lost.**
   - The questions set for the next meeting, each set in its own panel with the passages beneath, and the way the second set is answered by the page itself: the next scene opens on his answer in large type, and *The Tempest* closes on the third. It is the experience of being set a question and then hearing the teacher answer, with nothing asked of the visitor.
   - The *Othello* blackboard with one name followed across the six schemes and, under it, what he said as he wrote, marked as Pearce's report in this site's words. (Make sure it is seen: part 03.)
   - The figures that show the size of the work without an adjective: the reading list drawn to scale and the cantos drawn as strokes, each a way into the site. With them, the chart left unexplained and the plain sentences that say where the record stops.

**In order of worth:** show the *Othello* remarks without a press; open scene 1 on the knock and cut the questionnaire caveat in scene 2; move the two cards up into Falstaff and *The Tempest*; lift the three numbers out of the small notes; put the list first in Papers; then the phone points above. Nothing was refused by the permission system; I wrote only these four parts and the pictures under `review/class/shots-reader/`.
