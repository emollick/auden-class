# The class page, read by a first visitor: part 03 (scenes 6 to 14 at 1280, and the order)

Parts 03 and 04 are written one point to a line, unwrapped, to keep the whole report under 250 lines. Part 02 ran to 116 lines, over the limit of 100; I did not rewrite it, because parts are not to be rewritten.

- **6. *Othello* on the blackboard (`othello`).** The best scene, and it holds the worst fault of the page. The six schemes are redrawn in the site's own hand (no chalk, no green), all six legible at once, and following a name across them is analysis, not a game (`pick/othello-1280.00.png`). But what Auden said of each person as he wrote, which is the lecture itself, appears only when a name is pressed, and the lead-in "Follow one name" promises a highlight, not words. Unpressed, only the first scheme gets a reason, a name set between dashes is unexplained, and Miller's objection arrives before the visitor has heard the argument he objected to. Change: load the board with one name already followed (Iago, whom the questions are about), or let the lead-in say that his remarks stand behind the names.
- Same scene, the work set: "FOR NEXT TIME" is as clear as the first panel. Its last line in small italic (he left in the dark with his arms full) is the room at its best. The two rows beneath, which point on to the scenes where he answered, are what made me want to read on. Keep all three.
- **7. Falstaff and the Prince (`falstaff`).** Opens on his own answer, set large, with "Auden, as Pearce set it down" over it: the payoff of the question, and exact about whose words they are. Then five paragraphs of reported argument with nothing to look at; the fifth packs some ten claims into one paragraph and I slowed. The last paragraph (the notes stop, the dark, what was lost) is the room again and among the best on the page. Change: the card at the foot shows the very moment the third paragraph tells; move it up beside that paragraph.
- **8. *The Tempest* (`tempest`).** The first paragraph closes an arc opened in scene 3: he now faces the class. Then five more paragraphs of "he said", "he called", "he told the class" before the first thing to look at. Coming straight after Falstaff, **this is where the page drags most**: by the fourth paragraph I had skipped down to the blackboard. It is the one scene too long for what it gives before its figure. Change: bring the card of Ariel's song up to the paragraph on the sea, which is about it; no new words are needed. The three lines of the board, left without comment as he left them, are right.
- Same scene, the look: at 1280 the one-sentence paragraph on Valéry hangs at the foot of the left column, lower than the end of the right column, and reads as if it came after "At the close". Join it to the paragraph before it, or balance the columns.
- **9. A chart the width of the room (`chart`).** A title, five sentences, and the chart across the whole page as it ran across the room, with no explanation because none is recorded. The page's best use of silence: the visitor stands where the students stood. Change nothing at 1280.
- **10. The examination (`exam`).** Room and pleasure throughout: pens out for the first time that term, the bargain and its four-word ending, the kitchen sink, the one word left on a paper. The four cantos drawn as strokes, each with "Read it here", show the size of the task and ask nothing of me. "About thirty words a minute for three hours" is the most telling number on the page, and it stands in grey small type at the end of a note. Change: lift it to the figure's own line, as with the pages and weeks in scene 2. The note that sends me to the foot for the other accounts came just as I had noticed that the counts of cantos disagree; well placed.
- **11. Papers and exercises (`papers`).** Here the page becomes a file: a three-line block of citations beside the head, then three paragraphs, two of which say what has not been found, and which repeat things already told (the play outside the list, the critic, the records). But "THE WORK ON RECORD" under them is exactly what the owner asked for: eight things that were set, each with its witness, most with a way up to its scene or across to a passage. Change: put that list first, directly under the head, and let the paragraphs follow it as notes, thinned.
- **12. At 1223 Pontiac Trail (`friday`).** Two short paragraphs, each ending well. A breath after the file. Keep.
- **13. The next course (`after`).** The exchange over Wordsworth is the one place where a student speaks in class and is answered. The card of one word, with its last sentence ("That class was to do this for every word of 'Lycidas.'"), makes the term's project real without setting it to anyone. Keep.
- **14. What they said of it (`since`).** Six short quotations, each with who spoke, of what, and the page, the student who dropped out among them. The right ending. The sources follow, and the fold "Where this page may be wrong" is honest and rightly shut.
- **The order** is clear. From the office door to the examination it runs as one story, and the small heads carry it ("The same afternoon · after the recess", "The next meeting", "The same afternoon"). The last four are not afternoons and their small heads say so. I would move nothing.
- **The folds** ("What Auden wrote", "What two of the class wrote") are short and each is a payoff. Opened at 1280 they fill only the left half and leave the right bare. In scenes 3 and 7 the same kind of fold stands open under a small-capital label beside a card; in four others it is a shut italic row. Two dresses for one thing; harmless.
