# Critic by word, second reading: part .02 (scenes 7, 8 and 9)

## Scene 7, Falstaff and the Prince

20. [scenes[6].paras[0]] "He began with his own answer…"
    - Fault: "the question he had set" is one of four things set the time before; the reader has to work out which.
    - Put: "He began with his own answer to the question about Iago and Falstaff, and Pearce was a little startled."
21. [scenes[6].paras[1]] "In Falstaff it is usually missed…"
    - Fault: Hotspur is named with no word of who he is. Two paragraphs on, "the other rebel lords" explains him too late.
    - Put: "In Falstaff it is usually missed, and Auden pointed to what Falstaff does to the body of the rebel Hotspur when he is alone with it."
22. [scenes[6].paras[2]] "The Prince, he went on, is…"
    - Fault: the Prince stands in the title and here, and nothing says who he is. A reader who does not know the play cannot place "his father" or "the crown" lower down.
    - Put: "Falstaff's companion the Prince, he went on, is a schemer under the charm, one of the most hateful people in Shakespeare."
23. [scenes[6].paras[2]] "At the end of Part 2…"
    - Fault: in "helped him to the crown," "him" can be the Prince, who is the "he" of the whole paragraph.
    - Put: "At the end of Part 2 he drops his old companions without regret, because he has no more use for them, as the King his father dropped the Percies, the family that had helped him to the crown."
24. [scenes[6].paras[4]] "He read Falstaff as a disappointed…"
    - Fault: "are just what fail" has to be read twice.
    - Put: "He read Falstaff as a disappointed man, a countryman adrift in the city, whose wit and feeling are the very things that do not work in a world run on calculation."
25. [scenes[6].paras[4]] "The belly, he said, is a…"
    - Fault: "he" is Auden and then Falstaff in one sentence.
    - Put: "The belly, he said, is a mock pregnancy, of a kind the physician Georg Groddeck had studied, and the child in it is Falstaff himself."
26. [scenes[6].paras[4]] "In a world like that, he…"
    - Fault: the same: "he said" is Auden and "he stands for" is Falstaff.
    - Put: "Falstaff, Auden said, cannot last in a world like that, and neither can the Merry England he stands for."
27. [scenes[6].paras[5]] "Pearce's notes stop there. It was…"
    - Fault: six short sentences in a row, four of them opening on "He." The staccato calls attention to itself.
    - Put: "Pearce's notes stop there: it was dark and he was exhausted. More followed, and he believes it was on Rilke's *Elegies*, a book that is not on the list. He meant to write it down that night, and lost it. He also warns that Auden must have talked of other things that day."
28. [scenes[6].parts[0].doors[0].why] "The new King drops Falstaff, at…"
    - Fault: the page has not said that the Prince becomes King, so "the new King" is a stranger; the comma is not wanted.
    - Put: "The Prince, now King, drops Falstaff at the end of Part 2."

## Scene 8, *The Tempest*

29. [scenes[7].paras[0]] "By then, he says, Auden was…"
    - Fault: "he says" is Pearce and "he still spoke" is Auden, in one sentence.
    - Put: "By then, he says, Auden no longer feared the class and stood facing it, though still speaking to a point high on the back wall."
30. [scenes[7].paras[1]] "So a reader's knowledge of Shakespeare…"
    - Fault: a conclusion on "So" with no speaker; it reads as the site's own.
    - Put: "So, he said, a reader's knowledge of Shakespeare goes just as far as his experience of life."
31. [scenes[7].paras[3]] "He called the form romance, or…"
    - Fault: "or better a fairy tale" wants its commas, or "better" reads as part of the name.
    - Put: "He called the form romance or, better, a fairy tale."
32. [scenes[7].paras[3]] "Prospero's fault, he said, was to…"
    - Fault: the pronouns change owner four times: "he said" and "whom he called" are Auden; "himself," "his brother" and "turn him out" are Prospero; "he learns" in the next sentence is Prospero again.
    - Put: "Prospero's fault, Auden said, was to neglect the plain work of governing so as to perfect himself in secret arts, and his brother, whom Auden called Prospero's other self, was right to turn him out. What Prospero learns on the island is the art of living, which is to honor the claims of ordinary life."
33. [scenes[7].paras[4]] "He called Prospero Shakespeare's complete man…"
    - Fault: three names run together at the start. And "testament" is on the house list of stock words, though here it has its plain sense.
    - Put: "Prospero, he said, is Shakespeare's complete man, and Shakespeare himself, with the play as his testament." For the word: if it is the word Pearce gives Auden, print it in quotation marks so that it is seen to be his; if it is the site's, I do not know which sense is meant (a last will, or a statement of belief) and cannot choose for you.
34. [scenes[7].paras[4]] "It is no longer the rapture…" and "It is the plain first…"
    - Fault: "It is no longer X. It is Y." is the "not X but Y" build in two sentences, and neither has a speaker.
    - Put, as one sentence: "In them the rapture of *Romeo and Juliet*, which the class had been given to read, has become the plain first step toward marriage and a family."
35. [scenes[7].paras[5]] "He wrote them on the blackboard…"
    - Fault: the sentence before has Goethe as its subject, so "He" is Goethe for a moment.
    - Put: "Auden wrote them on the blackboard and left them without a word of comment."
36. [scenes[7].parts[0].src] "As Pearce copied it, pp. 151–52"
    - Fault: two labels for one thing. The boards of scenes 6 and 9 say "From Pearce's copy."
    - Put: "From Pearce's copy, pp. 151–52"
37. [scenes[7].parts[0].lines[0–2]] the three lines of the board
    - Not a mark on the words, only something that looks uneven: the first two lines end in a period and the third does not, and in the second the period stands outside the closing quotation mark. If Pearce prints them so, leave them.
38. [scenes[7].parts[1].paras[0]] "When he left the board he…"
    - Fault: the part opens on "he" straight under a line that names only Pearce.
    - Put: "When Auden left the board he went on with Gonzalo: a blunderer at times, and a bore, whom Shakespeare now sees as good at heart and to be trusted."
39. [scenes[7].parts[1].paras[0]] "Shakespeare no longer admires cleverness, and…"
    - Fault: no speaker. A judgment on Shakespeare in the site's own voice.
    - Put, joined to the sentence before so that "he said" covers it: "The brilliant talkers of the earlier plays, he said, have shrunk in this one to hangers-on: Shakespeare no longer admires cleverness, and for ordinary life he prefers the gentle and the honest."
40. [scenes[7].parts[1].paras[1]] "Then Auden talked briefly about Valéry…"
    - Fault: Valéry is named with no word of who he is, and "about … of that" changes preposition on the way.
    - Put: "Then Auden talked briefly about the poet Valéry, and Pearce kept none of it."
41. [scenes[7].parts[2].doors[1]] the small words of the second card
    - Fault: one label for two things. The card that opens the last speech of the play carries "Ariel's song" in its small words and "Prospero to the audience" at its foot. The small words are cut from the piece, so the mending is in what this card shows: leave the third term off, or put the heading of the passage there.
42. [scenes[7].parts[2].doors[0].why] "Ariel's song, where the change is…"
    - Fault: "the change" leans on a sentence four paragraphs and a blackboard back ("a change that comes close to…"). On the card it has nothing to point to.
    - Put: "Ariel's song, where the change the sea works is said twice as plain fact."
43. [scenes[7].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]] "Prospero is to be admired for…"
    - Fault: three clauses strung on "and," the second of which is a contrast.
    - Put: "Prospero is to be admired for his gifts and his strength, he wrote, but cannot be liked, and Caliban has the better of their argument."

## Scene 9, A chart the width of the room

44. [scenes[8].paras[0]] "When he had finished with *The…"
    - Fault: the scene opens on "he," and the only name above it is Pearce's, in the line of sources.
    - Put: "When Auden had finished with *The Tempest* he went to the left edge of the blackboard and copied out a chart he had made for the class."
45. [scenes[8].paras[0]] "Pearce copied it down. He records…"
    - Fault: two short sentences close the paragraph, and this "He" is Pearce where the "He" two sentences back is Auden.
    - Put: "Pearce copied it down and records no word of explanation."
