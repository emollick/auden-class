## "Where this page may be wrong": two findings

### B-11 (small) [caveats[0]]

- The claim: "Pearce lent them to Michael Kreyling, who wrote on Millar’s
  novels, and they have not been printed (Kreyling, p. 40)."
- Fault: (a) for "have not been printed", and (e) for the page.
- The record: Kreyling's notes credit Millar's class notes to the courtesy of
  Donald Pearce (A 399, A 525), and his p. 40 says which two books drew the
  fullest notes (A 403). No source says the notes are unprinted. The phrase
  comes from a suggestion of the first examiner (exam-others.md, lines 651 to
  655), who elsewhere asked that such sentences say "has been found", since
  they speak for everything that exists (lines 272 to 276 and 374 to 377).
  "Lent" is a reading of the credit. The credit is in the notes, not on p. 40.
- Smallest change: "Michael Kreyling, who wrote on Millar’s novels, had them
  by courtesy of Pearce, and no printing of them has been found (Kreyling,
  p. 40 and his notes)."
