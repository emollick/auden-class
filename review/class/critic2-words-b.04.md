# Critic by word, second reading: part .04 (scenes 12, 13 and 14, Sources, the fold)

## Scene 12, At 1223 Pontiac Trail

67. [scenes[11].paras[0]] "The class went on at his…"
    - Fault: "his house" opens the scene under a line that names only Miller, and Miller lived in the house too.
    - Put: "The class went on at Auden's house."
68. [scenes[11].paras[1]] "For Thanksgiving Day he invited anyone…"
    - Fault: the sentence before has Miller as its subject, so "he" can be Miller, who could as well have invited people to that house.
    - Put: "For Thanksgiving Day Auden invited anyone who wished to come to the house between two and four."

## Scene 13, The next course

69. [scenes[12].paras[0]] "Before turning to the poem of…", with [parts[0].items[0].text] and the last sentence of [parts[0].doors[0].why]
    - Fault: the project is told three times in one short scene: in the paragraph, in the first line of the list, and on the card. The second telling adds nothing to the first.
    - Put: let the list line carry it whole and shorten the paragraph. Paragraph: "Before turning to the poem of the day, Auden gave that class its project for the term, on Milton's “Lycidas.” A student who did it told Pearce that “Lycidas” was the one English poem he felt he really knew." List line: "For each word of “Lycidas,” its origin and every meaning it had had down to the year of the poem, listed from the *Oxford English Dictionary*".
70. [scenes[12].paras[1]] "Auden's own answer began further back…"
    - Fault: "the line between them" reads first as a boundary, then turns out to be a telephone line, and is then likened to a telephone.
    - Put: "Auden's own answer began further back, with how a poem gets written: the poet's conscious mind has to reach his daemon, or genius, and the connection between them is like the telephone between the village and the Castle in Kafka, which works on some days and fails on others."
71. [scenes[12].parts[0].items[1].by] "One section is on “Lycidas,” with…"
    - Fault: the line can be understood by itself, but it does not say plainly who records the examination. It ends in "*Prose*, vol. II," and nothing on this page says what that book is or whose. The Sources of this page have no entry for it, nor for *The Dyer's Hand*, though the folds of scenes 3 to 8 cite both.
    - Put: "Printed in Auden's collected *Prose*, vol. II, pp. 467–70. One section is on “Lycidas,” with gaps to be filled a word at a time, one is on Donne, Hopkins and scansion, and one is an essay". For the Sources, one entry in the page's short form, from the about page's own list: "W. H. Auden, *The Dyer's Hand* (1962); *Prose*, vol. II, edited by Edward Mendelson (2002)."
72. [scenes[12].parts[0].doors[0].why] "One word, as this site's notes…"
    - Fault: four short sentences, and the first explains the card instead of showing it. "This site's notes" is a working word: the reader has met no notes.
    - Put: "One word, done as that class was to do every word of “Lycidas”: *outrageous* meant violent, comes from the Latin for *beyond*, and has no root in *rage*."

The label of the card does not claim that the passage is one the class worked on: it says plainly that the class had "Lycidas." Good.

## Scene 14, What they said of it

73. [scenes[13].title] "What they said of it"
    - Fault: "they" and "it" have no owners in a title. In the list of the scenes it follows "The next course," and "it" reads as that course.
    - Put: "What the students said"
74. [scenes[13].paras[0]] "The class had names for him."
    - Fault: the scene opens on "him." The title does not name Auden, and the line above names five other people.
    - Put: "The class had names for Auden."
75. [scenes[13].parts[0].items[0].by] "Kenneth Millar · the teaching assistant who…"
    - Fault: the other lines say of what the words were said, and this one does not. "As Pearce tells it" hangs on "sat next to Pearce," where it is meant for the saying.
    - Put, if the words are about Auden (p. 132 will show): "Kenneth Millar, the teaching assistant who sat next to Pearce · about Auden, as Pearce reports it · Pearce, p. 132"
76. [scenes[13].parts[0].items[1].by] "Robert Chapman · about the class, once…" and [items[3].by] "Frances Aaronson Lewis · a senior that…"
    - Fault: "him" and "his" are Auden, who is named in neither line. The only man named is Chapman.
    - Put: "Robert Chapman · about the class, once it could follow Auden · Chapman, p. 508" and "Frances Aaronson Lewis · a senior that year, about one of Auden's two courses · Chapman, p. 513"

Inside the quotation marks of [scenes[13].paras[0]], the second nickname has a British double "l." If Pearce prints it so, it stays.

## Sources

77. [sources[0]] "…reprinted in his *Para/Worlds* (1989)."
    - Fault: "his" follows "edited by Alan Bold."
    - Put: "…pp. 129–57; reprinted in Pearce's *Para/Worlds* (1989)."

## Where this page may be wrong

78. [caveats[0]] "After them he wrote a hurried…"
    - Fault: "and later whole sentences stayed with him" can mean later in the term or later in life, and "later whole" runs together.
    - Put: nothing without the fact from p. 134. If it is later in the term: "After them he wrote a hurried page or two in a spiral notebook when a lecture was over. Later in the term whole sentences stayed with him."
79. [caveats[0]] "A second student kept notes as…" and "Pearce lent them to Michael…"
    - Fault: "them" has to be Millar's notes, and because it is odd that Pearce had them to lend, the reader doubts the pronoun and wonders whether Pearce's own are meant.
    - Put, if they are Millar's: "A second student kept notes as well: Kenneth Millar, whose notes Pearce lent to Michael Kreyling, the author of a book on Millar's novels. They have not been printed (Kreyling, p. 40)."
80. [caveats[1]] "He tells the three parts before…"
    - Fault: "the three parts" is a working word. The page has called these afternoons, and the reader must work out which three are meant.
    - Put: "He tells what comes before it in the reverse order: [the Thursday on Kierkegaard](#/class/three) first, then the first meeting, then the card on the office door. Here they are set by date."

Nothing else in the fold is marked. Its run of sentences of one build in [caveats[6]] ("Miller has six," "Farnan … also has six," "Nolan has," "Pearce has seven") is right for five accounts laid side by side.
