# Critic by word, second reading: part .06 (corrections, and what is still there from the first reading)

After my own reading was saved I opened the two scripts and then the first critic's report (review/class/critic-words-b.md). Six of my marks fall and four change.

## Marks withdrawn

- Mark 9. The six names are not a second telling. The fold ([caveats[4]]) rests the first column of scheme I on this sentence: "the six whom Auden named together in the lecture." Leave it.
- Mark 17. The asks are numbered on the page (the style sheet counts them), so "the second" and "the third" can be found.
- Mark 36. "As Pearce copied it" over the three lines, and "From Pearce's copy" over the schemes and the chart, differ on purpose. The lines are as he printed them; the layout of the other two is this site's ([caveats[4]], [caveats[5]]).
- Mark 40. The sentence is right as it stands: "of that" goes with "kept nothing," not with "talked." Keep it.
- Mark 41. The card does not show "Ariel's song." The script puts "Prospero to the audience" in the place of the piece's title. The prose file lists a word that this card does not print.
- Mark 45. "He" follows "Pearce" directly, and the last sentence is the page's model for saying that nothing is known. Keep both sentences.

## Marks changed

- Marks 2 and 5, and the first of the worst faults at the head of part .01. I wrote that the line "What Auden said as he wrote, in this site's words" stands under each text. It stands over it: the script prints it first (site/js/class-figures.js), and the prose file lists it last. So the reader is told whose judgments these are before reading them. The fault is light: two of the four texts say "Auden said" in their first sentence and two do not. The Puts still make the four alike. The list at the head of part .07 replaces the one in part .01.
- Mark 20. With the asks numbered, a shorter Put will do: "He began with his own answer to the second question, and Pearce was a little startled."
- Mark 49. The first critic asked that this paragraph be kept whole, and it reads well. My mark is only on the run of four short sentences before the last. It is the lightest in the report.
- Mark 84. My Put brought back a doubt the first critic had removed: "which Auden played" can be taken of the record or of the opening. Keep the three sentences and change only the two uses of "the class": "The page on [the class](#/class) has one more, made in 1903 or 1904. It holds the opening of the love duet from *Tristan*. Auden played the duet in class."

## Two more marks

91. [scenes[7].paras[3]] "What he learns on the island…"
    - Fault: a general judgment ("the art of living, which is to honor the claims of ordinary life") with no speaker. It is of a kind with marks 10, 19, 30, 34 and 39. Each of those sentences steps from the play to a claim about life, about a society or about Shakespeare, and there the site seems to speak for itself. Sentences that stay inside the plot under a paragraph's first "he said" I have not marked.
    - Put, in place of the last sentence of mark 32: "On the island, in Auden's account, Prospero learns the art of living, which is to honor the claims of ordinary life."
92. [scenes[6].paras[1]] "In Falstaff it is usually missed…"
    - Fault: marked by the first critic and still there. The sentence says that Falstaff does something to Hotspur's body and keeps back what. A reader who does not know the play is left outside.
    - Put: nothing without the fact. If Pearce says what Auden pointed to, say it in four or five words.
