# Critic by word, second reading: the class page from scene 6 to the end

Part .01. The marks are numbered through all the parts. "Put" is the sentence I propose.

The worst faults, worst first. This list was written after a first reading of
scenes 6 to 9; the last part gives it again for the whole share.

1. Who is speaking. Under "Follow one name" the texts for Othello and Iago give Auden's judgments as plain statements and name Auden late or last, so the site seems to say for itself that Othello "is not very intelligent." Single sentences in scenes 6, 7 and 8 slide the same way (marks 2, 5, 10, 19, and more in part .02).
2. A "he" or "his" with two owners where Auden and a character share a sentence (marks 3, 4; the Falstaff and Prospero paragraphs in part .02), and a "he" that opens a part straight under a line naming Pearce (mark 8; scenes 8 and 9).
3. Runs of short sentences of one build, most of them opening on "He" (the Iago text, the last paragraph of scene 7).
4. Pointers that make the reader count or guess: "the second," "the third," "For the next day" beside "next Tuesday."

## Scene 6, *Othello* on the blackboard

1. [scenes[5].paras[1]] "Pearce copied them down as a…"
   - Fault: "them" stands two sentences away from "six schemes"; the sentence between is about how Auden talked.
   - Put: "Pearce copied the schemes down as a sample of the diagrams Auden often put up."
2. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[0].says] "Venice needs him as a soldier…" (and the sentence after it, "Warm feeling is his mainspring…")
   - Fault: stated as fact in the site's own voice. Auden is first named in the third sentence, and the line that says whose view this is stands under the text. The texts for Desdemona and Cassio name him in their first sentence; these two do not.
   - Put: "Venice, Auden said, needs him as a soldier and looks down on him as an outsider, partly because he is black, and the strain is constant."
3. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[0].says] "Auden put that down largely to…"
   - Fault: "his" can be Auden's or Othello's, and "that" can be the sureness or the want of it. Also "sex-appeal" is hyphenated in the site's own sentence; the American form is two words (the board keeps its own spelling).
   - Put, if "that" is his doubt of himself outside action (the examiner can settle it from pp. 141–42): "Auden put the doubt down largely to Othello's lack of sex appeal."
4. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[0].says] "So he leans on his record…"
   - Fault: the sentence before has Auden as its subject, so "he" changes owner without a name.
   - Put: "So Othello leans on his record as a soldier, almost as a child would."
5. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[1].says] "Above all he lacks passion."
   - Fault: as mark 2. Auden is named only in the last sentence, so "He is the one brilliant person in the play" reads as the site's judgment.
   - Put: "Above all, Auden said, he lacks passion."
6. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[1].says] "He is the one brilliant person…" to "He is also the only one…"
   - Fault: four sentences in a row open on "He" and have one build.
   - Put: "The one brilliant person in the play, he has the mind [Pascal calls geometrical](#/pascal): he settles everything by reasoning, and reasons without a slip, though he neither sees nor cares for the principles he reasons from. No one else in the play shows any knowledge of the Bible."
7. [scenes[5].parts[0].follow[1].says] "…though the principles are nothing he sees…"
   - Fault: unclear on a first reading: which principles, and what it is not to "see" them. The wording in mark 6 says the same thing in order (he, then the principles he reasons from). If that is not what p. 142 says, the fact is missing and I cannot supply it.
   - Put: as in mark 6.
8. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[0]] "The play, he said, is first…"
   - Fault: a new part opens on "he," and the two lines just above it name both Pearce and Auden.
   - Put: "The play, Auden said, is first of all about a society in layers."
9. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[0]] "The Duke, Brabantio, Roderigo, Cassio, Lodovico…"
   - Fault: told twice. The six names are the first column of scheme I, which stands just above.
   - Put: "The gentles of his first scheme are the upper crust, as he said the class could see for itself."
10. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[0]] "A society like that breeds strain…"
    - Fault: a general claim in the present with no speaker. It reads as the site's own moral on the scheme.
    - Put: "A society like that, he said, breeds strain, misunderstanding, obsession, even paranoia (p. 141)."
11. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[1]] "It seemed to him flippant about…"
    - Fault: "It" has no singular noun to stand for; the sentence before speaks of "the diagrams."
    - Put: "All this seemed to him flippant about Shakespeare, and he says other students were let down as he was (Miller, p. 29)."
12. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[2]] "Near the end Auden said that…"
    - Fault: the paragraph before is Miller's memory, so "the end" has nothing to be the end of.
    - Put: "Near the end of the afternoon Auden said that the saint and the villain have much the same psychology."
13. [scenes[5].parts[1].paras[2]] "In both of them ethics and…"
    - Fault: "both" comes twice, and the second can be taken for ethics and esthetics.
    - Put: "In each of them ethics and esthetics, the terms of [the Kierkegaard afternoon](#/class/three), have all but become one thing, and saint and villain stand back from other people in the same way (p. 143)."
14. [scenes[5].parts[2].asks[0]] "For the next day: how does…"
    - Fault: does not follow from what stands around it. The part is labeled "For next time," the scene is "An afternoon before a weekend," the next ask says "next Tuesday," and scene 7 is "The next meeting." A reader cannot tell whether the class met the next day.
    - Put: nothing without the fact. What is missing is when Pearce says this was due (p. 143). If the record says no more than "the next day," leave it.
15. [scenes[5].parts[2].asks[3]] "They were to go with the…"
    - Fault: "They" can be the class, which is the nearest plural, or the stories.
    - Put: "The stories were to go with the study of *The Tempest*."
16. [scenes[5].parts[2].doors[1].why] "Falstaff answers a judge by turning…"
    - Fault: "his words" has two owners.
    - Put: "Falstaff answers a judge by turning the judge's words around."
17. [scenes[5].parts[2].answers[0].text] and [answers[1].text] "The next meeting opened on his…", "The lecture on *The Tempest*…"
    - Fault: "the second" and "the third" make the reader count four lines set large. Fine if the asks are numbered on the page; if not, name them.
    - Put: "The next meeting opened on his own answer about Iago and Falstaff." "The lecture on *The Tempest* in December closed on the question about Shakespeare."
18. [scenes[5].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]] "Twenty years later he began his…"
    - Fault: "the play with the villain" reads for a moment as one phrase.
    - Put: "Twenty years later his essay on the play opened with the villain."
19. [scenes[5].parts[2].folds[0].paras[0]] "Othello's fall is another man's work…"
    - Fault: no "he wrote," and the "so" reads as the site drawing its own conclusion.
    - Put: "Othello's fall, he went on, is another man's work, so an audience pities Othello without respecting him."
