## Scene 11, "Papers and exercises" (scenes[10]): three findings in all

### B-06 (doubtful) [scenes[10].parts[0].items[2]]

- The claim: the list sets "A paper called “The Comic Hero”" ("Millar’s,
  cited in Kreyling’s endnotes") as an item of its own, directly under "A
  paper comparing the *Divine Comedy* with *The Castle*" ("Kenneth Millar’s:
  Nolan, p. 53"). A reader counts two papers by Millar.
- Fault: (c) states as fact what the record says is not known, and (d).
- The record: Nolan p. 53 says one paper of Millar's compared the two books,
  and Kreyling's notes cite a paper of his for English 135 by that title
  (A 399, A 525). Nothing says they are two. The first examiner ruled on the
  same point when the scene said "Kreyling's notes cite another by its
  title": "another" is more than the notes have, and whether it is a second
  paper or the Dante and Kafka paper under its title is not on record
  (exam-others.md, lines 264 to 270 and 567 to 568). The sentence was mended
  then. The new list brings the two papers back by its layout.
- Smallest change: one item instead of two, "A paper comparing the *Divine
  Comedy* with *The Castle*", with the small line "Kenneth Millar’s: Nolan,
  p. 53. Kreyling’s endnotes cite a paper of his for the course called “The
  Comic Hero,” which may be this one." Or keep two items and add to the
  second: "Whether it is the paper above under its title is not recorded."

### B-07 (small) [scenes[10].parts[0].items[5].by]

- The claim: under "A poem put into English from a language never studied,
  Latin, French or German, with a dictionary and nothing else" the small line
  reads "Early in the term, in one course or the other. Chapman chose a poem
  of Rilke’s: Chapman, p. 509".
- Fault: (d) one word, and (e) a witness left off.
- The record, A 405: Chapman's own page, as read in search extracts, has the
  exercise set quite early in the semester, the dictionary as the only aid,
  each student picking the language, and Chapman himself doing a poem of
  Rilke. What the students picked was the language. Who picked the poem is
  not recorded. The three languages are given in the record from Carpenter
  p. 319, who follows Chapman (A 405, B 85 to 89). content/about.mjs line 101
  cites both: "Chapman, p. 509; Carpenter, p. 319".
- Smallest change: "Early in the term, in one course or the other. Chapman’s
  was a poem of Rilke’s: Chapman, p. 509; Carpenter, p. 319".

### Carried over from B-04 [scenes[10].parts[0].items[0] and items[6].by]

Not counted again. The two items from Stevens's notebook are treated
differently: the poems to a set scansion are "for one course or the other",
the papers comparing two works of art carry no caution. Both come from one
notebook (Farnan p. 73, A 400). If she sat in on both courses, neither item
is certainly English 135's. If the ground for "both" is too weak (B-04),
neither needs the caution. A 400 gives only a judgment of fit: the verse
sounds like the spring course, the papers fit what Pearce and Nolan report
of the fall one.

### Looked at and not counted

- [scenes[10].paras[2]] "Nolan says Auden played opera records in class to
  make points about the books (p. 53)." A 429 quotes Nolan's sentence, which
  has no words for "in class". Note A files it under its own heading "In
  class", and the first examiner proposed this very wording (exam-others.md,
  lines 298 to 304). I leave it. "Nolan says Auden played opera records to
  make points about the books" would be the sentence without the addition.
- [scenes[10].parts[0].items[5].ways] "Horace, in Latin", "Rimbaud, in
  French", "Goethe, in German". The labels claim nothing in so many words.
  But the only poem on record is Chapman's Rilke, the three authors named are
  all on Auden's list, and the links stand straight under the line about
  Chapman. A reader could take them for poems that were set. A word before
  them would remove the doubt, for example "To try on this site:". The other
  short links in the list ("The last lines of the *Paradiso*", "K. walks
  toward the Castle", "A song with its measure marked", and the three that go
  to scenes) cannot be misread in that way.
- [scenes[10].src] The line has "Miller, p. 28" and no "pp. 26–27", and no
  Carpenter. Under the rule of remake/class/BRIEF.md (a fact from another
  witness carries its own citation) nothing is wrong: items[7].by gives
  Miller's pages itself.
- [scenes[10].paras[0]] "none of the accounts read for this site tells of a
  quiz or a midterm examination." This is the wording the first examiner
  proposed, knowing that Miller has Auden say at the first class that they
  would be "quizzed" on what they thought (exam-others.md, lines 272 to 276;
  A 410). The class page gives that remark in scenes[1].paras[2] as "would be
  examined", and content/about.mjs line 91 as "would be quizzed". No
  contradiction, but the two pages use different words for one remark.
- [scenes[10].parts[0].items[4].by] "Two sets survive: Pearce, pp. 135 and
  143–44". E lines 263 to 270 put the questions of the second set on p. 143
  and only the shout about fairy stories on p. 144. The range matches the
  part it links to (scenes[5].parts[2], "Pearce, pp. 143–44"), so it stands.
  content/about.mjs line 96 has "pp. 135 and 143" for the same thing.
