# Critic by word, second reading: part .03 (scenes 10 and 11)

## Scene 10, The examination

46. [scenes[9].when] "December 1941 · the examination after Christmas"
    - Fault: the line over the heading repeats the heading's own word and does not say what happened in December.
    - Put: "Announced in December 1941 · held after Christmas"
47. [scenes[9].paras[0]] "Then he said what the final…"
    - Fault: the scene opens on "he," under a line that names four witnesses.
    - Put: "Then Auden said what the final examination would be."
48. [scenes[9].paras[0]] "The class would write out seven…" and "It was the version, he…"
    - Fault: the colon comes after "the Modern Library volume on the list," so the cantos that follow seem to unpack the list, sixteen words away from "seven cantos." Inside Auden's announcement the page steps out to say what Pearce names. Then "he told them" follows "Pearce names."
    - Put: "The class would write out seven cantos of the *Divine Comedy* from memory: the third and fifth of the *Inferno*, some of the *Purgatorio* and the last of the *Paradiso*. Of the *Purgatorio* cantos Pearce names the twenty-eighth. The text was the prose translation in the Modern Library volume on the list, the version, Auden told them, that Eliot used in *The Waste Land*."
49. [scenes[9].paras[1]] "He cut the seven cantos to…" down to "He did not telephone."
    - Fault: a paragraph of seven short sentences, the last four opening on "He" or "If he." The last one is a good plain ending and loses its force in the run.
    - Put: "He cut the seven cantos to five and offered a bargain: he was about to drive to California, and if he could not learn the five himself on the way, he would telephone and call the examination off. He did not telephone."
50. [scenes[9].paras[2]] "Some weeks on, a canto took…"
    - Fault: "Some weeks on" is British usage.
    - Put: "Some weeks later a canto took three afternoons."
51. [scenes[9].paras[3]] "Miller remembered that in the fall…"
    - Fault: "had typed pages … pinned" reads first as "she had typed pages." And "in the fall" and "six" stand two paragraphs under Pearce's seven cut to five, set in December, with no word that this is another account.
    - Put: "Miller remembered that in the fall Angelyn Stevens, a faculty wife who sat in on both of Auden's courses that year, kept typed pages of Dante pinned above her kitchen sink, to learn her cantos over the dishes: six of them, in his account (pp. 77–78)."
52. [scenes[9].paras[3]] "Dorothy Farnan, who was not in…"
    - Fault: she is named only by what she was not. The about page says who she was.
    - Put: "Dorothy Farnan, a graduate student who knew people in the class, tells of students up late over their cantos, and of senior professors who went to Auden and could not move him (pp. 76–77)."
53. [scenes[9].paras[4]] "He filled five examination books…", "He thinks the memorizing…", "He says he has…"
    - Fault: three sentences in a row of one build, "He" and a verb.
    - Put, for the last: "In his own classes since, he says, he has asked the same, and met the same resistance, and then gratitude."
54. [scenes[9].parts[0].note[0]] "Each stroke is a paragraph of…"
    - Fault: "as long as the paragraph" says that stroke and paragraph are one length.
    - Put: "Each stroke is one paragraph of the prose, drawn to its length."
55. [scenes[9].parts[0].note[0]] "Five cantos of their average length…"
    - Fault: "their" is two sentences away from "these four cantos," and the sentence between speaks of five and of seven.
    - Put: "Five cantos of the average length of these four would be about 5,300 words, or about thirty words a minute for three hours."
56. [scenes[9].parts[0].note[1]] "The other accounts differ on the…" and "They are set side by…"
    - Fault: "set" in two senses in two sentences: assigned, then laid out.
    - Put: "The other accounts differ on the number of cantos, on when they were assigned, and on whether they were an examination or stood in place of one. They are laid side by side at the foot of this page, under “Where this page may be wrong,” and at more length on [the page about this site](#/about)."

## Scene 11, Papers and exercises

57. [scenes[10].src] the line of witnesses and pages
    - Fault: not a fact but a mismatch on the page. The last line of the list cites Miller, pp. 26–27, and this line has only "Miller, p. 28."
    - Put: "Miller, pp. 26–28"
58. [scenes[10].paras[1]] "Pearce names *Romeo and Juliet* and…" and "Farnan says he recommended…"
    - Fault: "he" comes after Pearce and Wilson Knight and is Auden. With the next paragraph these are four sentences of one build (Miller says, Pearce names, Farnan says, Nolan says). "The critic" was said of Knight in scene 6.
    - Put, as one sentence: "Pearce names *Romeo and Juliet* and Wilson Knight (pp. 137 and 139), and Farnan says Auden recommended Georg Groddeck's *Book of the It*, which the library would lend only to graduate students (p. 77)."

### The list "The work on record"

Lines that stand by themselves and say plainly who records them: items[0] and items[1]. The rest:

59. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[1].ways] "The last lines of the *Paradiso*", "K. walks toward the Castle"; and [items[5].ways] "Horace, in Latin", "Rimbaud, in French", "Goethe, in German"
    - Fault: each label is true to its passage and promises no more than the link gives. But the script sets them straight under the witness line with only an arrow (site/js/class.js, the `set-ways` span). Under "Kenneth Millar's" the two passages read as the ones his paper compared. Under "Chapman chose a poem of Rilke's" three other poets read as the poems that were set.
    - Put: no change to the labels. Have the script put the page's own label before any link to a passage: "On this site:" The links to other scenes do not need it.
60. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[2].by] "Millar's, cited in Kreyling's endnotes"
    - Fault: the one line of the list with no page. Missing: the page of the endnote. I cannot supply it.
61. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[3].by] "Pearce's own: Pearce, p. 155"
    - Fault: the name twice in four words.
    - Put: "His own: Pearce, p. 155"
62. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[4].by] and its two links "For Tuesday", "For next time"
    - Fault: the links carry the labels of the parts they lead to, and here those say nothing: which Tuesday, which next time. And "survive" is a second word for what the heading calls "on record."
    - Put: "Two sets are on record: Pearce, pp. 135 and 143–44". Links: "The questions on *The Castle*" and "The questions after *Othello*".
63. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[5].text] "A poem put into English from…"
    - Fault: "a language never studied" does not say by whom, and the three languages trail after it.
    - Put: "A poem put into English from a language the student had not studied (Latin, French or German), with a dictionary and nothing else"
64. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[5].by] "Early in the term, in one…"
    - Fault: the first sentence has no witness, and "in one course or the other" is this site's caution; the reader cannot tell it from what Chapman says.
    - Put: "Early in the term; Chapman does not say in which course. He chose a poem of Rilke's: Chapman, p. 509"
65. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[6].text] "Poems written to a set scansion" and its link "A song with its measure marked"
    - Fault: "scansion" is a word of the trade and is said in plain words neither here nor in scene 13, where it comes again. The link calls the same thing "measure." And "a song" is Ariel's song (the link opens the *Tempest* piece at it), which scene 8 has already named.
    - Put: "Poems written to a set scansion, a fixed pattern of beats". Link: "Ariel's song, with its beats marked".
66. [scenes[10].parts[0].items[7]] "Cantos of the *Divine Comedy*, written out…" with "Pearce, pp. 153–54; Miller, pp. 26–27"
    - Fault: the small line gives two witnesses for one statement, and only one of them says it. On this page Miller has the cantos learned by heart as an exercise; the writing out is Pearce's.
    - Put: "Cantos of the *Divine Comedy*, learned by heart". Under it: "Written out at the examination: Pearce, pp. 153–54. Set as an exercise at the first class: Miller, pp. 26–27".
