# Card, second look (critic B)

Looked at: the eight pictures in review/card/final (the two served ones are the same bytes as site/card-1.png and site/card-x-1.png), lines 6 to 19 of site/index.html, build/card.mjs, and the About and class data for the facts. Measured with ImageMagick, to the screen only. Wrote this file and no other. Nothing was refused. What X and other sites do with a card is from memory, since I was told not to look it up.

## A. X's card

1. The whole card. Nothing wrong. It is plain and handsome, and nothing looks cheap or broken. Ink runs from row 42 to row 477 of 630 (75.9 percent), as the builder says. Sure.
2. The box at 310 and on a desk. Nothing wrong. At 310 the last dots stand 7.5 px over the box, and the box (283 px) fits in the 286 it has. At 506 the box lies on bare paper, 32 px under the dots. Sure.
3. The box at 280. The last dots stand 3.5 px over the box. They are 2.6 px high and hang 2.3 px under their own strokes, so they sit about halfway between their rule and the box and begin to look as if they rest on it. Nothing touches. Likely, and small. I would find about 10 px (of the 420) and bring the lowest mark to 73 or 74 percent, which doubles the gap. By my sums `--try '{"card-x-1.png":{"max":41,"ttl":34,"pad":23,"hdTop":6,"hdFoot":6}}'` gives 10: four from the passage and two from each of the others. Or leave it.
4. The same gap, a caution. X's own guide names 2 to 1 for the large card. If any phone still crops to that and not to 1.91 to 1, 3.5 px go from the head and from the foot at 280, and the box meets the dots. I believe phones show 1.91 to 1, as these pictures assume. Not sure. It is my reason for wanting the room in 3.
5. The words in the box. The title is 47 characters and wants about 283 px. A 280 px card gives it 256 and a 295 px card 271, so on cards under about 310 px X will cut it at or just after "Auden's course" and the year goes. Likely. Leave it: what is left still says whose course it is, and a shorter twitter:title would say less on every wider phone.
6. The order of importance. Right for X. The lines and their marks are what the site does, and X's box prints the title in type as large as the card's own title is on a phone. There the line under the title is 7 to 8 px and the author's line about 5 px: they cannot be read on a phone and can on a desk. Nothing to do. A matter of taste.
7. The head. The line under the title is now half again as long as the title (725 px against 472) and 58 percent of its size, so the two read almost as equals. It suits a running head. A matter of taste. Leave it.
8. The empty foot with no box on it. Not a fault. It reads as a page with a deep foot (152 px, against 42 at the head). Keep it bare: anything set there would be half covered on a desk and wholly covered on a phone. Sure.

## B. The general card

Nothing wrong, and nothing worse for the changes. The line under the title is still quiet: 30 px italic in the soft ink under a 93 px title. It reads at 506, and so does the author's line. At 300 both are texture, which is all that width allows. The shading is 5 levels in 255 at the very edge and shows as no frame at any size here. Sure.

## C. The description

Nothing wrong. Every word is plain. No stock word, no feeling told, no dash, no "we", nothing a machine would say. It is true to the site: the class page says the course met late in the afternoon, that three students wrote about it, and that one wrote down what he remembered within the hour. The beat, the rhymes and the figures are the first three of the site's six ways. No serial comma, as on the rest of the site. Sure.

1. "as his students wrote them down". The class page says the afternoons are told in the site's own words, from what the students wrote. A careful reader may expect the students' own pages. A matter of taste. I would leave it. If it troubles the owner: "the afternoons his students wrote down".
2. Length: 240 characters. Search results and some apps cut at about 155 to 160, inside "reading list", so the clause about the beat is what is lost there. The first 119 characters stand alone, so the cut is clean. A matter of taste. Leave it.

## D. The alt text

True to both pictures. I measured the strokes: four long ones in each line, the red dot under a short one, the ring under a long one, as the text says. It is in the right order for the ear (the words in the picture, the lines, the marks), the lines are heard as two plain sentences, and it is 411 of X's 420 characters. "Ariel's song" is not in the picture, but it is true and it helps. Nothing to cut. Sure.

1. To add, if room is found: where the two red marks stand. The last sentence gives the key, but it never says that there is one red dot and one red ring, each under the first syllable of its line. That is the point of the picture, and the front page's own note says it. Likely a loss to a listener. A plain sentence that says so costs about 37 characters and 9 are free. Dropping "its measure:" and "speaking" frees 22 more, and then only a clipped sentence fits (419). So leave X's text as it is. If the owner wants the place said, give it to og:image:alt, which has no such limit. What to do is a matter of taste.
2. For a later passage only: the slash the builder puts between lines that do not end in a stop is spoken as "slash" by some screen readers. A comma would be heard better. Likely.

## E. The tags

Nothing wrong or missing. Both addresses are whole, both files are in site/, both are 1200 by 630, and the type, width and height tags are true. X takes its title and description from the og tags, so no twitter:title or twitter:description is wanted. The two alt tags are the same and true to both pictures. Sure.

1. Know this: Slack, I believe, reads twitter:image before og:image, so Slack will show X's card with no box on it. Other sites may do the same. The card stands up there (A8). Nothing to do. Likely, from memory.
2. `<title>` is the bare name, and og:title adds "Auden's course of 1941". Fine if meant: in a search result the description says whose course it is in its first three words.

## Verdict

It can go up as it is. Nothing must be mended first. If there are ten minutes, spend them on A3: a little more room over X's box on a 280 px phone.

## After the mending

Looked again at the new card-x-1.png (the same bytes as site/card-x-1.png; the general card is unchanged), the three phone pictures, lines 16 and 19 of site/index.html, and the alt and joining code in build/card.mjs. Measured as before. Nothing was refused.

1. X's card. Right. The last ink now ends at row 462 of 630 (73.3 percent). At 280 the dots stand 7.5 px over the box (it was 3.5) and 2 px under their own strokes, so they plainly belong to their rule. At 310 the gap is 11.5 px. Cut to two to one at 280 it is still 4 px, and the head keeps 5.5 px: tight, but nothing touches and nothing is cut. Enough. Sure.
2. The narrower sides do no harm. At 280 X's box now overhangs the hairline by about 5 px on each side, which looks meant. Sure.
3. The smaller title does no harm that matters. It is about 12 px on the narrowest phone, a little under the type in X's box, and the line under it is now 62 percent of its size. It still reads first in the head. A matter of taste. Leave it.
4. Bare, with no box on it (A8, E1), the foot is now 168 px against 38 at the head. Still a deep foot and not a fault.
5. The last sentence of og:image:alt. Right. It is true to both pictures and to the data (the first syllable of the first line has a stress and no beat, and the first of the second a beat and no stress), and the builder now checks that. It is plain, and a listener would follow it. Sure. If anything, two sentences are heard a little better than one with four commas: stop after "off the beat" and begin again with "The second begins with a red ring". A matter of taste. It is 453 characters, which no site but X would mind, and X's text is still 411.
6. The comma. Right for the ear. Two small things for that later day. A line that ends in a stop and then a closing quotation mark or bracket, or in a dash, still gets a comma after it. And the comma stands inside the quotation though the poet did not write it, which I take to be the lesser fault. Likely.

Verdict as before: it can go up as it is.
