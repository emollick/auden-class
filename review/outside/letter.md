## 3. The letter itself

The letter can be read, and it says what the reviewer reports.

It is printed in Ursula M. Niebuhr, ed., *Remembering Reinhold Niebuhr: Letters of Reinhold and Ursula M. Niebuhr* (HarperSanFrancisco, 1991), in chapter twelve, "W. H. Auden," on pp. 281-82. It is headed Ann Arbor, December 19, 1941, and is addressed to Mrs. Niebuhr.

How it was read: in two Internet Archive scans of the book, `rememberingreinh0000nieb` and `rememberingreinh00rein` (both lending copies), through the Archive's public full-text search limited to one item, which returns extracts of about a hundred characters. I read the letter from end to end in such extracts on 5 October 2026. I did not borrow the book and did not see the page images. The two scans agree on every reading below.

What it says, in my words:

- The number: six cantos of the *Divine Comedy* (the exact words are "six cantos of the Divine Comedy by heart").
- The task: for their examination at the end of the semester the class has only to learn the six by heart and write them out.
- The class: it is very shocked at the moment and thinks it cannot be done. So the announcement was recent.
- The promise: Auden has promised the class that if he cannot learn the whole assignment himself he will reduce it. He does not say by how much. He says nothing of calling the examination off, of a telephone call, or of a committee.
- The journey: he is to try it "in the train between Chicago and Los Angeles."
- The day: he writes at eight in the morning; the vacation begins that day and he leaves at noon for California.
- Also about the course: some of his students are coming along nicely; there are several theories about him at Michigan, and he tells a joke about a professor who has heard he is a Platonist; he asks whether the Niebuhrs too find that American students are not used to severe criticism of their work and take it for personal dislike. He ends because he must pack and give away what is in the icebox.

The page. The search gives no page numbers, so the page rests on two things seen in the extracts. The running foot "W. H. Auden 281" falls in the middle of an earlier sentence of this letter, about the war, so everything after it is on p. 282. And the book's own index puts Waley's *The Way and the Power*, which Auden mentions after the passage on the class, on p. 282. The passage on the cantos lies between the two, so it is on p. 282, as Kirsch's reference says.

Two cautions. The printed text has marks of omission in three places, so the book does not print the whole letter. And it gives Waley's title as *The Way and the Power*, where Mendelson, quoting the manuscript, has *The Way and Its Power* (finding 4): the printed text is a transcript with at least one slip.

Sources: *Remembering Reinhold Niebuhr*, pp. 281-82, https://archive.org/details/rememberingreinh0000nieb and https://archive.org/details/rememberingreinh00rein (search inside).

## 5. The journey (draft notes, being checked)

- The letter to Ursula Niebuhr: vacation begins that day (Friday 19 December 1941); he leaves at noon for California; train between Chicago and Los Angeles.
- Charles Miller, *Auden: An American Friendship* (1983), pp. 63-64: weeks before Christmas Auden had proposed that he and Miller drive the green Pontiac to California for the vacation; Miller did not want to go west. Read in Internet Archive search extracts (`audenamericanfri00mill`); pages from Google Books search inside (volume id -UxaAAAAMAAJ).

- Pearce, p. 153: Auden said he would be driving to California over the vacation (kept scan).
