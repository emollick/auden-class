### week9.reading.2.auden.paras.3
- where: You are not asked to write anything about it.
- problem: "It" has no noun of its own. The sentence before has only the verb "compare", inside a statement about the site ("This site, not Auden, suggests that you compare"). The nearest noun is the novel, which closes that sentence, and the week sets a paper on the novel, so the last sentence can be read as a false instruction.
- fix: Begin the fourth sentence with the instruction, "Compare what Auden says of opera with this week's novel," and keep the rest of it from "in which" on. Replace the last sentence with: "The comparison is this site's and not Auden's, and you are not asked to write anything about it."
- weight: should

### week9.questions.1.auden.paras.4
- where: This site, not Auden, tries the formula on
- problem: The sentence says twice that the step is not Auden's: once in "not Auden", and again in its closing clause, which says that the essay does not mention the novel. One mark is enough, and the closing clause is the one that gives the student a fact.
- fix: Cut ", not Auden," so that the sentence begins "This site tries the formula on" and runs on as it stands.
- weight: should

### week10.questions.1.auden.paras.1
- where: and the record does not say which devil he meant
- problem: The hint under the question has already said both things in nearly the same words: that the question was the whole of a final examination at Swarthmore, and that the record does not say which devil was meant. The hint stays on the page above the handed-in paper, so the student reads the two statements again before reaching anything new.
- fix: Keep the opening as far as "Swarthmore College," and go on: "in a course on Elizabethan literature, and no answer of his has been found. The subject of that course points to the devil in Marlowe's play *Doctor Faustus*." The last sentence of the entry stands.
- weight: should

### week10.questions.1.auden.paras.3
