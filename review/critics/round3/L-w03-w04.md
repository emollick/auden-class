# L-w03-w04 (weeks 3 and 4), last read: both lenses

I read every marked sentence of wave1/w03.md and wave1/w04.md in its place, and checked each against the page as the program draws it (site/js/views/question.js, test.js, words.js, making.js, term.js). Six findings, all "should". Every other marked sentence is sound. In the marked sentences I found no broken grammar, no em-dash, hedge, exclamation mark or British spelling, and no verb of saying that does one job twice.

Two facts about the page bear on the findings. The paragraphs under a question are shown only after a paper on that question has been handed in, so a student who answered a different question that week has never seen them. Each such block is also shown by itself, on its own page.

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.5
- where: Donald Pearce, a student in the class, wrote down
- problem: The revision moved this paragraph to the end of the block. It now follows the paragraph on the New York lecture of 1947 and the students' notes from which that lecture was reconstructed. "The class" has no other owner in the block, so Pearce reads as one of the New York students, and the lecture on Augustine and Kafka reads as a New York lecture.
- fix: For "a student in the class" put "a student in Auden's class at Michigan in 1941". The rest of the sentence stays as it is.
- weight: should

### week3.questions.2.auden.paras.1

- fix: Auden writes about Augustine and time in [the essay's title, kept], an essay on masters and servants collected in *The Dyer’s Hand* (1962).
- weight: should

### week4.questions.1.gloss.1

### week4.questions.2.auden.paras.2
- where: the essay of 1948 reported in week 2

### week4.questions.3.auden.paras.3
- where: by bearing it he is reconciled to the law
- problem: The hero of Greek tragedy is "he" in this sentence, and the hero in general is "himself" and "he" in the next (rule 12). (One older sentence has the same "he" and is not marked, so I only point to it: in week4.questions.2.auden.paras.2, the sentence that begins "He envies". If it is to match, it can read: "He envies the heroes of Greek tragedy, who are made what they are by their situation.")
