## Patterns

1. A phrase that claims more than Pearce claims, taken over from the editors' ruling on how to introduce him: 2. See week7.reading.3.auden.paras.1 and week8.questions.2.auden.paras.2. Week 9 does not name Pearce. The live files have "after lectures" in both places.
2. A year of printing given as the year of writing: 1. See week7.questions.2.auden.paras.1. I then looked again at every other marked sentence in the three weeks that carries a year, and each year belongs to the event the sentence gives it to. Two examples: week8.questions.2.auden.paras.1 (1952 as the year of publication, fa.txt 24970–24974) and week9.questions.1.auden.paras.1 (1948 as the year of publication; the writing was about two years earlier, mendelson2017.txt 28672–28673).
3. A detail added about a required book that the site's copy of the book does not have: 1. See week8.intro.1. The other marked sentences that tell what happens in *Peer Gynt*, in the novel or in the opera were each read against the site's copy and agree with it. Two examples: week7.test.items.2.where and week8.questions.2.auden.paras.2.

Four findings in all: one "must" and three "should". The live files already answer each of them, so the editor has only to confirm that no older wording comes back.

Nothing else recurs. No marked quotation departs from its source, no marked paraphrase follows a source for seven words in a row, no marked page reference is wrong, and every step that is the site's own is marked as the site's.
