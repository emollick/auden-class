Third-round check of the marked sentences in `wave1/w04.md`, `w05.md` and `w06.md`. Every marked sentence that reports Auden, Pearce or another writer, every marked source line, and every plain fact in a marked sentence was compared with its source. Evidence is given as a file and line numbers (for the Pearce captures: file, line and character offsets). No source is quoted here, and no quotation of the site's is printed.

Short names. "gap2" is `research/A_pearce_gap2.txt` and "tr1" is `research/A_tr_out1.txt`, the captures that hold the pages of Pearce's essay missing from `research/auden/pearce.txt`. ## Findings, in the order of the text

### week5.reading.4.paras.1
- where: The English of the arias follows the German words.
- problem: True of most of the numbers and not of all, and round two left the fact for the editor to confirm (F--llmspeak.md, the same label). In the site copy the Queen's second aria (magic-flute act-2, block 45) goes word for word with the German (research/texts/mozart_zauberfloete_de_zeno.txt line 2212) and not with the Italian printed opposite it (.cache/texts/ia_magicflutediezau00moza_djvu.txt lines 2020–2022). But the Queen's first aria (act-1, block 119) and Pamina's aria (act-2, block 104) follow the Italian (scan lines 508–523 and 2149–2161) where it departs from the German (zeno lines 473 and 2528). The project's own build note says the opposite, that the English follows the Italian line for line (build/texts/notes/magic-flute.md, the paragraph headed "The version"). Neither statement holds without exception.
- fix: The English of most of the arias follows the German words.
- weight: should

### week5.reading.4.paras.2
- where: Papageno says that he wants no wisdom, is talked
- problem: The first two of the four things said here of Papageno are in the German spoken dialogue only (zeno lines 1837 and 1851–1877, the third scene of Act 2). The site copy leaves that scene out: act-2 passes from the priests' prayer (block 9) to the Moor's air (block 14), and nowhere in it does Papageno speak of wisdom or agree to a trial. A student who reads the libretto and then hears the scene spoken in German cannot find either statement. That he fails and is content is on the page (act-2, blocks 172–188 and 309).
- fix: In a spoken scene that the page leaves out, Papageno says that he wants no wisdom and is then talked into the first trial. He fails it, and in Act 2, Scene 8 he is content to go no further.
- weight: should

### week5.making.1.table.key.intro.1

- problem: The year is the book's and not the essay's. The essay was first printed in 1954, in *Encounter* and in *Thought* (vol. 29, no. 2, pp. 237–270; PhilArchive's list of Auden's articles, and a bookseller's record of the *Encounter* printing). - fix: In the essay from *The Dyer’s Hand* reported under the opera, the figure he sets beside Caliban is Papageno.
- weight: should

### week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2
- where: the ground he gives is that Pascal’s reason was
- problem: Weaker than the source. The book says what the reason told Pascal, namely that his heart was a corrupt one, and it draws a consequence, that the love of other people was not meant for him. This stands at the head of p. 48 (seen in the Internet Archive's search inside the scan prolificdevourer00aude; research/E_pd_pearce.md line 43 records the turn of the page). The site's clause names no state and leaves out the consequence, so the reader is not told the ground. V3's fix had the first half. The editor took the looser wording from V3's statement of the problem.
- fix: He concludes, [quotation kept, with its closing comma] and the ground he gives is that Pascal learned from his reason how corrupt his heart was, and so learned that human love was not for him.
- weight: should

Drift in the last rewording: the new sentence says more than the source, or less. 3 findings: week6.questions.1.auden.paras.1 (broader), week6.questions.1.auden.paras.3 (narrower) and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2 (weaker). 3. A year or a page that does not fit the sentence it serves. 2 findings: week5.making.1.table.key.intro.1, where the year of the book is given to an essay first printed earlier, and week6.questions.4.auden.sources.1, where the source line lacks the page of a sentence added to the paragraph above it.

Nothing else recurs. No quotation differs from its source and no marked source line gives a wrong page or year. Wherever the source is on disk I tested the marked sentences by script for runs of a source's words outside quotation marks, and found none of seven words or more. Week 4 has no findings.
