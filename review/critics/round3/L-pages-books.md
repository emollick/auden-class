# L-pages-books (the reading-list page, the Sources page, the book notes), last read: both lenses

Four findings, all "should". Every other marked sentence in the two parts is sound.

Cross-check of the two parts. I set every statement that the two parts share side by side: which nine texts were read by machine, which six were then read through and which two were corrected only in places, the twelve cantos, the eight readings taken from another printing, the edition and translator of each book, and what each text leaves out. They do not disagree on any fact or count. In two places one thing goes under two names, and those are the findings on text:baudelaire.note and text:dante-temple.note.

### page:about.sections.1.table.rows.8.2
- where: three different reasons, because Carpenter repeats Chapman’s
- problem: The clause explains why the number is three and not four, but the sentence never says that there are four accounts, so the reader has to count the citations before "because" makes sense. It reads as a patch on an earlier count.
- fix: Replace that sentence with: "Pearce, Miller and Chapman each give a different reason, and Carpenter repeats Chapman’s." Then begin the next sentence "In Miller’s account," so that the possessive has its noun.
- weight: should

### text:baudelaire.note
- where: The numbers are those of the leaves
- problem: The note stands on the contents page, where the only numbers in sight are the page counts beside the two journals, so "The numbers" has nothing to point at until a journal is opened. The noun "leaves" is followed at once by the verb "leaves", and work:baudelaire.copy calls the things that are left out "pages".
- fix: Replace both sentences with: "Inside each journal the entries carry Roman numerals, which are the page numbers of Baudelaire’s manuscript. Shipley leaves some of those pages out, so the numbering has gaps." If the editor wants the bibliographer’s word for a manuscript sheet kept, the table should use it too, and the verb beside it should be a different word.
- weight: should

### text:dante-temple.note
- where: was settled from the printed page
- problem: page:about.sections.7.paras.3 has this sentence with "the scan" in that place, and it says the twelve cantos were read through "against the scans" where this note says "against the pages". The eight other notes on texts read by machine also say "scan", as does the first sentence of this note. A reader who meets "the printed page" here may take it for a paper copy, which no other entry mentions.
- fix: In the sentence that begins "Where the copies disagreed", for "the printed page" read "the scan". In the sentence that begins "Twelve cantos", for "against the pages" read "against the scans". (Outside my part, for the editor to weigh: the app’s message under an unchecked canto names the same check a third way, as a check against the printed book.)
- weight: should

### text:horace.note
- where: and three stanzas of III.6
- problem: The second thing Conington left out comes after a relative clause about the first, so on a first reading the stanzas seem to belong to what the site prints in Latin. The reader has to go back to the verb to place them.
- fix: Replace the first sentence with two: "Conington left out four odes and three stanzas of III.6. The four (I.25, II.5, III.20 and IV.10) and the missing stanzas are printed only in the [Latin text](#/read/horace-la)."
- weight: should
