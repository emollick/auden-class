### week 10, line 13, "l’espérance"
- problem: The entry is the one bare word "hope", and nothing shows that it is a noun. Coming after the entry for the first word of the line, "never", it reads in English as an order not to hope, so a student will take the line for a second command like the one in line 6. The French cannot be an order: it is the thing, hope, with the word for "the" (the l’) before it. It is also the church's name for the virtue of hope, which bears on the Bible word of line 14.
- fix: `['l’espérance', 'hope; in religion, the virtue of hope. A noun, with l’ (the) before it: hope as a thing, and not a command to hope']`
- weight: must

### week 10, line 12, "selon"
- problem: "The sentence is left unfinished" gives one reading as a fact. French can end a sentence on this word (c’est selon, "that depends"), and the first form of the poem, of May 1872, puts a full stop after it. The Dial prints it here with a full stop close against the word and then three dots, which is the magazine's mark after a sentence that has ended; where a sentence breaks off it prints a space and three dots ("Night in Hell" has both). With "according to" alone the student cannot choose the other reading.
- fix: `['selon', 'according to, depending on. The word can also end a sentence by itself (c’est selon: that depends), meaning as may be, as things fall out. Nothing follows it here but the dots']`
- weight: should
