- where: first and second sentences (they begin "In *The Enchafèd Flood*" and "All of them are")

(1) Auden says what kind of men the mates are not; the site turns this into a statement that no evil is in them, which is stronger, and it sits oddly beside the fault the next sentence gives them. (2) Auden limits their courage with an adverb (it is the body's courage, 5097–5098); the site leaves the adverb out, so the courage and the cowardice meet in one sentence without the distinction that reconciles them. All of them are physically brave and loyal and bear no malice, but all suffer from a sloth of the spirit, which he calls a form of cowardice, and each has refused to grow up.
