## Week 13

### week13.intro.1
- where: Ahab hails each with the same question
- problem: True of four of the five. At the Rose-Bud it is Stubb who rows over, hails her and puts the question, while Ahab waits at the quarter-deck rail for the report (moby-dick c091, blocks 11 to 18; compare c100, c115, c128, c131).
- fix: The Pequod meets five more ships, and each is asked the same question.
- weight: could

### week13.questions.3.auden.paras.4
- where: As the ship goes down, Starbuck asks God
- problem: The three mates say these things while the whale is bearing down on the ship, before he strikes her (moby-dick c135, blocks 51 to 53). She sinks later in the chapter.
- fix: Change the opening clause of that sentence to "As the whale bears down on the ship," and leave the rest as it is.
- weight: could

### week13.questions.4.auden.paras.4
- where: In 1941 Auden had copied a sentence
- problem: 1941 is the year the book was published (New York in March, London in May). The whole book was written in 1940 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Double_Man_(book)), and Mendelson's *Later Auden* dates the compiling of the notes to the summer of 1940 (archive.org full-text search of laterauden0000mend). The rest of the paragraph checks: the Faber notes print Adams's sentence with only the book's title after it, and the list headed Modern Sources is in the Random House edition alone (archive.org full-text search of newyearletter0000aude and bwb_S0-DUH-879).
- fix: The notes to *New Year Letter*, published in 1941, include a sentence copied from this chapter.
- weight: could

### week13.making.1.table.key.rows.5.news
- where: Her captain has never heard of such a whale
- problem: False. The man who answers is the chief mate, a Guernsey-man, and Stubb puts the question to him. The captain speaks no English and is never asked about the whale (moby-dick c091, blocks 11 to 16 and 29 to 37).
- fix: Change the second sentence to "Her mate has never heard of such a whale." The first and third sentences stand.
- weight: must

## Week 14

### week14.test.items.16.speaker
- where: Gardena
- problem: The site's copy of the Muirs' translation spells the landlady's name Gardana (castle c06, four times; the other spelling occurs nowhere in the text). The checker compares whole words exactly once accents are stripped (answerMatches in site/js/lib/text.js), so a student who types the name as the reader prints it is marked wrong.
- fix: Add Gardana to the accepted answers. Keep Gardena for students who know a later translation.
- weight: must

### week14.questions.1.gloss.1
- where: K. has had advice from Barnabas, from Frieda
- problem: Barnabas gives no advice in Chapters 1 to 6. He brings Klamm's letter, offers to carry messages, and leads K. through the snow to his own family's house while K. supposes they are going to the Castle (castle c02, blocks 12 to 23). The other three do advise him.
- fix: Change the second sentence to "By the end of Chapter 6 K. has had a guide in Barnabas, and advice from three people: Frieda, the landlady and the Superintendent." The first and last sentences stand.
- weight: could

### week14.questions.1.auden.paras.4
- where: Auden left no answer to the question about Virgil
- problem: A negative that cannot be confirmed. A search of the scan of *Prose, Volume II* and of the research notes found no answer of his, which supports only the weaker statement.
- fix: No answer of Auden's to the question about Virgil has been found.
- weight: should

### week14.making.1.translate.glossary.2.5.2
- where: Sonnenuhr: a sundial. Sonne is sun, Uhr is clock
- problem: The word in the line is plural, and this is the one plural noun in the list whose entry does not say so (compare the entries for die Winde, Früchten, Tage, Briefe and die Blätter). The article die is also the feminine singular, so a student working from the list alone can write one sundial.
- fix: Sonnenuhr: a sundial. Sonne is sun, Uhr is clock. The sundials
- weight: could

### week14.making.1.translate.glossary.10.1.2
- where: It governs every verb from here to the end
- problem: Not the last one. The final word of the poem, treiben, is the present-tense verb of its own clause (wenn die Blätter treiben) and does not depend on wird. The four verbs that do are wachen, lesen, schreiben and wandern (rilke-de, first part, block 2).
- fix: will. It governs wachen, lesen and schreiben in this line, and wandern in the last
- weight: could

## Patterns

1. An act or a speech in a set book given to the wrong person (who asks, who answers, who advises). Count: 3. See week13.making.1.table.key.rows.5.news and week13.intro.1; also week14.questions.1.gloss.1.
2. A note in the German word-list that says too little or too much about the grammar. Count: 2. See week14.making.1.translate.glossary.2.5.2 and week14.making.1.translate.glossary.10.1.2.
3. The time of an event put a step too late. Count: 2. See week13.questions.3.auden.paras.4 and week13.questions.4.auden.paras.4.
4. An answer key out of step with the spelling in the site's own copy. Count: 1. See week14.test.items.16.speaker. Every other proper name in the two weeks' keys was read against the site's text and agrees.
5. A statement about the record put more strongly than the evidence allows. Count: 1. See week14.questions.1.auden.paras.4.

Apart from the year in week13.questions.4.auden.paras.4, no date, title, publisher, page reference or quotation that falls to this lens was found wrong.
