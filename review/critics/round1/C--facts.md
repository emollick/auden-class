# C, weeks 1 to 3: facts

Findings are in the order of the text. Evidence is a URL, or a text id and part from the site's own copies.

## Week 1

### week1.reading.2.paras.1
- where: Read the libretto, which is thirteen pages.
- problem: The count is right, but the copy printed (text id orpheus, parts act-1 to act-4) is divided into four acts; its note says it was published for the Metropolitan Opera House. Both versions named in the next paragraph are in three acts (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orfeo_ed_Euridice), so a student who follows a recording of either with this libretto open will find the act numbers do not agree.
- fix: flag only. If the editor wants a sentence, add after the page count: "It is printed in four acts. Recordings of both versions are in three, and their second act is the second and third here."
- weight: could

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1
- where: He names it once, as a date.
- problem: He names it at least twice. A second piece of 1948, "Opera Addict" (Vogue, July 1948), gives the same two operas with their years in brackets and says the operas between them fill 452 pages of Loewenberg's Annals of Opera. It is in the Princeton Prose, volume II, where the essay begins on p. 400. Evidence: search inside https://archive.org/details/prosetravelbooks0000aude for the opera's name next to Gluck's, and for "Annals of Opera".
- fix: "He names it twice, both times in 1948 and both times as a date." Add the second source to the list under the paragraph once the editor has the page: "Opera Addict," *Vogue*, July 1948, in *Prose*, vol. II, pp. 400–402.
- weight: must

### week1.test.items.14.where
- where: Purgatorio XXXI
- problem: The plunge is in that canto, but the river is not named there in the copy printed (dante-longfellow, pur-31, lines 91 to 102, has only the stream and the water). A student sent to XXXI to check the answer will not find the word. The name is given at pur-28, line 130, and again in pur-33.
- fix: "Purgatorio XXXI; the river is named in XXVIII"
- weight: could

### week1.questions.1.auden.paras.1
- where: Auden wrote no essay on the *Comedy*.
- problem: True if read strictly, and no such essay turned up in the Princeton Prose volumes that can be searched. But the anthology *The Poets' Dante* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2001) prints a piece by Auden on Dante's love for Beatrice, "The Vision of Eros", pp. 136 to 144 (https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/peter-s-hawkins/the-poets-dante/; https://www.librarything.com/work/473517). It appears to be the section of that name in his 1964 introduction to *The Protestant Mystics*; I did not see the anthology's own note of its source. A reader who knows that book may take the sentence for a mistake.
- fix: "Auden wrote no essay on the *Comedy* itself."
- weight: could

### week1.questions.1.auden.paras.1
- where: an introduction to Kierkegaard, written in 1952
- problem: 1952 is the year of publication, not a documented year of writing. The notes to the Princeton Prose, volume III, give the book as number 23 in the Living Thoughts Library, published by David McKay on October 17, 1952 (search inside https://archive.org/details/prosetravelbooks0003aude for "Living Thoughts of Kierkegaard"). I found no statement of when Auden wrote it.
- fix: Change one word, so that the sentence ends "an introduction to Kierkegaard, published in 1952."
- weight: should

### week1.making.1.table.key.after.1
- where: the circles above the city of Dis punish incontinence
- problem: Too wide. In Canto XI Dante asks about four groups outside the city, those in the marsh, those the wind drives, those the rain beats, and those who clash, and Virgil answers that their sin is incontinence (dante-longfellow, inf-11, lines 70 to 90). That is the second to the fifth circles. The first circle is also above the city and punishes no incontinence, and Virgil's account leaves the sixth circle, inside the walls, unplaced.
- fix: "Virgil's account in Canto XI: the second to the fifth circles, outside the city of Dis, are for incontinence; the seventh is for violence; the eighth and ninth are for fraud. He does not place the first circle or the sixth."
- weight: could

## Week 2

### week2.intro.2
- where: The translation is one that Auden is known to
- problem: As it stands the sentence says Auden set this translation. What is on record is the kind of exercise (a poem from German, French or Latin, in a language the student had not studied) and, for one student, a poem of Rilke's; see week2.making.1.source.1 below. Nothing ties the exercise to this ode, and the site's own source note says the ode is its choice.

- weight: could

### week2.test.items.2.where

- problem: The same small gap as in week 1. The life of Francis is told in XI, but the speaker gives his name only in the canto before (dante-longfellow, par-10, line 99). Likewise week2.test.items.6.where: the examination on faith is in XXIV, where the examiner is called only the man who was given the keys; his name comes in par-25, line 12.

- weight: could

### week2.making.1.brief.2
- where: the form it has in a dictionary
- problem: Not true of nine entries. The words quem, mihi, tibi, Babylonios, melius, quidquid, plures, Quae and Tyrrhenum stand in forms a dictionary does not list, and the word-list gives no dictionary form for any of them (site/data/course.json, week 2, first exercise). The same claim is made at week2.making.1.translate.wordsIntro.1. Every meaning in the word-list is right; this is the only fault found in it.
- fix: Add the forms to the nine entries: quem, "qui: which, what"; mihi, "ego: I. To me, for me"; tibi, "tu: you. To you, for you"; Babylonios, "Babylonius: Babylonian"; melius, "bonus: good. Better"; quidquid, "quisquis: whoever, whatever. Whatever"; plures, "plus: more. More, many"; Quae, "qui: who, which. Which"; Tyrrhenum, "Tyrrhenus: Tyrrhenian, Etruscan". Keep the rest of each entry as it is.
- weight: should

### week2.making.1.translate.cite
- where: revised by R. H. Chase (1895)
- problem: 1895 is the date of the printing the site's Latin was taken from (Boston, Allyn and Bacon, twentieth edition), not of the revision. The same title page carries a copyright entry of 1856, and the catalogue record gives Chase's dates as 1832 to 1885 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46938). A reader will take 1895 for the year Chase revised the text.
- fix: "The Latin is from A. J. Macleane's edition as revised by R. H. Chase, in the Boston printing of 1895."
- weight: could

### week2.making.1.source.1
- where: Which poems the class was given is not recorded.
- problem: One is recorded. Robert L. Chapman, who was in the class and is Carpenter's source for this passage, writes that he took a poem of Rilke's, and names it by its first line, "Lösch mir die Augen aus" (*Michigan Quarterly Review* 17:4, Fall 1978, p. 509; Google Books volume uDDiAAAAMAAJ in snippet view, saved by the earlier research as A_gb_mqr3.txt). His wording suggests the students found their own poems; nothing says poems were handed out. I could not read the page whole, because the university's archive refused the request (403). Chapman also settles who chose the language: in his account the students pick one they have not studied, as week2.making.1.brief.1 says, where Carpenter has Auden pick it for them. The brief is right and its cited source is the weaker one.
- fix: "The assignment is described by Robert L. Chapman, who was in the class, in “Auden in Ann Arbor,” *Michigan Quarterly Review* 17 (1978), p. 509, and after him in Carpenter's biography, pp. 318–19. Chapman says he took a poem of Rilke's. No other student's poem is recorded. This ode is the site's choice." The editor of week 14 may want the Rilke fact as well.
- weight: must

## Week 3

### week3.intro.2

- problem: It begins a week earlier. Week 14 sets Chapters 1 to 6 and has the novel in its heading; week 15 sets Chapters 7 to 18 (site/data/course.json, units 14 and 15).
- fix: "On this site *The Castle* comes in weeks 14 and 15, where the list puts it."
- weight: must

### week3.words.after.1
- where: He cut out the key words
- problem: A step is missing, and it changes the exercise. In Eberhart's account as Carpenter prints it, Auden took out the key words, put odd or nonsense words where they had been, and asked the boys to get as near the meaning as they could (search inside https://archive.org/details/whaudenbiography00carp_1 for "Elegy" with Eberhart's name). The boys were mending a spoiled text, not filling gaps. Carpenter also has Eberhart noting this in the present tense, as a thing Auden does, so "remembered" is doubtful. The rest of the paragraph agrees with Carpenter: four weeks, the school, Eberhart on the staff, boys too young to know the poem.
- fix: In the second sentence change "remembered" to "noted". Replace the last sentence with: "He cut out the key words and put odd or nonsense words where they had been. The boys, too young to know the poem, had to find the right ones by feel."
- weight: could

### week3.making.1.source.1
- where: of the Swarthmore College Libraries exhibit
- problem: Two things. First, the exhibit page is headed "Auden as a professor (1942-1945)" (https://archive.swarthmore.edu/auden/professor.html); the site gives the name without the article and with a capital P. Second, neither source named supports "the day before" in week3.making.1.brief.1. Spears has the composition class write the events of the day backwards (Swarthmore College Bulletin, March 1962, p. 4), and the exhibit page repeats him. The previous day is Carpenter's detail (search inside https://archive.org/details/whaudenbiography00carp_1 for "events backwards"), and the exercise depends on it.
- fix: "The assignment is reported by Monroe K. Spears, [quotation kept], *Swarthmore College Bulletin*, March 1962, p. 4, and on the page headed Auden as a professor in the Swarthmore College Libraries exhibit. Both say the events of the day. Humphrey Carpenter's biography makes it the previous day's events." The editor should add Carpenter's page, which I could not read off the scan.
- weight: should

## Patterns

1. An absolute that one counter-example breaks or strains: "once", "no essay", "not recorded", every circle above the city. Count 4. See week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1 and week2.making.1.source.1; also week1.questions.1.auden.paras.1 and week1.making.1.table.key.after.1.
2. A source is summarized with one detail dropped or moved, so the summary says something the source does not. Count 3. See week3.words.after.1 (the nonsense words left out) and week3.making.1.source.1 (which day); also week2.intro.2.
3. A statement about the site's own contents that the data does not bear out. Count 3. See week3.intro.2 (the week of *The Castle*) and week2.making.1.brief.2 (dictionary forms); also week1.reading.2.paras.1.
4. The place given for a test answer holds the event but not the name the answer needs. Count 3. See week1.test.items.14.where and week2.test.items.2.where; also week2.test.items.6.where.
5. A year attached to the wrong event: publication given as writing, a late printing given as the date of a revision. Count 2. See week1.questions.1.auden.paras.1 and week2.making.1.translate.cite.
