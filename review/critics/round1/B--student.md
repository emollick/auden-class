- fix: Add three or four words of identification after the name, for example "Alan Jacobs, a literary scholar, published a photograph of it on his site in 2012, with the permission of Auden's estate." Fact needed: the description of Jacobs the editor is willing to print.

- fix: "The three tables below list the books in the order of the sheet. For each one you will find the edition the sheet names, where it names one, the text you will read on this site, and the week in which the book is set."
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1

- fix: "Twenty-three entries. One is a choice between *Agamemnon* and *Antigone*: read one of them. - weight: must

### page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.1
- where: Nine operas, set as reading.
- problem: On this page the operas are reading only. The weeks ask for more: read the libretto, then hear the whole opera with the words open, which takes between an hour and a half and about four hours. A student who plans from the reading list will not have allowed for it, and is not told that a recording is needed.
- fix: "Nine operas. The sheet sets them as reading, and Donald Pearce, who took the course, recalls that Auden brought a phonograph and records to class. On this site you read each libretto and then listen to the opera right through, so you will need a recording. The listening takes from an hour and a half to four hours an opera." Fact needed: the longest running time among the nine.
- weight: should

### text:poems.note

### text:rilke.note
- where: corrected against the page images wherever a word looked
- problem: The sources page says each machine-read book was read against the pictures of its pages. This note says the Rilke was corrected only where a word looked wrong, which is a smaller claim. The student cannot tell how far any of the nine can be trusted. The two places also use different names for the scans.
- fix: flag only for the fact. Say one thing in both places and use one name, "page images". If the whole text was checked: "This text was read by machine from a scan of the edition of 1930, and then checked against the page images. Some misread words may remain." If only doubtful words were checked, change the sentence on the sources page to say that.
- weight: should

## Patterns

1. Catalog detail the student cannot use, repeated from entry to entry: 22. That no translator is named, 14 times (seven entries in the reading list, seven head notes); that the book has no date, 7 times; the program bound into one copy. See work:fidelio.copy and text:traviata.note.

2. Titles without the marking the house rules ask for: 16. In the head notes, the alternative titles of two operas, one periodical, five books and four poems, all in plain type, and one title in straight quotation marks; on the sources page, the three parts of the *Comedy* in plain type. See text:poems.note and text:rilke-de.note. The other house rules hold in this part: no em-dashes, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical questions, no hedging words. Three small slips besides: a British idiom (page:about.sections.1.after.1), a year with no comma after it (work:orpheus.copy), and one page range shortened where the rest are written in full (page:about.sections.1.table.rows.12.2).
