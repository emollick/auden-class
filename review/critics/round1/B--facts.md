# B, pages and books: fact check

Findings are against the snapshot B-pages-and-books.md and in its order. The live source (content/pages.mjs, content/works.mjs, the index.json files) was being revised while I worked; where I saw that it already differs from the snapshot, the finding says so. Evidence is given under "problem". Archive.org items are named by identifier; "fts" means the scan was read through archive.org full-text search.

### page:syllabus.intro.1
- where: One sheet of paper survives from the course
- problem: The list is on record as two leaves. Bloomfield and Mendelson, item K7, describe a 2 fol. reading list (fts, whaudenbibliogra0000bloo); the picture shows only the first. Other papers survive too: Kreyling's notes cite Kenneth Millar's class notes, his annotated copy of the list and a paper he wrote for English 135 (fts, novelsofrossmacd0000krey), and Fuller, *W. H. - fix: A mimeographed reading list is what survives of the course. The standard Auden bibliography records it as two leaves. Alan Jacobs published a picture of the first leaf in 2012, by permission of the estate. Keep the link to his site as it stands.
- weight: must

## Patterns

Thirty findings: 9 must, 6 should, 15 could.

1. A citation that does not carry what the row lays on it: a wrong page, the wrong course, or a source that says something else (8). page:about.sections.1.table.rows.14.2; page:about.sections.8.sources.15.
2. A round word where the source gives a number, a date or a kind (5). page:about.sections.2.paras.1; page:about.sections.6.paras.1.
3. A statement true of part of its subject and put as true of the whole (5). work:dante.copy; page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.2.
4. A statement about the site that its own data, tables or build notes contradict (4). page:about.sections.6.paras.2; page:about.sections.7.paras.3.
5. A detail of a source or of a copy wrongly given, or not confirmable (4). page:about.sections.8.sources.12; work:mann.copy.
6. The surviving record of the course said to hold less, or to show more, than it does (4). page:syllabus.intro.1; page:about.sections.9.paras.2.

Two things outside the count. Nolan's page is one out both times it is cited (52 for 53), which the page's own warning about page numbers allows for. And the publishers, years, translators and Gutenberg numbers, which are most of what this part states, were right almost without exception.
