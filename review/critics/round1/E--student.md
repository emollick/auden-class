## Patterns

Counts are of findings above. A few findings fall under two heads and are counted under both.

1. A pronoun or pointing word with no clear owner (16). "He", "his", "it", "that", "this course", "the same essay", "the same notes". The paragraphs that report Auden are the worst place for it, because four or five men are in play at once: Auden, the author, a character, Pearce. See week7.reading.3.auden.paras.1 and week9.reading.2.auden.paras.2.

2. A term, a name or an allusion left unexplained (15). Opera-house words, terms from theology and philosophy, people and books named as if known. See week8.reading.2.paras.3 and week9.questions.1.auden.paras.2.

3. An instruction that cannot be carried out as written, or that says two things (12). The largest is the opera note in each week, which tells me to hear the opera and never says where a recording comes from. Others give a length twice and differently, or a one-line description that disagrees with the rules. See week7.reading.3.paras.1 and week8.making.1.brief.2.

4. A report of Auden that skips a step, stops short, or never comes back to the book I wrote on (9). See week8.questions.1.auden.paras.3 and week7.questions.2.auden.paras.4.

5. A hint that does not do a hint's work (4). One gives no help, one gives half the answer, two have to be read twice. See week7.questions.2.gloss.1 and week8.questions.2.gloss.1.

6. A sentence turned for its sound, where a plain statement was wanted (4). Usually the last sentence of a paragraph. See week9.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week7.making.1.brief.2.

7. "I", "me" and "my" in a report of Auden with no quotation marks, so that the speaker cannot be told (3). See week8.reading.2.auden.paras.2 and week9.questions.2.auden.paras.2.

8. The week's opening leaves out the opera (3). See week7.intro.1 and week9.intro.1.

9. An essay cited with no date, against the promise on the About page (1 finding, 5 places). See week7.questions.1.auden.paras.1 and week9.questions.1.auden.paras.1.

10. House rules (2 findings). Book titles without italics in the six week headings, and one em-dash in a chapter title copied from the translation. See week7.sub and week7.test.items.11.where. Two British usages were also changed to American ones inside fixes: see week8.reading.2.paras.3 and week8.reading.2.auden.paras.2. The second of those words turns up again, used the British way, in the question at week9.test.items.8.q.
