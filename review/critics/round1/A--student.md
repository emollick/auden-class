# Part A, the app's own wording: read as the student

### ui:colophon.1
- where: sets out what is documented
- problem: The link text is a plural noun with a singular verb after it, so the sentence reads as a slip until you see that the noun names a page. ui:home.how.after.2 has the same construction, and on the front page the two stand on one screen saying the same thing.
- fix: Second sentence: The [Sources](#/about) page says what is documented and what the site itself supplies. At ui:home.how.after.2, second sentence: The [Sources](#/about) page says which is which. (Or cut that entry, since the footer is on every page.)
- weight: should

### ui:nav.ledger
- where: Ledger
- problem: The name is a figure of speech. It does not say what the page holds, which is the student's progress: pages read against the calendar, test scores, papers handed in, cantos known.
- fix: Progress. If "Ledger" is kept, explain it where it is first used (see the fix at ui:home.how.list.2).
- weight: could

### ui:standing.during
- where: Week {unit} of {units}
- problem: "Week" means one of the fifteen parts of the course. A student who picked a term of 30 or 52 weeks at enrollment has just been told its length in calendar weeks. On the year-long term the first "week" lasts 24 days, and the front page counts the days left in it.
- fix: flag only. Keep "week" for the part of the course, and say so once where the length is chosen: see the wording at ui:enrol.pace.two.note and ui:enrol.pace.year.note. This entry can then stand.
- weight: must

### ui:state.known
- where: known
- problem: On a week's page this word shows that the week's share of a canto is finished, each stretch written once with the text covered. On the By heart page the same word has a stricter, stated meaning: the whole canto written out on two different days. A student who sees it in week 1 will think that rule is met.
- fix: done
- weight: should

### ui:home.lede
- where: W. H. Auden taught English 135 once
- problem: Neither the front page nor the Sources page says who Auden was. A student who knows little about him needs one word here.
- fix: Begin: The poet W. H. Auden taught English 135 once, …
- weight: should

### ui:home.lede
- where: on Thursday afternoons in the fall
- problem: The Sources page gives two accounts of when the class met: the reading list says Thursdays, and Pearce says two afternoons a week. The lede states one of them as settled, and ui:term.intro.1 builds on it.
- fix: flag only. Either drop the day (… once, in the fall of 1941, …) or keep it and add "by the reading list".
- weight: should

### ui:home.lede
- where: This is that course, laid out
- problem: The first claim a student reads is that this is Auden's course. The foot of the same page and the Sources page say the weekly work is the site's reconstruction. The lede should say it too.
- fix: Second sentence: This site reconstructs that course as a term you can take. The reading list is his, and so is the final examination as one student remembered it. The weekly work is this site's, of kinds he is known to have assigned.
- weight: should

### ui:home.what.1

- fix: … cantos of the *Divine Comedy* from memory.
- weight: should

### ui:home.what.1
- where: twenty-three set books
- problem: "Set book" is British, and the voice is meant to be American. The reading list page heads the same books as required reading, so they have two names. The verb in this sense is in ten other entries; the one that stops a reader is the bare heading at ui:cantos.set.title.
- fix: twenty-three required books. Likewise ui:home.fig.works: required books and operas; ui:home.how.list.2: The required books … In the message shown when JavaScript is off (index.html): gives the examination. The other seven are reworded in the fixes at ui:home.lede, ui:enrol.pace.semester.note, ui:cantos.intro.1, ui:cantos.version.temple.note and ui:cantos.set.title.
- weight: should

### ui:home.what.2
- where: No part of this site imitates Auden
- problem: The paragraph opens with two things the site does not do, an answer to a worry the student has not had, and keeps what the student does for the second sentence. The last sentence also promises Auden on "the book"; several of the paragraphs shown report what he said in class, or say he wrote nothing on it.
- fix: You read the books and do the work. The site checks what can be checked by rule: the cantos you write from memory and your answers on matters of fact. Nothing here grades your opinions, and nothing speaks in Auden's voice. Once a paper is handed in, the site shows what Auden wrote or said on the same matter, with the source.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.title

- fix: flag only. Repeat the list on the page headed by ui:term.title, or link to it from each week.
- weight: could

### ui:home.how.1
- where: Every week has the same parts.
- problem: The list that follows leaves out the operas. Nine of the fifteen weeks have one, with a libretto to read and a box to check when the opera has been heard. A student who reads only this list will not know that listening is part of the course.
- fix: Every week has the same six parts. Nine of the weeks add an opera: you read the libretto, which is printed here, and hear the opera through.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.1
- where: compare your words with the author's
- problem: Eight of the fifteen passages come from translations (weeks 1, 2, 7, 8, 9, 10, 14 and 15), so the words to be guessed are a translator's. The slip runs on through ui:words.intro.1, ui:words.show, ui:words.result and ui:words.after.1. Week 3's passage is also not from that week's reading.
- fix: *Missing words.* A passage with some of its words removed, nearly always from the week's reading. Fill the gaps before you open the book, then set your words beside the book's.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.2
- where: Most are printed here.
- problem: The student wants to know whether there are books to buy, and "most" does not say; all but one are on the site. The next sentence names "the ledger" before anything has said what it is, and "counts it against" can be read as a penalty.
- fix: *The reading.* The books in the order of the list. All but one are printed here; the exception is *The Family Reunion*, which you must borrow or buy. Check off what you have read, and the Ledger page shows whether you are ahead of the calendar or behind it.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.3
- where: The next stretch of a canto
- problem: Here "stretch" means the week's share, about a third of a canto. On the By heart pages a stretch is six to nine lines, and a week's share is several of them. "With the book shut" is also one of four ways the site says what its control labels "Text covered".
- fix: *By heart.* The next third of a canto, about fifty lines, learned a few lines at a time and then written out with the text covered.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.4
- where: marked at once
- problem: "Mark" for grade is British, and on this site the verb also means checking a book off as read (ui:reader.mark) and finishing an exercise (ui:making.done). The noun "marks" is ambiguous in the same way. Eight entries use the grading sense; three have findings of their own below (ui:enrol.intro.2, ui:test.mark, ui:question.intro.2).
- fix: *A test.* Passages to identify and questions of fact, checked as soon as you finish. Also ui:heart.rule.2: How Auden graded the examinations is not recorded. ui:final.over.body, second sentence: What you wrote will be checked as it stands. ui:final.over.mark: See the result. ui:notebook.reset.body: It erases your calendar, your record of reading, your scores and everything you have written.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.5
- where: One question, answered in at least
- problem: Every one of the fifteen weeks gives between two and five questions and asks for an answer to one of them, so this line is wrong for the whole course. The last clause promises Auden on "the same book", which some weeks cannot give.
- fix: *A question.* Two to five are asked each week. Answer one, in at least 150 words. Once your paper is handed in, the site shows what Auden wrote or said on the same matter.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.6
- where: a chart to draw, a page of
- problem: The four items can be read as the contents of every week's exercise; they are examples, one to a week. "To draw" is also wrong for the page, where each chart is a table with boxes to fill.
- fix: *An exercise.* One each week, of a different kind: one week a poem to put into English from a language you have not studied, another week a chart to fill in, a page of dictionary work or a parody.
- weight: should

- fix: The term ends with the examination: five cantos of Dante, about 700 lines of the poem in all, written out from memory in three hours.

### ui:enrol.intro.2

### ui:heart.how.1
- where: Read the whole canto aloud once
- problem: The five steps (this entry to ui:heart.how.5) never name the controls that carry them out: the tabs Read, Learn and Write out, and the three settings of the text. They also have their own words for the last setting, "the page covered" in step 2 and "nothing showing" in steps 3 and 4, where the control says "Text covered". - fix: 1. Open a canto. Under Read, read the whole of it aloud once, to know where it goes. 2. ## Patterns

Counts are of findings in this report. Where one finding stands for several entries of the text, the number of entries follows. A finding can show more than one fault.

1. An instruction leaves out what the student needs in order to act: that a button is final, when a thing counts as done, what happens next. 24 findings. See ui:words.intro.1, ui:notebook.keep.1.
2. One word for two things, or two words for one. 23 findings. Among them: week, known, stretch, mark, paper, work and book, set and required, the three names of the libretto, the two labels for the prose translation, pieces, See and Open, Back and Previous. See ui:state.known, ui:final.paper.title.
3. A statement that is not so on the site's own figures or pages, or not so for some students. 15 findings. See ui:term.intro.1, ui:heart.state.new.
4. A label or message that does not say what it does or what it counts. 9 findings. See ui:heart.cantos.change, ui:words.result.
5. British idiom in a voice meant to be American. 8 findings, 28 entries: the verb "set" in 11, "mark" and "marks" for grading in 8, "sit" and its noun in 7, and two single phrases. See ui:cantos.set.title, ui:question.intro.2.
6. A name or term the student has not been given. 6 findings. See ui:home.lede, ui:cantos.version.temple.note.
7. The translator spoken of as the author. 5 findings, 5 entries. See ui:home.how.list.1, ui:words.show.
8. The site talking about its sources or about itself between an instruction and the work. 5 findings. See ui:words.intro.2, ui:test.intro.2.
9. Vague where the site has the number. 4 findings. See ui:home.how.list.2, ui:heart.how.5.
10. The promise of Auden on the same book or opera, which some weeks cannot keep. 4 findings. See ui:unit.opera.after, ui:question.intro.2.
11. Coy, theatrical or joking. 4 findings. See ui:ledger.kicker, ui:notebook.intro.1.
12. "He" for Auden with nobody named near it. 3 findings, 6 entries. See ui:enrol.pace.semester.note, ui:question.intro.1.
13. A title not in italics. 3 findings, 3 entries. See ui:home.what.1, ui:final.intro.1.

On the brief's question about tone: no message cheers and none scolds. Two joke (ui:making.syll.note, ui:notebook.intro.1) and two strike an attitude (ui:ledger.kicker, ui:cantos.version.italian.note). The rest of the app's wording is plain, and most buttons say what they do.

Seen in the program and not in the wording I was given, for the editor to pass on:

- The program prints an em-dash for an empty value: in the missing-words result where a gap was left blank, and in the ledger's table where no test, paper or exercise is recorded. The house rule bars the em-dash.
- The dates in the list of weeks and in the ledger are joined by a spaced en-dash, where the front page joins them with "to". The house rule keeps the en-dash for ranges of numbers.
- The week 11 exercise has a passage for Longfellow's version and one for the Italian, and none for the prose version. A student learning the cantos in prose is given Longfellow's verse to correct.
