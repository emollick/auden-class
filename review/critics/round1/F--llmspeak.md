# F, weeks 10 to 12. Lens: writing that reads as machine-made

Findings are in the order of the text. A fix gives only the sentences that change; "(the rest stands)" means the remainder of the entry is to be left as it is. Quotations and quoted titles are not copied here; they appear as [quotation kept] or [title kept]. The name of Melville's novel is also left out of the fixes and appears as [book title kept].

## Patterns

Most frequent first. Nearly all of them sit in the paragraphs that report Auden; the instructions, test questions and word-list are clean.

1. British or old-fashioned idiom in a text meant to be American and plain. 12 instances: "get," "getting" and "have" by heart; "sat the examination"; "round" three times; "so that we should have"; three inverted sentences; "sets up as." See week10.questions.2.auden.paras.4 and week12.questions.1.auden.paras.2.
2. Signposts: a sentence that announces what Auden says next and says nothing itself. 10, nine of them the first sentence of a paragraph. See week11.questions.1.auden.paras.4 and week12.questions.2.auden.paras.3.
3. Staccato: runs of short sentences of one shape where one or two would do. Flagged in 11 entries; the paragraphs hold 16 sentences of five words or fewer. See week11.questions.2.auden.paras.4 and week12.reading.3.auden.paras.2.
4. Cleft constructions ("What X does is Y," "X is what Y"). 8, three of them opening a paragraph. See week11.questions.1.auden.paras.3 and week12.reading.3.auden.paras.2.
5. Whose statement is it: Auden's opinion given as fact, or the site's own remark set among Auden's with no mark. 8 entries. See week12.reading.3.auden.paras.2 and week10.questions.1.auden.paras.4.
6. Inconsistent form. 7 kinds in 7 findings: italics in headings, the name of the overture, apostrophes, the lines that locate a test passage, one page range, "the site" against "this site," straight quotation marks. See week11.words.cite and week10.test.items.2.where.
7. A denial in one sentence and the assertion in the next, which is "not X but Y" with a full stop in the middle. 5 entries, four flagged; the fifth, week11.questions.3.auden.paras.1, answers a real misreading and can stand. See week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2 and week11.questions.3.auden.paras.3.
8. A short sentence or tag added for the beat. 4. See week11.questions.3.auden.paras.2 and week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2.
9. Vague where the exact thing is known: an unnamed magazine, a May with no year, "a good deal," "something like this." 4. See week10.intro.2 and week11.intro.3.
10. The week opens on a noun phrase with no verb and a count of pages. 3 of 3. See week10.intro.1 and week12.intro.1.
11. A pair counted out as "two ... One is ... The other is." 2. See week10.questions.3.auden.paras.3 and week11.questions.1.auden.paras.2.

Not found in this part: em-dashes, exclamation marks, rhetorical questions outside the set questions, lists of three by reflex, hype words, dates in British form.
