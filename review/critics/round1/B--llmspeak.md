# B, pages and books: writing that reads as machine-made

Part read: every entry of B-pages-and-books.md (reading-list page, sources page, the notes on each book, the head-notes in the reader).

General verdict before the findings. Most of this part is plain and should be left alone: the table rows, the works cited, the catalogue lines. I found no em-dash, no exclamation mark, no rhetorical question and no hype word in it. The machine shows in four places. (1) Captions written as verbless fragments, then repeated in the same mold. (2) Short flat sentences set at the end of a paragraph for the beat. (3) Head-notes that splice a caption to a clause with a semicolon. (4) Confident statements on the sources page that other entries on the same site contradict. The fourth matters most, because that page exists to be trusted.

## Page: syllabus

- fix: Write "The instructor's name is typed as [quotation kept]". Then: "The sheet carries no year; the standard bibliography of Auden puts it in the fall semester of 1941."
- weight: could

### page:syllabus.intro.3

- problem: Signpost. The column heads already say what the tables give. The one fact in it is that the order is the sheet's.
- fix: "The tables keep the order of the sheet."
- weight: could

### page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1

- problem: A verbless caption. All three section openers on this page are cast in this mold (a number, a noun, a comma, a tag), and by the third the reader hears the mold before the fact.
- fix: "The sheet lists twenty-three required books. One entry is a choice between two Greek plays."
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1

### page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.1

### page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.2

- problem: The table just above has a column of editions that the sheet does name, so the flat statement reads as a contradiction. It is true only of the libretti. "The opera house" is left unnamed, though four of the head-notes name it.
- fix: "The sheet names no editions of the libretti. Most of the copies printed here are the English halves of two-language libretti sold at the Metropolitan Opera House by Fred Rullman of New York." (If that is the house meant; three of the Rullman head-notes do not name one.)

### page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.1
- where: Eighteen books, recommended and not required.
- problem: Third fragment opener. The "and not" half is there for the beat: the heading directly above says recommended, and no reader supposes otherwise.
- fix: "The sheet recommends eighteen books of criticism."
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.1
- where: and ends at the foot of the leaf
- problem: The two pages say "sheet" sixteen times and "leaf" once, here, with "foot" beside it. It is variation for its own sake in a bibliographer's costume, and a student may take the leaf for a different thing from the sheet.
- fix: "The picture shows the first eight and stops at the bottom of the sheet."
- weight: should

## Notes on the books (work:…)

The catalogue formula ("X's translation of [year] is printed here") is right for a table column and should stay. The findings below are the places where the formula breaks or leaves the student's question unanswered.

### work:agamemnon.copy
- where: MacNeice's translation (1936) is in copyright.
- problem: The year of a translation is given two ways inside one note: in parentheses for the edition on the list, with "of" for the one printed. Four notes use the parenthesis (this one, the Sophocles, the Ibsen, the Rimbaud); twelve others use "of" alone.
- fix: Use "of" throughout: "MacNeice's translation of 1936"; "The Fitts and Fitzgerald translation of 1939"; "The translation of 1892 by William and Charles Archer"; "Schwartz's translation of 1939".
- weight: should

### work:horace.copy
- where: is printed here, and the Latin
- problem: A second subject tacked on after the verb. It is a mannered inversion, and the note on the Dante does the same job plainly with "with".
- fix: End the sentence "is printed here with the Latin."
- weight: should

### work:faust.copy
- where: Bayard Taylor's translation of 1870 is printed here.
- problem: The note says a different translation is printed and gives no reason, so the student's first question goes unanswered. The sources page promises that where a substitute stands in for an edition in copyright, the reading list says so. The notes on Horace, Augustine and Ibsen are silent in the same way.
- fix: flag only; the editor must supply the reason in each of the four. Form: "The Everyman volume is Albert Latham's translation, which [is in copyright / has no reliable text online]."
- weight: should

### work:baudelaire.copy

- problem: Two things the student needs are missing. The note does not say that the Isherwood is in copyright, though the notes on Aeschylus, Sophocles and Rimbaud say it of theirs. - fix: After the first sentence add "It is in copyright." In the second, after the year, add ", under the title *Intimate Papers*,".

### work:orpheus.copy

## Patterns

Most frequent first. By importance the order is different: the contradictions (no. 6) first, then the posed closers (no. 3), the fragment openers (no. 9) and the spliced captions (no. 7).

1. One stock clause on translators and dates. "Names no translator" 14 times (7 table rows, 7 head-notes, counting the *Dutchman*'s variant), "bears no date" 7 times. Needed in the head-notes; a stamp in the table. See work:fidelio.copy, text:traviata.note.

2. "Is printed here." as the last words of a note: 11 notes, and "printed here" in 20 entries in all. This is a catalogue formula in a table column. Leave it. See work:phedre.copy, work:castle.copy.

3. An entry that closes on a sentence of nine words or fewer: 11 prose entries on the two pages. Three are posed and are flagged (page:about.lede, page:about.sections.2.paras.1, page:about.sections.3.paras.2). The rest state a fact the student needs. The habit is worth watching all the same: when every paragraph lands on a short flat sentence, the plainness starts to sound like a manner.

4. The flat negative that reports a gap ("the sheet gives no…", "no … survives", "is not recorded"): 10 times. See page:about.sections.3.paras.1, page:about.sections.5.list.4. This is the page doing its job, and most should stay. Three of the ten are also closers counted in no. 3. Three are inexact and are flagged: page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1, page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.2, page:about.sections.4.table.rows.10.2.

5. Titles unmarked or wrongly marked, against the house rule: 7 entries. Five head-notes have bare titles (text:poems.note, text:rilke-de.note, and three more), one has straight quotation marks (text:tristan.note), and the three parts of the Dante are in roman in page:about.sections.2.paras.1.

6. A statement on the sources page that another entry contradicts or does not bear out: 6, plus one doubtful. The parody (page:about.sections.4.paras.1), Rougemont (page:about.sections.1.table.rows.11.1), the fifth canto (page:about.sections.5.list.5), the collation of the scanned books (page:about.sections.7.paras.3), 280 pages against 4,700 (page:about.sections.3.paras.1), the promise that the list gives the reason for each substitute (see work:faust.copy, work:baudelaire.copy). The doubtful one is the single surviving sheet (page:syllabus.intro.1). None of these is a matter of style, but they are where a reader stops trusting the page, and a plain confident sentence is what makes each of them look settled.

7. A verbless caption spliced to a full clause with a semicolon: 5 head-notes. See text:carmen.note, text:fidelio.note. The fix is the same in each: full stop, new sentence.

8. Two wordings for one thing: 4 cases. "Taken by machine" 6 times and "read by machine" 3 (text:orpheus.note, text:carmen.note). The year of a translation in parentheses in 4 notes and with "of" in 12 (work:antigone.copy, work:faust.copy). One page range shortened and the rest in full (page:about.sections.1.table.rows.12.2). Curly apostrophes in one entry and straight in all others (text:antigone.headings).

9. A section opener written as a number, a noun and a tag, with no verb: 3 of the 3 on the reading-list page. See page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1, page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.1.

10. One fact said more than once on the sources page: 2 facts, 5 statements. The six questions of Auden's own are counted three times (page:about.sections.4.paras.1, page:about.sections.4.table.rows.8.2, page:about.sections.5.list.3). That Pearce names four cantos is said twice (page:about.sections.2.paras.3, page:about.sections.5.list.5).

Looked for and not found in this part: em-dashes, exclamation marks, rhetorical questions, "not X but Y", hedging (the "about" and "roughly" here are honest approximations of number), hype, the inflated verbs and nouns on the lens list (one borderline case, "mapped"). Groups of three: about a dozen, each listing three real things, with one exception that is flagged (page:about.sections.5.list.3). "This site" as subject: 11 times, all on pages whose business is to say what the site did.
