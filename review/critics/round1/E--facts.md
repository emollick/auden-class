# E: weeks 7 to 9, facts

Checked against the site's own copies (text id and part given), site/data/course.json, and the web. Reports of what Auden wrote in *The Dyer's Hand*, *The Enchafèd Flood*, *Forewords and Afterwords* and Pearce's essay, and page numbers in those four, were left to the other critics, as the brief says.

## Findings

### week7.reading.3.auden.paras.4
- where: It is Brangäne and Kurwenal who bring everything about.
- problem: The names are Wagner's and are right, but the libretto the site prints spells the two servants Brangaena and Kurvenal, and the heroine Isolda (text id tristan, all three acts). A student who has just read that libretto meets the German forms here for the first time.
- fix: It is Brangäne and Kurwenal (Brangaena and Kurvenal in the English printed here) who bring everything about.
- weight: could

### week7.words.cite
- where: in William and Charles Archer's translation of 1892
- problem: The passage is printed in the wording the Archers revised in 1907, which is what the site's note on the book says it prints (course.json, works, peer-gynt; texts/peer-gynt/index.json, note). The 1892 edition (London: Walter Scott; https://archive.org/details/peergyntdramatic00ibse) words two lines of this speech differently. One of the ten words the student must supply, "echo", is not in the 1892 text, which has "murmur" in its place.
- fix: Ibsen, *Peer Gynt*, Act 1, Scene 1. Translated by William and Charles Archer (1892; the text is their revision of 1907).
- weight: should

### week8.questions.1.auden.sources.1
- where: (Ecco Press, 1994), pp. 50–51
- problem: Two things. (1) The page range stops a page short. The reply that the third paragraph reports begins on p. 52. In the archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/prolificdevourer00aude) the full-text search shows the foot line of p. 49, then an even page, then an odd page that ends where Auden says he will take the Inquisitor's arguments in turn; the Inquisitor's sentence on freedom opens the page after it. The earlier research note E_pd_pearce.md has the same page breaks and puts the reply on pp. 52 to 58. (2) The year is given two ways. The Auden Society's list has Ecco Press, 1993 (https://audensociety.org/books.html) and the Library of Congress number is 93-2409; library records give 1994 for the first trade edition (Open Library, ISBN 0880013451). I could not see the copyright page.
- fix: Print the pages as pp. 50–52. Flag for the year: print what the copyright page of the copy used says; if it cannot be seen, keep 1994.
- weight: should

### week8.questions.2.gloss.1
- where: He takes his examples from the newspapers
- problem: Ivan says he copies his cases from newspapers and from books. In the chapter he also draws on a man he met in Moscow, a pamphlet translated from the French, a poem of Nekrasov's and a collection of antiquities (karamazov, b05c04).
- fix: Change the second sentence to: He takes his examples from newspapers and books, and limits them to children.
- weight: could

### week8.questions.2.auden.paras.4
- where: for the last book on the list, *The Castle*
- problem: *The Castle* is not the last book on the list. The sheet, and the site's own table of it, end the required reading with T. S. Eliot's *The Family Reunion*, which is also read after it in week 15 (course.json, works 23 and 24; units 15). It is the last novel on the list.
- fix: Keep the second temptation in mind for the last novel on the list, *The Castle*.
- weight: must

### week8.making.1.brief.2
- where: The chapter is twenty-six pages long.
- problem: The site's reader gives this chapter as 27 pp. The stored length is 26.5 pages (texts/karamazov/index.json, b05c05) and the reader rounds it up (site/js/views/reader.js, line 70), so the student sees one figure in the exercise and another in the contents of the book.
- fix: The chapter is twenty-seven pages long.
- weight: should

### week9.intro.1
- where: Book X then leaves the brothers
- problem: Book X leaves Mitya and Ivan, but not Alyosha. He is in four of its seven chapters, from "The Lost Dog" to "Ilusha", and Kolya's long talk in Chapter 6 is with him (karamazov, b10c04 to b10c07).
- fix: Book X then leaves Mitya under arrest and goes among schoolboys, and Alyosha goes with it.
- weight: should

### week9.making.1.rules.2
- where: the lines have five, eight, eight and four syllables
- problem: True of Campion's poem only as he sounded it. His second stanza, which the site shows afterwards as the answer, is printed there with "Heaven" and "heavenly" in full (course.json, units 9, making, answer). The site's own counter gives those two lines nine and five syllables (site/js/lib/text.js: heaven 2, heavenly 3), so Campion's stanza fails the rule the student has just been held to. The 1602 text marks the first elision ("Heau'n"), and so does the Toronto text (https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/poems/rose-cheeked-laura).
- fix: Leave the rule. In the answer print "Heav'n is music", as Campion did, and add this sentence to the note shown with it: Campion sounds "Heav'n" as one syllable and "heavenly" as two.
- weight: should

### week9.making.1.answer.after.1
- where: as one sentence of argument
- problem: The poem is not one sentence. As the site prints it, the first stanza ends with a full stop and so does the second; only the third and fourth run together (course.json, units 9, making, model and answer).
- fix: Read the four stanzas through as one argument.
- weight: could

## Patterns

1. A statement about a book or the list that is looser than the book (4): week8.questions.2.auden.paras.4, week9.intro.1; also week8.questions.2.gloss.1 and week9.making.1.answer.after.1.
2. The prose says one thing and the site's own data, reader or printed text shows another (4): week8.making.1.brief.2, week9.making.1.rules.2; also week7.words.cite, which disagrees with the site's note on the book, and week7.reading.3.auden.paras.4.

One citation outside the four excluded sources is a page short, week8.questions.1.auden.sources.1. The other two I could test hold: Carpenter's index gives 264f. for St. Mark's School, and the table of contents of the Spender volume puts Mandelbaum's piece on pp. 119 to 122.

No wrong date, name, title, place or first performance was found in these three weeks. All 42 test items have the right answer and the right place. All 21 passages set for the student to place, and the three passages with missing words, stand word for word in the part named.

Not confirmed and not reported above, because they are statements that something does not exist: week7.questions.2.auden.paras.1 (no account of the course says what he made of the novel), week8.questions.2.auden.paras.1 (nothing in print on the chapter). A search of the archive.org copies of *Forewords and Afterwords*, *The Enchafèd Flood*, *A Certain World*, the Shakespeare lectures and the third volume of the prose found the novel named once, in passing, and nothing on Book V, Chapter 4. That is support, not proof.

Outside my lens, for whoever checks Pearce's page numbers: the earlier research notes disagree on where he lists the reading set for the lecture on love. C_auden_on_works.md gives p. 136; E_pd_pearce.md and A_michigan_course.md give p. 137. The site prints p. 137 (week8.reading.2.auden.sources.3).
