## Patterns

Most frequent first. A count is of places in the text. Where a place has no finding of its own above, it is named here.

1. A term, a name or a book used before it is explained, or knowledge of Auden's other writing taken for granted. 19 findings. See week4.questions.1.auden.paras.2 and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2. Nearly all are in the paragraphs shown after a paper is handed in.

2. A place in a test given without the book, or without the number of the pensée. 19 items under 4 findings: 7 questions of fact in week 4, 4 in week 5 and 6 in week 6, and the 2 Pascal passages in week 5. See week4.test.items.2.where and week5.test.items.11.where.

3. A pronoun, a "this" or a clause that can be read two ways. 14 findings. See week5.questions.1.auden.paras.1 and week6.questions.2.auden.paras.3.

4. A report of something Auden wrote after 1941 with no date, though the Sources page says these paragraphs give the dates. 12 paragraphs: week4.questions.1.auden.paras.4 (the second essay), week4.questions.2.auden.paras.1, week4.questions.3.auden.paras.1 and paras.2, week5.reading.4.auden.paras.1, week5.questions.2.auden.paras.1, week5.questions.3.auden.paras.3, week6.reading.7.auden.paras.1, week6.questions.1.auden.paras.3, week6.questions.2.auden.paras.4, week6.questions.3.auden.paras.1 and paras.3. The fix is the same each time: give the year of the essay, or of the book it is printed in.

5. One thing under two names, or one word for two things. 11 places. Six have findings; see week4.questions.1.auden.paras.2 and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.3. Five have none: week4.making.1.table.key.columns.2 (the column I filled comes back under another heading), week6.reading.7.auden.paras.1 (Leporello's catalogue is called the list, which everywhere else is the reading list), week6.questions.3.auden.paras.3 (the heroine has her French name here only), week6.questions.5.auden.paras.1 (the dandy has a capital here and a small letter in the question), week6.making.1.brief.2 (the proverbs of Hell are given to the Devil who speaks in the fourth question).

6. An instruction or an opening paragraph that leaves out what to do, how much, or what comes next. 10 findings. See week4.intro.1 and week6.intro.1. One more place has no finding: week4.intro.2 does not say that a new canto, Inferno V, begins this week, as the opening of week 1 did for its canto.

7. A report of Auden that promises more than it gives: something he is said to have made of a fact, a use allowed to each of two things, a question said to be answered. 6 findings. See week4.questions.1.auden.paras.2 and week5.questions.3.auden.paras.2.

8. A pointer to words I may never have been shown, because they stand behind a paper on another question, or on another page. 5 findings. See week5.reading.4.auden.paras.1 and week6.questions.5.auden.paras.3.

9. A hint that fails. One cannot be carried out, one gives the answer away, one names no place, one points to last week's reading without saying so, and one leads to a wrong word. 5 findings. See week4.questions.3.gloss.1 and week5.questions.1.gloss.1.

10. The site's own instruction inside the block headed as Auden's, with nothing to mark the change of speaker. 3 places: week4.questions.2.auden.paras.4, week6.questions.5.auden.paras.3, and week5.questions.2.auden.paras.4, which has no finding above.

11. A test that marks a true answer wrong. 3 findings: week5.test.items.11.ask, week5.test.items.13.ask and week4.test.items.5.speaker.

House rules. In this part I found no em-dash, no en-dash outside a range of pages, no exclamation mark, no rhetorical question and no date in the wrong form. The one breach is titles in plain type in headings (week4.making.1.title).
