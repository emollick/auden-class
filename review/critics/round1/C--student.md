# C, weeks 1 to 3: read as the student who has to use it

Findings are in the order of the text. In a fix, [quotation kept] stands for a quotation that the text has and the fix keeps.

## Week 1

### week1.intro.1
- where: in the prose Auden set or in verse
- problem: Neither version is named and nothing says how to choose between them. A student who has not studied the reading-list page cannot tell which prose is meant or whose verse, and "the prose Auden set" can be read as a boxed set.
- fix: End the second sentence after "sixty-seven cantos". Then add: "Read them in the prose of Carlyle, Okey and Wicksteed, which is the translation Auden assigned, or in Longfellow's verse. You can change from one to the other at the head of any canto in the reader."
- weight: should

### week1.intro.2
- where: Begin Inferno III by heart
- problem: A verb is missing: a canto is learned by heart, and nobody begins one by heart. The title is in italics in the paragraph above and plain here.
- fix: First sentence: "Begin learning *Inferno* III by heart on the first day." Leave the second sentence as it is.
- weight: should

### week1.intro.3
- where: goes with it. It is short.
- problem: Two "it"s in a row with different owners, and the opera is not named. The paragraph also says only what the opera section says in full a few lines further down the same page.
- fix: cut. If a pointer is wanted here: "This week also has the first of the nine operas, Gluck's *Orpheus*. Its libretto is thirteen pages."
- weight: should

### week1.reading.2.paras.1
- where: Then hear the opera through
- problem: Nothing says where the opera is to be heard: the site has no recordings and this page does not say so, so the student looks for something to play and finds only a checkbox. The thing to keep open also has three names, the libretto, the words, and on the checkbox the text.
- fix: "Nine operas are on the list. Auden assigned their libretti as reading, and this one comes first. Read the libretto, thirteen pages long. Then listen to the whole opera with the libretto open. This site has no recordings: find one in a library or on a streaming service. Any complete recording will do. The opera lasts about an hour and a half."
- weight: must

### week1.reading.2.paras.2
- where: and you may hear either
- problem: The permission is the one thing here to act on, and it sits at the end of the history, without the titles under which recordings of the two versions are sold. Nothing says that the libretto in the reader is an English translation in four acts while both of Gluck's versions have three, so a student following along loses the place when the libretto's second act ends.
- fix: "The words are by Ranieri de' Calzabigi. You may listen to either of Gluck's two versions. The first was sung in Italian in Vienna in October 1762 and is sold as *Orfeo ed Euridice*. The second, longer and in French, was made for Paris in 1774 and is sold as *Orphée et Eurydice*. The libretto printed here is an English translation in four acts. Most recordings have three: their second act is the second and third acts printed here, and their third act is the fourth." The editor must check the last two sentences against the copy in the reader.
- weight: should

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1
- where: He names it once, as a date.
- problem: The app's line above this block promises what Auden wrote about this opera, and the block opens by saying that nothing has been found. The second sentence is cryptic, and the third runs two claims together and has to be read twice.
- fix: "Auden left nothing that has been found on *Orpheus* itself, so what follows is about opera in general. He mentions *Orpheus* once, to mark the year in which an age began. In 1948 he put the *Divine Comedy* above every Greek work. In the same essay he says that the seventy-five years in which the Athenian tragedies were written are outdone, as a run of work in a single art, by [quotation kept]. By his count that age ran from Gluck's *Orpheus* in 1762 to Verdi's *Otello* in 1887, a hundred and twenty-five years. Each of the nine operas on the list was first performed inside that span." For this week the app's line (unit.opera.after) should promise what Auden wrote about opera, not about this opera.
- weight: should

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.2

1. A name, a book or a term used with no word of introduction (15). "Set" for "assign" six times (week1.intro.1, week1.reading.2.paras.1, week2.intro.2, week2.making.2.brief.2, week2.making.2.source.1), "hear ... through", "first given", "by you", "take more trouble over", "comes into it", "comes round", "cannot be got", "counsellors", and the word order of week1.making.1.reflect.
