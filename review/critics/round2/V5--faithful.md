# V5 (weeks 11 to 13), lens: faithful to the sources

Second-round check of every entry in V5.md that reports what Auden or Donald Pearce wrote, said or did. No source text and no quotation from the site is printed here. Evidence is a scan file with line numbers (for `pearce.txt`, line and character offset, written L3@n), or a file in `research/` with a line number.

Result: 18 findings (0 must, 7 should, 11 could). All 30 quotations match their sources. All 22 citations of title, date and page are right. In week 13, which was rewritten most recently, the chart of the nine ships and its key, the paragraph on Chapters 128 and 132, and the paragraph that cites "American Poetry" are faithful to their sources. The four findings in week 13 are small.

## Patterns

1. The site's own bridge, inference or added detail stands unmarked among statements given to Auden or to a witness. Count: 7. See week11.questions.3.auden.paras.3 (two places) and week12.questions.1.auden.paras.4. The others are week11.questions.1.auden.paras.4, week11.questions.2.auden.paras.2, week11.questions.3.auden.paras.4 and week13.questions.2.auden.paras.3. Three are short linking sentences set between two reported statements, and the cure for each is to cut it. Four are a detail that the rewording added: who is addressed, where a thing was said, how many things were compared, and who it was that fell silent.

2. A report that is stronger, narrower or more definite than its source. Count: 6. See week12.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week12.questions.1.auden.paras.3. The others are week11.making.1.brief.1, week12.reading.3.auden.paras.4, week12.questions.2.auden.paras.2 and week13.making.1.brief.1. Four of the six turn on one word ("all," "alone," "the same," "each").

3. Closeness: seven of the source's words in a row outside quotation marks. Count: 5. See week11.questions.3.auden.paras.1 and week13.questions.4.auden.paras.1. The others are week11.questions.1.auden.paras.3, week12.making.1.brief.1 and week13.questions.1.auden.paras.2. No run is longer than seven, and the longer runs that the first audit listed for these weeks are gone.

Not counted as findings, for the editor's eye only: runs of six words, one short of the limit. They begin "he pushes open" (week11.questions.1.auden.paras.1), "it do not" and "man who has" (week11.questions.1.auden.paras.2), "a pound of" (week12.reading.3.auden.paras.2), "and makes it" (week12.questions.1.auden.paras.1), "must will and" and "and he ends" (week13.questions.1.auden.paras.3), and "religion says prayers" (week13.questions.4.auden.paras.2). The run that begins "must will and" goes straight on into a quotation.

No statement in this part rests on a note that the researchers marked as only recalled. Nothing was left unchecked. One point rests on a researcher's gloss and not on Auden's printed words: the extract of "The Christian Tragic Hero" gives only the end of the sentence about the last offer, and the note beside it says that the offer is the Rachel's call for help (C_auden_on_works.md line 1099). The web did not supply the whole sentence.

## Checked and right

Every entry in the part that reports Auden, Pearce or another witness was checked. Each line gives the label, the number of statements checked (s) and of quotations checked (q), and where the source stands. "Finding" means the label has an entry above and everything else under it was right. Totals: 260 statements, and 26 facts about the book in the third column of the chart; 30 quotations, all matching; 22 citations, all right.

Three conventions, so the counts can be read. A quoted single word counts as a quotation. A title in quotation marks was checked as a citation and is not counted as a quotation. Where the source runs on after the quoted words, the period or comma inside the site's closing quotation mark is the site's own and was not counted as a difference.
