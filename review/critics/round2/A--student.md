# Part A, read as the student who has to use it

Where a fix keeps words that the text has inside quotation marks, they are written [quotation kept]. Several fixes give only the words that change, with the place they go.

### ui:nav.papers
- where: Papers
- problem: The page this opens holds the papers, the replies to Auden, the exercises and the notes, yet by the site's own usage a paper is the weekly essay only. The name is then used for the whole collection in ui:notebook.title and in every pointer to "the Papers page".
- fix: flag only. The editor must decide whether the page keeps this name. If it changes, "Written work" says what is there, and ui:notebook.title, ui:enrol.intro.2, ui:words.after.1 and ui:store.warning must change with it.
- weight: could

### ui:home.lede
- where: nearly all of it is of kinds he gave
- problem: The end of this sentence has to be read twice. "Is of kinds" is hard to parse, and "own" contrasts with nothing.
- fix: Replace the last seven words with "modeled on work he gave his classes".
- weight: should

### ui:home.what.1
- where: By the fullest account, the final examination was
- problem: The sentence before says the class would be tested on what they thought, and this one says the examination was memory work, with nothing to mark the contrast. "Libretti" in the first sentence has no gloss.
- fix: Begin the last sentence "The final examination, though, was by the fullest account three hours of". In the first sentence write "nine operas whose words, the libretti, were to be read".
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.1
- where: listen to a recording of the opera
- problem: The list under this says which book must be borrowed or bought, but nothing on the page says the nine recordings are not supplied. A student planning the term learns it only on the week's own page.
- fix: Add a third sentence: "The site has no recordings, so you must find those yourself."
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.2
- where: Mark each part as read when you finish
- problem: Two lines up, "parts" are the six parts of a week's work. Here a part is a division of a book, and the sentence can be taken to mean the six.
- fix: Write "Mark each part of a book as read when you finish it".
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.5
- where: Answer one of them, in at least 150
- problem: The page has already said the site does not grade "your papers", and it later points to a Papers page, but it never says that a paper is the answer to the week's question.
- fix: "Answer one of them in a paper of at least 150 words."
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.after.2
- where: Some of this is documented and some is
- problem: This is the third time the page says it: the lede has already told the student which parts are Auden's and which the site's, and the colophon at the foot repeats the pointer to Sources. Here it stands between the account of the week and the form the student has come to fill in.
- fix: cut
- weight: should

### ui:home.week.lastday
- where: Today is the last day.
- problem: It does not say the last day of what, and the same page shows a figure for the last day of term.
- fix: "Today is the last day of the week."
- weight: could

### ui:unit.omit
- where: It is left out of the week’s pages
- problem: This reads as if the book were not printed on the site, when the checkbox under it offers the book to read. What is meant is that its pages are not counted.
- fix: Second sentence: "Its pages are not counted in the week’s reading, no test depends on it, and no question on it is required."
- weight: should

### ui:unit.work
- where: The work
- problem: On the home page and on The term page "The week’s work" is all six parts. Here "The work" heads only the four that follow the reading.
- fix: "The rest of the week’s work"
- weight: could

### ui:unit.words.what
- where: Do it before you open the book
- problem: "It" has no owner, since the sentence before names a passage and nobody does a passage. The home page gives the same instruction with a plain verb.
- fix: "A passage with words taken out. Fill the gaps before you open the book."
- weight: should

### ui:words.source
- where: his classes at Swarthmore College
- problem: The student has heard only of Michigan in 1941. Swarthmore arrives with no word on when Auden taught there.
- fix: Begin "At Swarthmore College, where he taught from 1942 to 1945, Auden gave his classes poems they had not seen" and go on as now. The dates are the ones the Sources page gives.
- weight: could

### ui:test.intro.1
- where: name the book as well
- problem: The book is picked from a menu and not typed, so "name the book", coming just before the rule on spelling, sends the student looking for a place to write a title. The condition that matters most, no books, is the last short sentence.
- fix: Open with "Take the test without the books." and drop the last sentence. For the third sentence write "If the passages are from more than one book, also choose the book from the menu."
- weight: should

### ui:test.source
- where: Monroe Spears reports that Auden’s examinations
- problem: Spears is a bare name, with nothing to say who he was or when he wrote, and Swarthmore is undated here too.
- fix: "At Swarthmore College, where Auden taught from 1942 to 1945, his examinations [quotation kept], as Monroe Spears reported in the college’s bulletin in 1962. See [Sources](#/about)."
- weight: should

### ui:question.intro.1
- where: that this was what they would be tested
- problem: A remark about 1941 sits between two instructions, and its "this" has no single owner (the word count, or the book and the student's own thinking). It also says the class would be tested on the very thing the next paragraph says is not graded here, and the home page has already reported the remark.
- fix: Cut the second sentence. The first and third stand as they are.
- weight: must

### ui:making.notyet.table
- where: of the {n} boxes needed
- problem: There is no verb, and the message can be read as saying that only {have} of the boxes are needed.
- fix: "Boxes filled: {have}. The exercise asks for {n}."
- weight: should

### ui:making.notyet.emend
- where: corrections needed before you can finish
- problem: The same fault as the message for the chart: no verb, so it is not clear whether {have} counts what is done or what is wanted.
- fix: "Corrections made: {have}. You need {n} before you can finish."
- weight: should

### ui:making.emend.result.sound
- where: one word that was sound
- problem: In the message before, what the student does is "marked" and what was done to the passage is "changed"; here what the student does becomes "altered". "Sound" for "not changed" is also a poor word to lean on in an exercise on verse, where it is first read as a noun.
- fix: "You also marked one word that had not been changed.|You also marked {n} words that had not been changed."
- weight: should

### ui:heart.intro.2
- where: learn long stretches of poetry by heart
- problem: On this page a "stretch" is the site's name for the few lines learned at one go, defined a little further down, and here the same word means something long. The short second sentence is also set at the end of the paragraph for the beat.
- fix: "Auden made his Michigan classes learn long passages of poetry by heart, and students who objected were told to try it first." The Sources page joins the two statements the same way.
- weight: should

### ui:heart.how.3
- where: then with first letters only
- problem: This can be read as an order to write only the first letters. What is meant is that the text shows only the first letter of each word, and the same wording is in ui:heart.learn.intro.1 and on the button ui:heart.learn.toLetters.
- fix: "Write the stretch three times, first with the text open, then with only the first letter of each word showing, then with the text covered." On the button: "Now with only first letters showing".
- weight: should

### ui:heart.how.4
- where: and write it together with what came before
- problem: The numbered steps go from one stretch straight to writing out the whole canto. Nothing says to go on to the next stretch and repeat, or how far to go in a week.
- fix: Add at the end of the step: "Then go on to the next stretch and do the same, as far as the week asks."
- weight: should

### ui:heart.how.6
- where: say the canto aloud to them before you write
- problem: The list is numbered, and this sixth step has to be done before the fifth.
- fix: Make it step 5 and shorten it to "If someone will listen, say the whole canto aloud to them." The step that begins "Under Write out" then follows as step 6.
- weight: should

### ui:heart.state.once
- where: written out once; due again on {date}
- problem: "Once" counts only the time that came within the limit. A student who has written the canto out three times and come within the limit once sees this beside a count of three on the Progress page.
- fix: "within the limit once; due again on {date}"
- weight: should

### ui:heart.unverified
- where: Do not learn it by heart in this version
- problem: The remedy cannot be followed as written, because the version is one setting for all five cantos and the note does not say where it is changed or that a different fifth canto would also do. "Read through against the printed page" and "misread word" do not say who read or misread what.
- fix: "The text of this canto in the prose version has not been checked word for word against the printed book, and a wrong word may remain. Do not learn it from this text. Use the button “Choose the version and the fifth canto” on the By heart page, and either pick a different fifth canto or change all five cantos to Longfellow’s verse or the Italian."
- weight: should

### ui:reader.unverified
- where: compared by machine with other copies
- problem: This note stands at the foot of most cantos of the prose version. Its first sentence tells how the text was prepared, which the reader can do nothing with, when the one thing needed is the warning.
- fix: "The text of this canto has not been checked word for word against the printed book, and a wrong word may remain."
- weight: should

### ui:heart.learn.intro.1
- where: Read the stretch aloud twice

- fix: First sentence: "Read the stretch aloud until you can hear it without the text."
- weight: should

### ui:heart.write.intro.1

### ui:cantos.fifth.intro.1
- where: Choose any other canto by its number below
- problem: In the prose version, which Auden assigned and which the site starts with, most cantos cannot be chosen (eight can, by the site's data), as the note printed directly under this says. The first sentence is false for anyone learning in that version, and "three weeks" does not say which three.
- fix: "Choose another canto by its number below. You will learn it in weeks 7 to 9 of the course and write it at the examination, so read several before choosing."
- weight: must

### ui:cantos.unverified.note
- where: read through against the printed book
- problem: The idiom does not say who did the reading, and "misread word" does not say who misread it. The same thing is worded three ways ("against the printed page" in ui:heart.unverified, "against the page" in ui:reader.unverified, "against the printed book" here and in ui:cantos.unverified.title), and the third sentence has "Longfellow’s version" where every other entry has "Longfellow’s verse".
- fix: "In the prose version only a canto whose text has been checked word for word against the printed book can be chosen. The rest may still contain a wrong word and are for reading only." In the third sentence change "version" to "verse". For ui:cantos.unverified.title: "Not checked word for word against the printed book".
- weight: should

### ui:final.rules.4
- where: Spelling counts as it does on the By heart

- fix: For the second sentence write "Spelling counts, but British and American spellings of one word pass as the same." In the third sentence add "accents" after "punctuation".
- weight: should

### ui:notebook.keep.2
- where: open this page there and choose
- problem: Nothing says that the saving button puts a file on the student's device, and the instruction for moving leaves out the step that matters, which is taking that file to the other device. Followed as written, the student opens the page on the new device, presses the restoring button and has nothing to give it.

Third sentence: "To move to another device, carry that file there, open this page and choose [quotation kept]." Keep the second sentence, and end the fourth "with what is in the file." ## Patterns

1. One name for two things, or two names for one (9). "Part" for a part of the week and a part of a book, "stretch" in two senses, "papers" for all the written work, "this course" for the site, three wordings for the cantos not yet checked. See ui:home.how.list.2 and ui:notebook.text.
2. Wording that can be read two ways because a pointer has no owner or a verb or object is missing (9). See ui:making.notyet.table and ui:unit.words.what.
3. An instruction that leaves out a step, a place or a condition (7). See ui:notebook.keep.2 and ui:heart.how.4.
4. Two entries that disagree (5). See ui:heart.learn.intro.1 with ui:heart.how.2, and ui:cantos.fifth.intro.1 with ui:cantos.unverified.note.
5. The site explaining itself or its sources where the student needs only the instruction (3). See ui:question.intro.1 and ui:reader.unverified.
6. A person, place or term with no introduction (3). See ui:test.source and ui:words.source.
7. A step or condition placed after the thing it governs (2). See ui:heart.how.6 and ui:test.intro.1.

Not found in this part: no message scolds, cheers or jokes, and there is no em-dash, exclamation mark or hedge. The three questions are real questions that ask the student to confirm a step.
