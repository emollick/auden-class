# V3, weeks 6 and 7: is the report of the source faithful?

Second-round check of every entry in `parts/V3.md` that reports what Auden or Donald Pearce wrote, said or did: 50 entries, about 200 statements, 17 quotations. Evidence is given as scan file and line numbers (character offsets for the Pearce captures, which are a few very long lines). No sentence of a source and no quotation of the site's is printed here.

Five sources that are not on disk in full were looked at again today through archive.org's public full-text search of the scans the researchers used: *The Prolific and the Devourer*, *New Year Letter*, the 1947 *Intimate Journals*, Carpenter's biography and Ansen's *Table Talk*. Where a finding rests on that search, it says so.

19 findings: 1 must, 10 should, 8 could.

### week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2
- where: "Auden’s verdict is one sentence:"
- problem: The long quotation is right word for word, but it is not one sentence. In the book a comma follows its last word and the sentence runs on to give the ground, that Pascal's reason was what showed him the state of his heart; it ends over the page, on p. 48 (archive.org full-text search of the scan prolificdevourer00aude). So the period inside the closing quotation mark is not the source's, and "one sentence" is untrue. Three smaller things in the same paragraph: seven words in a row from "about Pascal as" are the source's; "by which he means" gives Auden a definition narrower than the book's, where dualists divide what exists into a good part and an evil part (God and Satan, body and soul, and so on); and "had less of" is weaker than the source, which says he lacked it.
- fix: "In the book Auden says that Pascal is to him what Montaigne was to Pascal. [The parenthesis on nos. 62 to 65 kept, with this sentence added inside it: The gloss is this site's.] He calls Pascal [quotation kept] of the dualists. A dualist, as the book uses the word, holds that what exists is divided into a good part and an evil part. Like most people, he says, Pascal prized what he lacked, the heart, over what he had, the reason. Auden's verdict begins [quotation kept, with the source's comma in place of the period] and goes on to the ground for it, that Pascal learned from his reason how corrupt his heart was."
- weight: must

## Patterns

1. Closeness: the source's own words for seven or more in a row, outside quotation marks. 6 entries, 7 runs. See week6.questions.1.auden.paras.1 (two runs, from Pearce) and week7.questions.1.auden.paras.5 (ten words). For comparison: of the eight runs of ten words or more that the first audit listed for these two weeks, seven are now under seven words and one is down to exactly seven (week7.questions.2.auden.paras.2).
2. A statement made stronger, broader, narrower or weaker than the source. 6 entries. See week6.questions.4.auden.paras.3 ("every division") and week7.reading.3.auden.paras.4 ("each turn").
3. Citation: pages too narrow, a page off, or missing. 5 entries. See week6.questions.1.auden.sources.2 and week6.questions.4.auden.sources.1. In one case a quotation has no citation at all (week6.questions.5.auden.paras.3).
4. Closeness one word under the limit: the sentence follows the source almost word for word, and only a single dropped, added or changed word keeps the longest run below seven. 3 findings: week6.questions.3.auden.paras.1, week7.questions.1.auden.paras.1, week6.questions.1.auden.paras.3. A check that allows for one such word finds the same thing in milder form in three more places, which I have not listed as findings: the second of the three reasons in week7.questions.2.auden.paras.3 (fa.txt 9654), the sentence that begins "He says in it" in week6.questions.5.auden.paras.1 (the introduction, p. 18), and six words in a row from "a Christian society" in week6.questions.3.auden.paras.3 (dh.txt 8304).
5. A detail changed in rewording. 2 entries. See week7.questions.1.auden.paras.4, where the rewording removed a long run that the first audit had listed (dh.txt 20801) and changed the sense in doing so, and week6.questions.5.auden.paras.1.
6. The site's own gloss standing unmarked inside a report of Auden. 2 entries. See week6.making.1.brief.1 and the parenthesis in week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2.
7. A quotation closed with a period where the source's sentence runs on, and called a sentence. 1 entry: week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2.

Seen in passing, outside this lens: house rule 7 keeps Auden's "one" out of the paraphrase, and it stands in three of these paragraphs outside quotation marks: week6.questions.2.auden.paras.2 ("whatever one suffers"), week6.questions.3.auden.paras.1 (in the finding above) and week7.questions.1.auden.paras.1 ("of one’s own").

Not counted as a fault: in five quotations the comma or period inside the closing quotation mark is the site's own, by American convention, where the source has a dash, a comma or no mark (week6.questions.4.auden.paras.4, week6.questions.5.auden.paras.2 and paras.3, week7.making.1.brief.2, and the one in pattern 7). Only the last misleads, because the text calls it a sentence. Also not counted: two runs of seven or eight words that are a name or two short words followed by a title (week6.questions.4.auden.paras.1, week7.reading.3.paras.3).
