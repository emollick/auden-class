### week9.intro.1

### week9.reading.2.auden.paras.3

## Patterns

1. A name with no word of introduction at its first mention this week (rule 11). Six names in five entries: Mitya and Alyosha, Grushenka, Violetta, Fenya, Andrey. See week9.intro.1 and week9.reading.2.auden.paras.1. The two test questions (items 4 and 6) can stand, since a test may assume the reading.
2. The verb of saying set into the middle of the sentence in one build ("X, he says, ..."). Six times in five entries, and twice in neighboring sentences in week9.reading.2.auden.paras.1. See also week9.questions.1.auden.paras.2. No separate finding. In the first of those, vary one, for example by opening the sentence about the playwright with "Auden grants that".
3. An instruction that does not say what I am to produce or what happens next. Five entries. See week9.reading.2.paras.1 and week9.questions.1.auden.paras.4.
4. A pointer with no clear owner, or with two ("it", "the difference", "those two lines", "the application"). Four entries. See week9.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week9.making.1.answer.after.2.
5. Order: the thing I need is not where I would look for it, or a note about the site's choices stands in front of it. Four entries. See week9.reading.2.paras.3 and week9.questions.2.auden.paras.4.
6. One thing in two forms, or one phrase for two things. Three cases: the two forms of the test references, "characters" and "people" for the same figures, and "the old man" for both the father and the servant. See week9.test.items.2.where and week9.words.setting.
