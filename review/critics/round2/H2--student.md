# H2 (week 10), read as the student

### week10.intro.2
- where: Rimbaud wrote it at eighteen
- problem: Rimbaud is never introduced in this week: no first name, and no word that he was a French poet. Rule 11 asks for it, and a student who knows little needs it here.
- fix: Begin the sentence "Arthur Rimbaud, the French poet, wrote it" and leave the rest as it stands.
- weight: should

### week10.reading.3.paras.2
- where: before Scene 7
- problem: The sentence gives the second act two scenes and then puts the overture before a Scene 7. A student cannot tell how an act of two scenes has a seventh.
- fix: In many performances an overture, *Leonore* No. 3, is played in the second act where the stage setting changes. The libretto printed here divides the act into more than two scenes, and the place is just before its Scene 7. (Editor to confirm how the printed libretto numbers its scenes.)
- weight: should

### week10.reading.3.paras.3
- where: after a French libretto
- problem: Coming straight after the words about a later revision, "after" reads as a matter of time, as if the revision followed the French libretto. The meaning is that the German words were based on the French ones.
- fix: The words are by Joseph Sonnleithner, who worked from a French libretto by Jean-Nicolas Bouilly, and Georg Friedrich Treitschke revised them later.
- weight: should

### week10.reading.3.paras.3
- where: the scenes between them
- problem: "Numbers" is an opera term that is not explained. "Scenes" now has a third meaning in three paragraphs: the two scenes of the act, the libretto's Scene 7, and here the spoken dialogue.
- fix: The arias and ensembles are sung in German, and the dialogue between them is spoken.
- weight: should

### week10.questions.1.auden.paras.3

### week10.questions.2.auden.paras.2
- where: reports what she heard

- fix: Dorothy Farnan, a graduate student at Michigan in 1941, was not in the class. In *Auden in Love*, her book of 1984, she reports what those who were in it told her. The sentence on memory work can then begin "She says" as it does now.

### week10.questions.2.auden.paras.4

### week10.questions.3.auden.paras.1

### week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2
- where: as their authors projected in imagination
- problem: "Projected" can be taken as a verb or as a description, and neither reading is plain. The half after the colon is passive, so a student cannot tell who learns what the deed feels like. The rest of the paragraph depends on this sentence.
- fix: Auden takes three heroes, Faust, Don Juan and Captain Ahab of *Moby-Dick* (week 11), to be figures in whom their authors pictured themselves. A hero of this kind, he says, acts less for the sake of the deed than to find out how doing it feels. (Editor to check my reading of the first clause against the lectures.)
- weight: must

### week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2

- fix: Artists of this kind, Auden says, experiment on themselves, so that each is both the subject and the recorder of the experiment. The clause on excitements and terrors then stands as a sentence of its own, beginning "They knew". (Editor to check "experiment on themselves" against the lectures.)
- weight: should

## Patterns

1. A name, a date or a work missing where the student needs it (5): week10.intro.2, week10.questions.2.auden.paras.2. Also questions.2.auden.paras.3, questions.3.auden.paras.4 and making.1.source.1.
2. Accepted-answer lists that may refuse a right answer, if the program wants an exact match (5): week10.test.items.6.a, week10.test.items.12.a. Also items 4, 11 and 14.
3. A sentence that can be parsed two ways, most often because a phrase attaches to the wrong word (4): week10.questions.3.auden.paras.1, week10.questions.3.auden.paras.4. Also reading.3.paras.3 and questions.3.auden.paras.2.
4. A report of Auden that leaves its key term, or its link to what the student was asked, unexplained (4): week10.questions.1.auden.paras.3, week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2. Also reading.3.auden.paras.1 and questions.1.auden.paras.4.
5. Rule 12, "he" or "his" for the artist or poet in general (3): week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2, week10.questions.3.auden.paras.3. The third is "his own choice" in questions.3.auden.paras.4, which the fix given there removes.
6. A pointer with no owner (3): week10.questions.2.auden.paras.4 ("the same"), week10.questions.3.auden.paras.2 ("the experiment"). Also "either" in reading.3.auden.paras.3.
7. One thing under two names or in two forms (3): week10.reading.3.paras.3 ("scenes"), week10.test.items.2.where. Also the overture's name in reading.3.auden.paras.1.
