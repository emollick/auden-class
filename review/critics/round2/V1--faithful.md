# V1, faithfulness to sources: weeks 1 to 3

Second, independent check of every statement in part V1 that reports what Auden, Donald Pearce or another witness wrote, said or did, and of the facts about the required books stated in those paragraphs. No source text and no quotation from the site is printed here. Evidence is a file name and line numbers; for `pearce.txt` it is the line and character offset. In a fix, [quotation kept] stands for a quotation the fix keeps and [title kept] for an essay title in quotation marks that it keeps.

All nine quotations in the part match their scans word for word. The findings are about paraphrase, closeness and citation.

## Findings, in the order of the text

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1
- where: He names *Orpheus* in two pieces of 1948
- problem: Only the Greek Reader introduction is shown to name the opera (fa.txt 416–417). The one passage on record from the *Vogue* piece names the composer, Gluck, as the point from which the form grew without a break (C_auden_on_works.md 650–657 and 1357); its full text could not be read, but a search of the Prose II scan for the opera's title returns four passages and none is in that piece.
- fix: "He names Gluck in two pieces of 1948, both times as the point where a period starts. Only one of them, [title kept], gives the title *Orpheus*. That essay says ..."
- weight: should

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1
- where: says that no Greek work is as great as
- problem: The source limits the claim to a single work of literature (fa.txt 410), and it gives the span of Athenian drama as about seventy-five years, where the site makes the figure exact (fa.txt 414).
- fix: "says that no single Greek work of literature is as great as the *Divine Comedy*"; and in the next sentence "the seventy-five or so years of Athenian drama".
- weight: should

### week1.words.after.1
- where: the speech to the crew repeats what the Serpent said
- problem: The source says that the speech echoes the Serpent's words (ef.txt 505–506). That claims a likeness, and "repeats" claims the same words.
- fix: for "repeats" put "echoes".
- weight: should

### week1.questions.1.auden.paras.1
- where: Auden wrote no essay on the *Comedy* itself
- problem: The notes support only that none was found (C_auden_on_works.md 53), and they record a review by Auden of a Dante volume in 1947, mostly about translations (line 61). The flat negative goes past the evidence; the opera paragraph of this same week uses the safer form.
- fix: for the first clause put "No essay by Auden on the *Comedy* itself has been found," and keep the sentence as it is from "but he touches" on.
- weight: should

### week1.questions.1.auden.paras.2
- where: the way God appears to someone who denies him
- problem: In the source the thing denied is the relation to God, and "deny" is paired with a second verb, "distort" (fa.txt 8435–8437). "Denies him" reads as disbelief, which the passage does not say and which does not fit the damned in Dante.
- fix: "but the way God appears to anyone who twists or denies that relation." The site's own reading at the end of paras.3 would then end "without ceasing to deny that relation."
- weight: should

### week1.questions.2.auden.paras.1
- where: goes wrong in three ways
- problem: Dante's line says that the chosen love may err, which is a possibility and not a rule (the site's copy, dante-longfellow pur-17, block 31; Auden gives the Italian at dh.txt 6280–6282).
- fix: "can go wrong in three ways"
- weight: could

### week2.questions.1.auden.paras.2
- where: more suffering than those citizens will ever know
- problem: The source says only that by the end the hero suffers more than the chorus does (fa.txt 942–943). What the citizens will ever know is the site's addition.
- fix: end the sentence "and ends by suffering more than they do."
- weight: could

### week2.questions.1.auden.paras.3
- where: the confidence of the fortunate that they cannot be made
- problem: Both of Auden's definitions say that the hero believes he is a god, or godlike, and for that reason beyond suffering (fa.txt 957–959 and 1032–1033). The paraphrase keeps the immunity and drops the belief that explains it.
- fix: after "that they" insert "are like gods and".
- weight: could

### week2.questions.2.auden.paras.2
- where: in the Greek stories of how the world began
- problem: The source cites one Greek cosmology beside Genesis (ef.txt 335–337). The paraphrase makes it the Greek stories in general.
- fix: "in one Greek story of how the world began"
- weight: should

### week2.questions.2.auden.paras.4
- where: leaving the land is what a person of spirit does
- problem: The source makes it the wish of everyone who has sensibility and honor to get away from land and city (ef.txt 574–575). The paraphrase turns a wish into an act, puts "spirit" for the two qualities, and drops the city.
- fix: "and then about the Romantics, for whom every person of feeling and honor longs to get away from land and city."
- weight: should

### week2.making.1.brief.1
- where: from whichever of German, French and Latin the student had
- problem: Chapman says that each student picked, from the three, a language not yet studied (his p. 509, in A_gb_mqr3.txt lines 5 and 9), and Carpenter follows him (B_pedagogy.md 87). "Whichever" removes the choice and implies that each student lacked exactly one of the three.
- fix: "At Michigan Auden had each student choose a language the student had not studied, out of German, French and Latin, and translate a poem from it with a dictionary as the only help."
- weight: should

### week2.making.1.source.1
- where: Chapman says that he took a poem of Rilke’s
- problem: Neither Chapman nor Carpenter says in which of Auden's two Michigan courses the translation was assigned, and the researcher judged that it probably belonged to the other course (A_michigan_course.md 336 and 405). The note says only that Chapman took both, so a reader will take the exercise as documented for this course.
- fix: add after that sentence: "He does not say in which of the two courses the translation was assigned."
- weight: should

### week2.making.2.source.1
- where: No source says that Auden assigned this exercise at Michigan
- problem: This is true of terza rima, but the notes record a nearer witness than the essay of 1962: Angelyn Stevens, who attended both courses, listed verse written to a given meter among the work (A_michigan_course.md 400, from Farnan, p. 73; see also line 336).
- fix: flag only. The editor must decide whether to narrow the sentence to "No source says that Auden assigned terza rima at Michigan" and to add Stevens's report with its citation.
- weight: could

### week3.words.after.1
- where: odd or nonsense
- problem: Seven words in a row, beginning with these three, are Eberhart's own as Carpenter prints them on p. 264, and they stand outside quotation marks (found by phrase search in the archive.org scan whaudenbiography0000carp). The rest of the sentence is in the site's words.
- fix: "put strange or meaningless ones in the gaps"
- weight: should

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.2
- where: between the self, which is mind and body together
- problem: Auden's two terms are flesh and spirit, and he defines flesh as all of a person's bodily and mental powers in their enslaved, self-loving state (fa.txt 1769–1773). "The self" is not his word, and with it the pairing is lost.
- fix: from the where-words to the end of the sentence: "between the flesh, by which he means mind and body together in their self-loving state, and the spirit, which bears witness to what a person was meant to be."
- weight: should

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.3
- where: when a Christian
- problem: Eight words in a row, beginning with these three, follow the review word for word outside quotation marks (fa.txt 1775).
- fix: "From this division the review argues that a Christian such as Augustine, writing on ethics, starts from the act that is neither reasonable nor pleasant ..."
- weight: should

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.3
- where: People act, Auden says, either
- problem: In the source each of the two phrases is tied to a motive, the first to love of self and the second to love of God (fa.txt 1799–1800). Without the motives the sentence loses its link to the self-loving flesh of the paragraph before.
- fix: "People act, Auden says, either out of love of self, [quotation kept], or out of love of God, [quotation kept]"
- weight: could

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.4
- where: took notes at the lecture on Augustine, and they survive
- problem: Pearce says that notes were forbidden, that he wrote his down in a nearby room after each lecture, and that what he prints are fragments of a lecture on the ego in Augustine and Kafka (pearce.txt line 3, chars 2978–3990, his p. 134); the site's about page reports this correctly, so the two pages now disagree. The fragments also do not name the *Confessions*: they speak of Augustine's account of a conflict within the self (chars 3990–4964).
- fix: first sentence: "Note-taking was forbidden in the class. Donald Pearce, one of the students, wrote down what he could after each lecture, and he prints fragments from one on Augustine and Franz Kafka, whose novel *The Castle* is assigned in weeks 14 and 15." In the second sentence, for "the *Confessions* matter" put "Augustine's account of the divided self matters", and for "the book" put "it".
- weight: must

### week3.questions.1.auden.paras.5
- where: *Confessions*, in the
- problem: Eight words in a row, beginning with these three, follow the reconstructed lecture word for word outside quotation marks. The sentence is recorded at C_auden_on_works.md 170, and a phrase search of the archive.org scan lecturesonshakes00aude finds the run unbroken.
- fix: "first appears in the *Confessions*, where Augustine tells of robbing the pear tree."
- weight: should

### week3.questions.1.auden.sources.2

A second capture, A_tr_out1.txt, has the markers in the same places, and A_michigan_course.md 509 gives p. 134.

- weight: should

### week3.questions.2.auden.paras.2
- where: the ancients thought of time as a pendulum or a
- problem: The source qualifies the statement: it holds of the ancients by and large (dh.txt 6768). The paraphrase makes it a rule without exception.

### week3.making.1.brief.1
- where: to describe the events of the day before in reverse
- problem: Spears (p. 4, read in swat_spears.pdf) and the library exhibit page (swat_prof.html) say only the events of the day, and Mandelbaum, who taught the course with Auden, describes an account of the student's own day up to the class (B_pedagogy.md 195–197). "The day before" is Carpenter's version alone, as the site's source note says, yet the brief states it as the documented assignment.
- fix: "One of his composition assignments there was to set down a day's events in reverse order." Then add to the last sentence of the source note: ", and the exercise here follows him."
- weight: should
