Second, independent check of every entry in `parts/V6.md` that reports what Auden or Donald Pearce wrote, said or did. No sentence of a source is written out here and none of the site's quotations is repeated. Evidence is a file name and line numbers (for `pearce.txt`, line and character offset, as `L3@n` or `L5@n`). ## Patterns

1. A detail changed in the rewording (7): the term, the place, the date or a name is not the source's. See week15.questions.1.auden.paras.5 and week15.questions.4.auden.paras.3; also week14.questions.2.auden.paras.3, week15.questions.3.auden.paras.2, week15.questions.3.auden.paras.4, week15.questions.4.auden.paras.2 and week15.reading.3.auden.paras.1.
2. An opinion given to Auden as his own when he credits it to someone else, or when it is the site's inference or gloss (5). See week15.reading.3.auden.paras.4 and week15.questions.1.auden.paras.2; also week14.questions.1.auden.paras.3, week14.questions.2.auden.paras.4 and week15.questions.2.gloss.1.
3. A statement made firmer or broader than the source, or given a detail the source lacks (5). See week15.reading.3.auden.paras.3 and week15.questions.2.auden.paras.3; also week14.questions.1.auden.paras.1, week14.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week15.questions.1.auden.paras.1.
4. The source's own wording kept outside quotation marks (4). Seven words or more in a row in week14.questions.3.auden.paras.2, week15.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week15.questions.3.auden.paras.2; a near-run in week15.questions.3.auden.paras.3. One more is just under the mark and is not listed as a finding: in week15.questions.2.auden.paras.1 the seven words before the quotation are Muir's, with one of them shortened.
5. Citation slips (2): week15.reading.3.auden.sources.2 and week15.questions.3.auden.sources.2.

Not counted as findings, for the editor to decide once: in 13 quotations the last mark inside the closing quotation mark is the site's and not the source's. Every word matches. The site ends its own sentence or clause there, and the source runs on or stops differently. A period where the source has a comma, a dash, a parenthesis or no stop: week14.questions.1.auden.paras.3, week14.questions.2.auden.paras.2, week14.questions.4.auden.paras.2, week15.reading.3.auden.paras.2, week15.reading.3.auden.paras.4 (both quotations), week15.questions.1.auden.paras.2 (the second), week15.questions.1.auden.paras.6 (the last), week15.questions.3.auden.paras.3 (the second), week15.questions.4.auden.paras.2 (the second). A comma where the source has a period: week14.questions.2.auden.paras.3, week15.questions.2.auden.paras.1, week15.questions.2.auden.paras.3 (the third). This is the American way of setting a stop inside the quotation marks. If the rule is letter for letter with punctuation, the stop belongs outside.
