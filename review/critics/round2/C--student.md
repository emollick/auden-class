# Part C, read as the student who has to use it (round 2)

### work:dante.copy
- where: the prose translation of the Temple Classics
- problem: The Temple Classics are never said to be a series of books, and the cell then speaks of the Temple volumes and the Temple editor. A student cannot tell whether the name is a publisher, a series or a title.
- fix: Open the cell with: "The Modern Library volume reprinted the prose translation that J. M. Dent of London published in its Temple Classics series (1899–1901):" and keep the three translators as they stand.
- weight: should

### work:dante.copy
- where: is open to read, and any change made for
- problem: The sentence has to be read twice. "Open to read" is not the usual phrase for a scan that is free online, and the passive at the end hides who does not know. This is the text the student learns word for word, so the point should be plain: the reprint may differ, and the site could not compare the two.
- fix: "The text here comes from the Temple volumes. No scan of the Modern Library reprint can be read free online, so this site could not check whether the reprint changed any wording."
- weight: should

### work:dante.copy
- where: the Italian are printed with it
- problem: The sentence before is about the *Inferno* alone, so "it" seems to mean Carlyle's part. A student could take it that the other two parts have no Longfellow and no Italian.
- fix: "All three parts can also be read in Longfellow's verse translation and in Italian."
- weight: should

### work:agamemnon.find
- where: which is in copyright. It can be
- problem: The entry offers a loan without saying what it is for. A student who means to do the work cannot tell whether MacNeice is wanted or whether the week's test fits it. The Kierkegaard entry answers that question for its book, and this one does not. The same is true of work:antigone.find.
- fix: Keep the first sentence and replace the second with: "You do not need it, because the week's work is made from the translation printed here. To read the play as the class read it, [borrow MacNeice's translation from the Internet Archive](same address)." Do the same for *Antigone*.
- weight: could

### work:pascal.copy
- where: The Everyman volume is W. F.
- problem: Every other translation printed from the required list carries its year in this table, and this one has none. A student cannot tell how old the English is.
- fix: flag only. Add the year after the translator's name, in the form the other cells use. The fact is missing here: the year Trotter's translation was first published, which the site gives nowhere.
- weight: could

### work:kierkegaard.textLabels.1
- where: Hollander, abridged
- problem: The two notes call this text a selection and the reader's head says selections. "Abridged" is a third name, and it suggests a whole book cut down, which this text is not.
- fix: "Hollander, selections"
- weight: could

### work:baudelaire.copy
- where: he leaves out some pages of both journals
- problem: Nothing has said that the book is two journals, so "both" has nothing to point to. The cell also leaves open whether the week's work needs the missing pages, a question the Kierkegaard and *Don Giovanni* cells settle for their books.
- fix: Replace the last sentence with: "Shipley calls the book *Intimate Papers*. It is made up of two journals, *Rockets* and *My Heart Laid Bare*, and he omits some pages of each. No test or question depends on the omitted pages." The last of these needs a fact I do not have: the editor must confirm it against week 6 or leave it off.
- weight: should

### work:baudelaire.find
- where: in which an introduction by Auden replaces
- problem: On the week's page this entry stands without the table, and nothing in it has said that the 1930 edition had an introduction or who Eliot is. The reader meets a bare surname and has to supply the noun.
- fix: "The list names the Random House edition of 1930. Christopher Isherwood translated it, and T. S. Eliot wrote the introduction. A later edition (Marcel Rodd, 1947) has the same translation with an introduction by Auden in Eliot's place, and it can be [borrowed from the Internet Archive](same address)."
- weight: should

### work:rimbaud.copy
- where: It leaves the poems that Rimbaud quotes from himself
- problem: The sentence can be taken two ways until its last word, because the closing phrase attaches to "quotes" as easily as to "leaves". It also assumes the student knows that Rimbaud quotes his own verse in this book. In the sentence before, *The Dial* is named without a word on what it is.
- fix: After *The Dial* add ", an American magazine,". Replace the third sentence with: "One chapter quotes several of Rimbaud's own poems, and the translator left them in French."
- weight: must

### work:family-reunion.find
- where: borrow it from the Internet Archive
- problem: This is the one required book the student must get from outside, and the instruction hides a step. The Archive lends this scan only to a reader who is signed in, and signing in needs a free account. The other four loan links (*Agamemnon*, *Antigone*, Kierkegaard, Baudelaire) lead to the same kind of loan.
- fix: Keep the first sentence. Replace the second with: "Buy a copy or find one in a library. You can also [borrow it from the Internet Archive](same address), where a loan needs a free account." If the loan has a time limit worth stating, the editor must look it up. I checked only that all five scans are still offered for lending.
- weight: should

### work:magic-flute.copy
- where: is cut or sung as recitative
- problem: "Recitative" is an opera term that the site explains nowhere, and the week 5 page takes care to say the same thing in plain words. A student new to opera is stopped by it.
- fix: "It follows the Italian version of the opera, which cuts the spoken scenes or has them sung."
- weight: should

### work:carmen.copy
- where: with sung recitatives in place of the spoken dialogue
- problem: The same unexplained term. The week 8 page says the thing plainly.
- fix: "In this libretto the scenes between the songs, which were spoken at the first performance, are sung."
- weight: should

### text:agamemnon.headings
- where: 1. The Watchman; the Elders
- problem: The translation has no such parts, and nothing says that the six parts and their headings are the site's own. A student who cites part 4 in a paper should know that the numbering exists only here. The same holds for text:antigone.headings.
- fix: Add one line at the head of each play: "The play has no acts. The division into six parts, and the headings, are this site's." (Five parts for *Antigone*.)
- weight: could

### text:carmen
- where: Text: Internet Archive.
- problem: The Sources page says that each book read by machine links to its scan. In this part only the Dante note carries links. The other eight heads (*Carmen*, *Don Giovanni*, *Fidelio*, *The Magic Flute*, *Orpheus*, Rilke, Rimbaud, *La Traviata*) name the Internet Archive and show no address, so a student who doubts a word has nowhere to check it.
- fix: flag only. If the program does not already make the source name a link, link it to the scan in all eight, or change the sentence on the Sources page.
- weight: should

### text:carmen.note
- where: with sung recitatives in place of the spoken dialogue
- problem: The unexplained opera term again, here at the head of the text where the student needs the plain statement.
- fix: "In this libretto the scenes between the songs are sung, so a recording that has them spoken will not match the words."
- weight: should

### text:carmen.note
- where: read by machine from a scan and may contain
- problem: The Sources page says that six of these texts were afterward read through against the pictures of the pages, and that *Fidelio* and the Rilke were corrected only where the reading was in doubt. This closing sentence stands word for word in four of the six notes and with small changes in the other two. None of them mentions the reading through, and *Fidelio* carries the same sentence. The two pages tell the student different things about how far to trust the text.
- fix: flag only, since the editor must say which account is right. If the Sources page is right, the six notes (*Carmen*, *Don Giovanni*, *The Magic Flute*, *Orpheus*, Rimbaud, *La Traviata*) could end: "The text was read by machine from a scan, then read through against the pictures of the pages. A misread word may still remain."
- weight: should

### text:dante-temple
- where: from scans at the Internet Archive of the printings
- problem: The source line says what the first sentence of the note under it says again with the links, so the student reads the printings twice. The three titles in the line are also without italics, against rule 9. The Rilke head repeats its note in the same way, in fewer words.
- fix: "Text: The Temple Classics (J. M. Dent, London)." Leave the scans and the printings to the note.
- weight: should

### text:dante-temple.note
- where: the four set for the examination among them
- problem: Every other page gives the examination as five cantos, so "the four" reads as a second count until the student recalls that the fifth is chosen. "Set" is also the British classroom word that house rule 4 excludes. The Sources page has the same phrase.
- fix: "Twelve cantos were then read through against the pages. They include the four that every student writes out at the examination."
- weight: must

### text:dante-temple.note
- where: with an argument and notes to each canto
- problem: "Argument" is the old word for a summary printed before a canto, and a student will take it in its everyday sense. The end of the same sentence says only the English is given, yet the reader offers the Italian as a version and the reading list says the Italian is printed with this translation.
- fix: "The volumes print the Italian opposite the English and give each canto a summary and notes. Only the English is taken from them here, and the Italian offered under Versions comes from another edition."
- weight: should

### text:fidelio.note
- where: the library that owns this copy dates it 1903
- problem: No copy has been mentioned when "this copy" arrives, since the scan comes up only in the next sentence. The closing sentence is also the one used for the texts that were read through, though the Sources page says this one was corrected only where the reading was in doubt.
- fix: "It gives neither translator nor date. The library that owns the scanned copy dates it 1903. The text was read by machine from that scan and corrected against the pictures of the pages only where a word looked wrong, so some misread words may remain."
- weight: should

### text:horace
- where: The Odes and Carmen Saeculare
- problem: Two things a student meets in this text are explained only on the reading-list page or nowhere. Four odes turn up in Latin with no English, and the head does not say why. The week 2 page calls the last poem the Secular Hymn, while the head gives only its Latin name, which also contains the name of an opera on the list.
- fix: Add a note at the head: "Conington did not translate four of the odes, and they stand here in Latin only. The poem named second in the title is the one the week 2 page calls the Secular Hymn." If the reader already explains the four odes where they occur, only the second sentence is needed.
- weight: should

### text:magic-flute.note
- where: the dialogue is cut or sung as recitative
- problem: The unexplained term again. The note also stops short of what the difference means for the listener, which the *Carmen* note does say.
- fix: "This libretto follows the Italian version that London and New York heard in the nineteenth century, which cuts the spoken scenes or has them sung. A recording in German will have speech that is not in this text."
- weight: should

### text:poems.note
- where: impression of 1931
- problem: The sentence before calls the same thing a printing, and "impression" is the British book-trade word. One thing has two names inside one note.
- fix: "printing of 1931"
- weight: could

### text:rimbaud.note
- where: It leaves the poems that Rimbaud quotes from himself
- problem: The same two-way sentence as in the reading-list table. Here it stands just before the student meets pages of French verse inside an English text, so it is the one sentence that has to be clear.
- fix: "One chapter quotes several of Rimbaud's own poems, and the translator left them in French."
- weight: must

### text:tristan.note
- where: Those two are taken here from Fred
- problem: This note is shown by itself, and it is the only opera note that names Rullman without saying who he is.
- fix: "Those two are taken here from the libretto of the same translation that Fred Rullman of New York published."
- weight: should

## Patterns

1. Notes on the machine-read texts that do not agree with the Sources page: 8 heads without a visible link to the scan, and 7 notes whose closing sentence says less than the Sources page or the wrong thing. See text:carmen.note and text:fidelio.note.
2. A name or a fact used as if the student already had it, when it was given on another page or nowhere: 7. See work:baudelaire.find (Eliot's introduction) and text:tristan.note (Rullman). The others are work:dante.copy, work:baudelaire.copy, work:rimbaud.copy, text:horace and text:dante-temple.note.
3. A loan link that leaves a step or its purpose unsaid: 5 links. See work:family-reunion.find and work:agamemnon.find.
4. The opera term "recitative" with no explanation, where the week pages use plain words: 4. See work:magic-flute.copy and text:carmen.note.
5. A count or a name that differs from the rest of the site: 4. See text:dante-temple.note (four cantos against five) and work:kierkegaard.textLabels.1. The others are text:poems.note and the second finding on text:dante-temple.note.
6. One sentence that reads two ways, used twice: 2. See work:rimbaud.copy and text:rimbaud.note.
7. A pointer with a doubtful owner: 2. See work:dante.copy and text:fidelio.note.

The titles, authors, edition names and version labels, and the cells for the eighteen books of criticism, gave a student no trouble apart from what is listed above.
