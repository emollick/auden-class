# Part B, read for machine-made writing (round 2)

Part B has none of the gross marks: no hype, no hedging, no verb of saying in every sentence. What remains is joinery: a pointer with no noun, a statement made in a paragraph and again in the table under it, a short sentence left standing for the beat. House rule 1 is kept throughout: no em-dash, no exclamation mark, no rhetorical question.

## Page: syllabus

### page:syllabus.intro.1
- where: The standard bibliography of Auden describes it
- problem: The book is leaned on here and again in the next paragraph and is never named, so "standard" has to be taken on trust. The Sources page names it; this page should too, once.
- fix: The standard bibliography of Auden, by Bloomfield and Mendelson, describes it as two sheets.
- weight: could

### page:syllabus.intro.3
- where: the edition the list names, where it names one,
- problem: The aside falls inside a comma series, so the reader counts it as the second of four items and has to go back. It looks like a qualifier added in revision.
- fix: For every required book and opera they give the edition (where the list names one), the text this site prints, and the week you read it in.
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1
- where: This site prints both books. It leaves them out
- problem: The point is a contrast (printed, but not required) and it has been split into two flat sentences, the first of them five words long. The tail then seems to say the same thing twice, because a newcomer does not yet know that the tests and the questions are different work. The second sentence of the entry also has "One is ... read one of them," an echo that tells the student what a choice is.
- fix: Second sentence: "One entry lets you choose between *Agamemnon* and *Antigone*." Last two sentences: "This site prints both books but does not require them. It leaves them out of the weekly page count and the tests, and you never have to write a paper on either."
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.2
- where: Most of those printed here come from the libretti
- problem: "Those" are libretti, so the sentence says that libretti come from libretti. The five-word sentence after it ("This site prints the English") can be folded in.
- fix: Most of the texts printed here are the English versions from the bilingual editions that Fred Rullman of New York published.
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.1
- where: No work on this site depends on them
- problem: "Work" can be a book or an assignment, and this page is a list of books. Read the first way the sentence is nonsense; the student has to try both.
- fix: Nothing assigned on this site depends on them, and none of them is printed here.
- weight: should

### page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.2
- where: and stops at the bottom of the first sheet
- problem: The top of the page has already said the picture is of the first sheet, so a picture of that sheet cannot do other than stop there; the fact meant is that the sheet ends after the eighth book. The paragraph then closes on a four-word sentence ("Both are corrected here") whose "Both" has to be worked out.
- fix: First sentence: "The first sheet ends after the eighth of them, so the published picture shows only those eight." Last two sentences: "Three of the authors' names are wrong on the list, and Miller's copy adds slips of its own; this site corrects both."
- weight: should

## Page: about

### page:about.title
- where: What is documented, and what is not
- problem: The comma and the mirrored second half are a stock heading shape. It is also inexact: the page does not list what is undocumented, it lists what the site supplied, and its own section headings say so.
- fix: What is on record and what this site supplied
- weight: could

### page:about.lede
- where: The rest of this site is reconstruction.
- problem: "The rest" has nothing to be the rest of: the sentence before is about what is known, not about a part of the site. The short verdict is also placed last for weight.
- fix: Everything on this site that does not come from them is the site's reconstruction.
- weight: could

### page:about.sections.1.paras.1
- where: They are cited below by surname and page
- problem: "They" follows a sentence about one man, Carpenter, and is meant to reach back over eight names, one of whom (Millar) is not cited at all. The pointer has come loose from its noun.
- fix: The accounts are cited below by the author's surname and the page, and the full references are listed under Works cited at the bottom of the page.
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.1.table.rows.6.2
- where: The two do not agree
- problem: The reader has just been shown two sets of days and hours and can see that they differ. The sentence explains the obvious, and "the two" is loose, since three sources have been named.
- fix: cut
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.1.table.rows.7.2
- where: (known here only from a search summary)
- problem: "Search summary" is the builder's working term and tells a student nothing about how far to trust the figure. It reads as a note to self left in the table.
- fix: Chapman is reported to give an enrollment of twenty-two; this site could not read that page of his article. (Flag: the editor must confirm that this is what the parenthesis means.)
- weight: must

### page:about.sections.1.table.rows.18.1
- where: each choosing a language he or she
- problem: "He or she" breaks house rule 12 and is the one stiff phrase in the table. In the next row "He" then has to mean Auden again.
- fix: Replace the last clause with: "each working from a language they had not studied"
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.2.paras.1
- where: A committee of students called it impossible.
- problem: The sentence before has "The cantos" for its subject, so "it" has no owner in reach. The third sentence of the paragraph is also three statements joined by "and ... and ... which," and its opening "It" comes straight after "what it would be."
- fix: Third sentence, as two: "The examination would last three hours and would come after the Christmas vacation. The class would write out, from memory, seven cantos in the prose translation of Carlyle, Okey and Wicksteed, the Modern Library volume named on the list." Then: "A committee of students called the task impossible."
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.2.paras.3
- where: claimed to have eight cantos by heart still
- problem: "Still" trailing at the end of the sentence is a mannered word order. In the last sentence "mark her down" is the British classroom verb; house rule 4 wants "grade."
- fix: "Some of them claimed that they still had eight cantos by heart." Last clause of the paragraph: "and did not lower her grade."
- weight: could

### page:about.sections.2.paras.4
- where: He names four of the five. You choose
- problem: The paragraph says that Pearce alone names the cantos and in the next breath takes it back to four of five, which reads as a patch. It then ends on two short sentences in step, the second a fact that section 5 states again.
- fix: Replace the last three sentences with: "This site follows Pearce, the only one to name the translation, the length of the examination and four of the five cantos. The fifth is your own choice, made on the [By heart] page."
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.3.paras.1
- where: and neither of the books marked
- problem: "With ... neither of the books" asks a list of things included to carry a thing left out, and the "that" which follows has no noun to point to. The same three-part tally comes back twice in section 6.
- fix: Counting one Greek play and the nine libretti, and leaving out the two books marked [quotation kept], the reading comes to about 280 pages a week.
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.4.paras.1
- where: Two are not. He drew charts
- problem: "Two are not" is a three-word sentence staged before the explanation. The two sentences after it are then said again in the table directly below (rows 13 and 14), the one about charts almost word for word. The second sentence is also slack: "kinds of work ... are ones that."
- fix: Keep the first sentence and the last. Between them: "Auden is known to have given a class most of the kinds of work on this site, at Michigan or elsewhere, and the table says where. The two exceptions are the charts and the parody, and the table explains each."
- weight: must

### page:about.sections.4.table.rows.4.3
- where: Six of the questions set here
- problem: "Set" is the British classroom verb. House rule 4 has questions assigned or put to the class.
- fix: Six of the questions assigned here come from Auden.
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.5.list.7
- where: These paragraphs report, in this site’s words
- problem: "These paragraphs" points at a noun that has not been used; the item opens with "What you are shown." A swapped opening has left the pointer behind.
- fix: Open the item "The paragraphs you are shown after you hand in a paper." and begin the next sentence "They report".
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.5.list.8
- where: that he did not apply it to himself
- problem: Two "to"s and a "himself" that could belong to either verb make the clause hard to parse, and "the application is this site's" is a formula where two plain words would do. "Explanations of Auden" is also vague: the site explains his ideas, not him.
- fix: Explanations of Auden's ideas. Where a paragraph applies an idea of his to a book he did not apply it to, the paragraph says so.
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.6.paras.1
- where: The earliest uses this site found are of
- problem: Four sentences in a row open with "The," two of them short and flat, and "are of February 2013" is not idiomatic. The staccato draws attention to itself.
- fix: Join the third and fourth sentences. Open "The earliest uses of it that this site found date from February 2013, when the books blog" and run on into what is now the fourth; change ", and the website" to "and the website"; drop "the same month."
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.6.paras.3
- where: one Greek play, neither of the books marked
- problem: After the colon the reader expects what the 4,240 pages consist of and is given one play, nine libretti and an absence. It is the tally of section 3 again, in the same awkward form.
- fix: The term as laid out here comes to 4,240 pages. It has one Greek play and the nine libretti and leaves out both books marked [quotation kept].
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.7.paras.1
- where: and the reading list says which
- problem: On this page "the reading list" has meant Auden's mimeographed list since the first line, and that list cannot say which translation the site prints. The page of the site is meant.
- fix: another translation is printed, and the table on the reading-list page says which.
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.7.paras.3
- where: Nine were read by machine from scans
- problem: "Nine" opens a paragraph with no noun to count. The same images are then called scans, pictures of the pages, the pictures, the page and the scan, so the reader cannot tell whether one thing or several is meant. "Set for the examination" is the British verb again.
- fix: Open "Nine of the texts were read by machine". Keep one word for the images: write "against the scans" in both places, and "the scan was checked wherever they differed". Write "the four required for the examination".
- weight: should

### page:about.sections.8.sources.14
- where: gives the examination question and the two exercises
- problem: "The two exercises" points at nothing in this entry; the reader has to go back through the table to find which two cite the exhibit.
- fix: Its page [title kept] gives the examination question, the poems with words left out and the day written backward.
- weight: could

### page:about.sections.8.sources.17
- where: (pp. 465–466) and does not print it
- problem: The sense is a contrast and it has been joined with "and." The same flattening is at page:syllabus.intro.2, where the list has no year "and" the bibliography supplies one.
- fix: Change "and does not print it" to "but does not print it".
- weight: could

### page:about.sections.9.paras.1
- where: names the source so that you can check it
- problem: The paragraph has four "it"s and the last one changes owner, from the site to the fact. It also makes the page end on an echo of "checked it" two lines above.
- fix: End the sentence "so that you can check the fact yourself."
- weight: could

## Patterns

1. A pointer with no clear owner (house rule 10): 11. "They," "it" twice, "that," "The two," "These paragraphs," "Nine," "Both," "The rest," "the two exercises," "the reading list" used for the site's page. See page:about.sections.1.paras.1 and page:about.sections.5.list.7. Several look like the residue of a revision that changed the noun and left the pronoun.
2. A short sentence staged before or after the real statement: 8. See page:about.sections.4.paras.1 ("Two are not") and page:about.sections.1.table.rows.6.2. Others: the last sentence of page:about.lede, of page:syllabus.kinds.critical.intro.2 and of page:about.sections.2.paras.4; the five-word sentences in page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1 and page:syllabus.kinds.opera.intro.2; the run of short openers in page:about.sections.6.paras.1.
3. The same statement made in two or three places: 5. The charts and the parody are explained in page:about.sections.4.paras.1 and again in rows 13 and 14 of the table under it. The page tally (one Greek play, the libretti, the two marked books) is given in page:about.sections.3.paras.1 and twice more in section 6. That you choose the fifth canto is said at sections.2.paras.4 and at sections.5.list.5. The six questions that come from Auden are counted in sections.4.paras.1, table row 4 and sections.5.list.3; I have left that one alone, since a summary list may repeat.
4. British classroom words and "he or she" (house rules 4 and 12): 4. "Set" at page:about.sections.4.table.rows.4.3 and page:about.sections.7.paras.3; "mark her down" at sections.2.paras.3; "he or she" at table.rows.18.1.
5. A contrast flattened into "and" or a full stop, where the house rules want "but": 3. See page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1 and page:about.sections.8.sources.17.
6. The builder's working words left in: 2. "Search summary" at page:about.sections.1.table.rows.7.2; scans called by five names at page:about.sections.7.paras.3.

Looked for and not found as faults: a verb of saying in every sentence; reflex groups of three (the triples here are real lists); hedging; hype; a formula closing block after block. "This site" appears 20 times in the part, and on two pages whose job is to separate the record from what was supplied each use but one (sections.5.list.8) earns its place. "The list" opens five of the nine paragraphs on the syllabus page; the paragraphs stand under separate headings and I have not asked for a change.
