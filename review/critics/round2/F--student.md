## Patterns

1. A pointer with no clear owner ("it," "there," "his," "the lecture," "the objection," "the same year," "as much"). 11 entries. See week5.reading.4.paras.1 and week6.questions.2.auden.paras.3.
2. A person, work or term not introduced at its first mention in the week (Mozart, Tamino and Papageno, Polonius, Kierkegaard's term, Goethe, the statue, Leporello, Oedipus and Macbeth, Montaigne, Isherwood, Tennyson). 11 entries. See week5.reading.4.paras.2 and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.2.
3. Two entries that disagree, or seem to because nothing reconciles them (Papageno fails or refuses; Caliban's counterpart; December and October; every part or sixteen boxes; *Faust* left out and listed). 5 entries. See week5.making.1.table.key.intro.1 and week5.making.1.rules.4.
4. A report of Auden that I could not connect to the question, or could not follow without the source in front of me. 5 entries. See week5.reading.4.auden.paras.2 and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.3.
5. A lecture, memoir, essay or review named only by its year or a short title, so that I could not find it. 5 entries. See week6.questions.5.auden.paras.3 and week6.questions.4.auden.paras.4.
6. An instruction that leaves out something I need in order to carry it out (where to hear the opera, which boxes may stay empty, which cantos are open, whether to name the saying). 4 entries. See week6.intro.4 and week6.making.1.rules.3.
7. The paraphrase borrowing "one," or using "his" for a person in general. 4 instances in 3 entries. See week6.questions.2.auden.paras.3 and week6.questions.3.auden.paras.1.
8. A verb of saying missing where the judgment is Auden's, or repeated in the same place in consecutive sentences. 3 entries. See week5.questions.1.auden.paras.1 and week5.questions.3.auden.paras.3.
9. The site speaking of itself where a plain instruction would do. 2 entries. See week5.questions.2.auden.paras.4 and week6.questions.5.auden.paras.3.
10. The same thing said twice in neighboring entries. 2 entries. See week5.intro.2 and week5.making.1.brief.3.
