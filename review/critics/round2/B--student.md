# Part B, read as the student who has to use it (round 2)

Both pages can be followed, and the tone is right throughout: nothing chummy, nothing that plays at 1941. What gets in the way is mostly this: things a student needs are missing or left to another page, statements do not agree with each other or with the rest of the site, and names and terms arrive before they are explained. I checked the counts and rules against the other pages of the site where a finding depended on them, and I say so where I did.

## Patterns

Counts are of findings, not of single words.

1. A pointer with no clear owner, or one word doing duty for two things (10). "The rest", "the two", "he" after Pearce's name, "work", "the reading list", "Nine", "those". See page:about.lede and page:about.sections.5.list.7.
2. Something the student needs is missing here or stands only on another page (9). Who picks the Greek play, whether the two marked books are optional, that the site has no recordings and that they are sung in another language, what to do about the play that is not printed, which cantos can be learned in prose. See page:syllabus.kinds.required.intro.1 and page:about.sections.7.paras.1.
3. A name or term used before it is explained, or never explained (8). The spring course, the Saturday class, Ansen, Spears, Mandelbaum, Bader, Bloomfield and Mendelson, "search summary". See page:about.sections.1.table.rows.18.1 and page:about.sections.4.table.rows.6.3.
4. Two statements that do not agree, on this page or between this page and another (6). Four of the five against four of the seven; nothing recorded against little recorded; one book not printed against two; two note-takers against three; two page ranges for one examination. See page:about.sections.2.paras.4 and page:about.sections.5.list.4.
5. The British "set" for assigned or required, against house rule 4 (2). See page:about.sections.4.table.rows.4.3 and page:about.sections.7.paras.3.
6. A series or a run of figures too tangled to hold at one reading (2). See page:syllabus.intro.3 and page:about.sections.6.paras.2.
7. One statement made twice a few lines apart (2). The paragraph on kinds of work and the last two rows of its table; that paragraph and the third item of the Supplied list. See page:about.sections.4.paras.1 and page:about.sections.5.list.3.
8. Table cells of more than one sentence with no final period (1 finding, 9 cells). See page:about.sections.1.table.rows.2.2 and page:about.sections.1.table.rows.6.2.

Nothing in the part scolds, cheers, jokes or plays at being Auden. I found no em-dash, exclamation mark or rhetorical question, and no person in general called "he".
