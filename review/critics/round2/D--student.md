## Week 1

### week1.intro.1
- where: The *Divine Comedy* is first on the list
- problem: The first sentence of the course does not say who wrote the book or what kind of book it is (rule 11), and its "it" can be taken for the list, as if the examination covered every book. The translation Auden assigned is not named, so I cannot match it to the versions offered on the By heart page.
- fix: Dante's *Divine Comedy* is first on Auden's reading list, and it is the only book the final examination covers. It is a poem of a hundred cantos in three parts, written in Italian in the early 1300s. This week read the first two parts, the *Inferno* and the *Purgatorio*, which make sixty-seven cantos. Read them in the prose translation by Carlyle, Okey and Wicksteed, the one Auden assigned, or in the verse translation of 1867 by the American poet Longfellow.
- weight: should

### week1.intro.2
- where: Start learning *Inferno* III by heart
- problem: It does not say where the learning is done, or that a version must be chosen first. The By heart page offers three versions, the Italian among them, and says that work begun in one is not carried over to another, so a wrong start is lost work.
- fix: On the first day open the By heart page, choose the version you will learn in (the prose, Longfellow's verse or the Italian) and begin *Inferno* III. Lines learned in one version are not credited in another. The whole canto is due when week 3 ends.
- weight: should

A recording of either will do." Then cut the second sentence of week1.reading.2.paras.3, which this replaces.

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.1
- where: He names *Orpheus* in two pieces of 1948
- problem: The first sentence says that nothing has been found and the second that Auden names the opera twice, which stopped me. Then one of the two pieces is named and the other never is, so I waited for a second that did not come. He names it in two pieces of 1948, an essay about Greek writers and a *Vogue* article on opera, each time only to mark where a period begins. The essay sets the seventy-five years of Athenian drama beside [quotation kept] and ranks the later age higher. He counts that age from Gluck's *Orpheus* in 1762 to Verdi's *Otello* in 1887, and all nine operas on the list had their first performances inside it. What follows is about opera in general. Cut the sentence on the *Divine Comedy* here, or move it to a note on Dante.
- weight: must

### week1.reading.2.auden.paras.2

In [title kept], an essay in that book, he says that those years were also the great age of liberal humanism, when freedom and progress were believed in without question. Where this fix says "great age", keep the term the paragraph now uses. Leave the remaining sentences as they are.

## Week 2

### week2.intro.1

- problem: The paragraph names neither play, and Horace is given no introduction anywhere in the week (rule 11). It does not say that the play is chosen in the reading list or that the test follows the choice, which the program does.
