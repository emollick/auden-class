# V4, faithful to the sources: weeks 8 to 10

Second-round check of every entry in V4.md that reports what Auden or Donald Pearce wrote, said or did. Nothing is copied from a source or from the site's quotations. Evidence is a file and line numbers. For `pearce.txt`, which is three long lines, L1@n, L3@n and L5@n mean line 1, 3 or 5 at character n. "Mendelson" is `research/F_work/mendelson2017.txt`. "Full-text search" means the public search of archive.org inside one scanned book, which returns a line or so round a phrase; I used it only where the notes did not settle a point.

Findings: 24 (must 0, should 12, could 12).

## Patterns

1. A small detail changed in the rewording: 9 entries. Who failed, what the craving is called, three aims for four (twice), "wrote" for "published" (three times), which sentence comes before which, "the same" for "similar". See `week8.questions.1.auden.paras.2` and `week9.questions.2.auden.paras.1`. 2. Runs one word short of the limit: 8 places. Six words in a row are the source's, or a clause keeps the source's order with a word changed. None reaches seven, so none is a finding, but the two that come nearest to a whole sentence of the source are worth a look: `week9.questions.1.auden.paras.1` (the comparison of the habit to two others, dh.txt 7024–7025) and `week9.questions.2.auden.paras.2` (the definition of the sense of sin, dh.txt 7590–7591). The others: `week9.questions.1.auden.paras.2` (dh.txt 7184), `week10.reading.3.auden.paras.3` (dh.txt 22262), `week10.questions.1.auden.paras.3` (dh.txt 5646–5647), `week10.questions.3.auden.paras.3` (ef.txt 6136–6137), `week8.making.1.brief.1` (the first two aims), `week8.questions.1.auden.paras.3` (the device of the attendant).
3. Silent changes at the edge of a quotation: 7 places. Every word matches, but a capital is put on the first word, a comma is added where the quotation is broken, or the closing mark differs from the source's. See `week8.reading.2.auden.paras.2` (capital on the first word, a comma after the second word, and a period at the end where the source has a semicolon, dh.txt 5811–5813) and `week9.reading.2.auden.paras.3` (a comma closing the first half of the split quotation, where the source has no mark, dh.txt 22206–22207). The others close on a mark the source does not have at that point: `week8.reading.2.auden.paras.3`, `week8.questions.2.auden.paras.2` (the first quotation), `week9.reading.2.auden.paras.2`, `week9.questions.2.text` and `week10.questions.2.auden.paras.4`. These are ordinary American conventions. Flag only, in case the editor holds quotations to the source's own marks.
4. A connective or a qualifier supplied or dropped: 5 entries. See `week8.questions.1.auden.paras.4` (two uses of "because") and `week10.questions.2.auden.paras.3` (the speaker's own doubt left out). Also `week8.reading.2.auden.paras.4`, `week9.questions.1.auden.paras.3`, `week10.questions.2.auden.paras.4`.
5. Seven or more of the source's words in a row outside quotation marks: 4 entries. See `week8.questions.2.auden.paras.3` and `week10.questions.2.auden.paras.3`. The other two are a section heading (`week8.reading.2.auden.paras.3`) and the novel's own formula (`week8.questions.1.auden.paras.6`).
6. The site's own step not marked as the site's: 2 entries, `week9.reading.2.auden.paras.3` and `week9.questions.1.auden.paras.4`.
7. A negative about the whole record where the notes show only that nothing was found: 2 entries, `week10.questions.2.auden.paras.1` and `week10.making.1.source.1`.
8. Citation: 2 entries. One page range misses its passage (`week10.questions.1.auden.sources.2`), and one statement has no source named for it (`week8.questions.1.auden.paras.7`). Every other citation in the part has the right title, date and pages.
