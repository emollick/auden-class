# Part A, machine-made writing: findings

Most of this part is clean. The labels, buttons and counters are plain and I pass over them. What follows is in the order of the text.

### ui:home.lede
- where: nearly all of it is of kinds he gave
- problem: The join is "and" where the sense is a contrast, the plural "of kinds" is awkward, and "own" sets Auden's classes against nobody else's. The sentence before has the same build (clause, comma, "and", clause), so the two read as a matched pair.
- fix: The weekly work was made for this site, but nearly all of it is of a kind he gave his classes.
- weight: should

### ui:home.what.1
- where: One student remembered him adding that they would be
- problem: The lede has just used "as one student remembered it" for the examination. The Sources page gives that to Pearce and this remark to Miller, so one anonymous frame used twice in adjacent paragraphs merges two witnesses into one. The frame comes a third time in ui:question.intro.1.
- fix: Begin the sentence "Another student remembered him adding that" and leave the rest as it is. (The Sources page says Miller took the course, so "student" holds.)
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.1
- where: words removed, nearly always from the week's reading
- problem: The phrase about the week's reading hangs on "removed", so the sentence says the words were taken from the reading when it means the passage was.
- fix: First sentence: "A passage, nearly always from the week's reading, with some of its words removed." The second sentence stays.
- weight: should

### ui:home.how.list.5
- where: Each week asks from two to five.
- problem: A week does not ask, and "from two to five" must reach back to the italic head for its noun. This is plainness pushed until it is mannered.
- fix: *A question.* Each week has from two to five questions. Answer one, in at least 150 words.
- weight: could

### ui:home.how.after.1
- where: The term ends with the examination: five cantos of
- problem: The colon names the examination and then delivers the figures, which is the staged reveal of house rule 3. It is also one of six sentence-colons in the running prose of this one page (ui:home.what.2, ui:home.how.1, ui:home.how.list.6, this entry, ui:enrol.intro.1, ui:enrol.intro.2), with three more in the notes under the form. Three of the six introduce true lists. The other three are a habit.
- fix: The term ends with the examination, in which you write out five cantos of Dante, about 700 lines of verse, from memory in three hours.
- weight: could

### ui:home.how.after.2
- where: Some of this is documented and some is this
- problem: This is the third statement of one disclaimer on one page. The lede has said which parts are Auden's and which the site's, and the footer under the same page (ui:colophon.1) sends the reader to Sources in nearly the same words. The first sentence is also a mirrored "some, and some" that names nothing.
- fix: cut
- weight: should

### ui:home.before.body
- where: The first week's books are listed, if you want
- problem: Nothing is listed on this page. Under the sentence there are only two buttons, so "are listed" sends the student looking for a list. The comma before "if" turns a plain offer into an aside.
- fix: {n} day to go. You can open week 1 now if you want to start the reading early.|{n} days to go. You can open week 1 now if you want to start the reading early.
- weight: should

### ui:enrol.intro.2
- where: Nothing is sent anywhere. Everything you do here
- problem: One fact is given three ways in two sentences (sent nowhere, kept in this browser, nowhere else), and the footer a few lines below (ui:colophon.2) repeats seven of the same words exactly. The advice on how often to save also differs from ui:notebook.keep.1.
- fix: Replace the first two sentences with: "Everything you do here stays in this browser: what you have read, your scores and your papers." The third sentence stays.
- weight: should

### ui:unit.omit
- where: copy of the list that has been published
- problem: The relative clause is a long way round to "the published copy". It also puts two nouns between "this book" and the "It" that opens the next sentence, so the pointer lands on the list first (house rule 10).
- fix: Someone wrote [quotation kept] beside this book on the published copy of the list. The book is left out of the week's page count, no test depends on it, and no question on it is required.
- weight: should

### ui:unit.words.what
- where: Do it before you open the book.
- problem: "it" has nothing to point to but "a passage", and a passage is not done. The same loose "Do it" closes ui:test.intro.1 and comes again in ui:words.intro.1.
- fix: A passage with words taken out. Fill the gaps before you open the book.
- weight: should

### ui:words.intro.1
- where: your words are fixed and cannot be changed
- problem: "fixed" and "cannot be changed" say one thing twice, and "fixed" can be read as "put right". In the second sentence "stands in the book" is a bookish turn for an American handbook; the same verb returns in ui:making.emend.how and ui:making.emend.field.
- fix: Second sentence: "In each gap write the word you think the book has." Last sentence: "When you press the button below, your words can no longer be changed."
- weight: could

### ui:test.intro.1
- where: A name or a few words is enough, and
- problem: The two halves pull against each other (a short answer will do, the spelling must be exact), so "and" is the wrong join. It reads as two sentences stitched together in revision. The closing "Do it" has no noun in the paragraph to point to.
- fix: Last two sentences: "A name or a few words is enough, but names must be spelled correctly. Take the test without the books."
- weight: should

### ui:test.source
- where: Monroe Spears reports that Auden's examinations at Swarthmore College
- problem: The name comes with no word of who he is, and this note is printed under the test in every week (house rule 11).
- fix: Begin "The critic Monroe Spears reports that" and leave the rest, the quotation included.
- weight: could

### ui:question.intro.1
- where: that this was what they would be tested on
- problem: "this" has to gather up the whole instruction before it, and the reader cannot tell whether it means the word count, the book or the thinking (house rule 10). "One of the class" is also a second name for a witness the home page calls "one student".
- fix: Middle sentence: "One student remembered Auden saying that the class would be tested on what they thought of the books." The first and third sentences stay.
- weight: should

### ui:making.notyet
- where: The rules marked with a cross above are not
- problem: "A cross" for the mark of an unmet rule is British usage. An American student calls that mark an X, and the site elsewhere says "Check the box" in the American way (ui:unit.opera.after).
- fix: The rules marked with an X above are not yet met.
- weight: could

### ui:making.check.endWords.ok
- where: The end-words repeat as the form requires.
- problem: Hyphenated compounds that American usage writes open give the text an antique, British look: this one, "line-endings" (ui:making.rhyme.words, ui:making.rhyme.note), "Word-list" (ui:making.translate.words) and "blue-books" (ui:final.intro.2).
- fix: The end words repeat as the form requires. Likewise "line endings", "Word list" and "blue books". Flag only for the last if the hyphen is Pearce's own.
- weight: could

### ui:heart.intro.2
- where: learn long stretches of poetry by heart
- problem: Further down the same page "stretch" becomes the site's own unit of learning, a few lines long, so one word carries two senses on one page. The paragraph also states what Auden did and names no source, where the sibling notes (ui:words.source, ui:test.source) name one.
- fix: Change "stretches" to "passages", and add at the end: "See [Sources](#/about)."
- weight: could

### ui:heart.write.intro.1
- where: at the text until you have compared.
- problem: "compared" has lost its object, and the reader must find the button to learn what is compared with what. In the last sentence it is the comparison that is "over the limit", when the limit is on wrong words.
- fix: Third sentence: "Do not look at the text until you have pressed “Compare with the text.”" Last sentence: "Each comparison is recorded, and if more words are wrong than the limit allows, the canto comes due again."
- weight: should

### ui:final.intro.1
- where: what the final would be: a written examination
- problem: Two faults. The opening announces ("what the final would be") and then reveals after a colon, against house rule 3, in a sentence of fifty-one words. Then three sentences in a row have one build (clause, comma, "and", clause), and the shortest of them is set last, so the paragraph ends on a beat.
- fix: In the first sentence write "told the class that the final would be a written examination", end the sentence after "vacation", and begin a new one "They would write out"; the rest is unchanged. Begin the sentence about the drive "Before he drove to California for Christmas he promised to telephone" and leave the rest. With that sentence recast, the last one can stay.
- weight: should

### ui:final.rules.4
- where: Capitals, punctuation and line breaks do not count.
- problem: The same rule in ui:heart.rule.1 lists accents as well. A student who writes the Italian cannot tell whether accents count at the examination.
- fix: flag only. If accents are ignored here too, write "Capitals, punctuation, accents and line breaks do not count."
- weight: could

### ui:notebook.keep.2
- where: keeps everything and can be restored
- problem: The subject of the first sentence is a button's name, and a button cannot be restored. The grammar looks as if a noun was swapped for the label in revision.
- fix: First sentence: "[quotation kept] puts everything into one file, which can be restored later." The other sentences stay.
- weight: could

### ui:notebook.text
- where: Download the papers as plain text
- problem: The file holds the replies, exercises and notes as well as the papers, and house rule 4 keeps "paper" for the weekly essay.
- fix: Download your work as plain text
- weight: could

### index.html (fourth entry under this label, the notice shown without JavaScript)

- problem: The site explains itself in a list of three and gives the reader nothing to do. A person who sees this notice needs one instruction.

## Patterns

1. A sentence built as clause, comma, "and", clause: 24 of about 200 full sentences. One by one they are harmless. The fault is in the clusters and in the places where "and" does the work of "but". There are three in a row in ui:final.intro.1 and two in a row in ui:home.lede and in ui:reader.unverified. The frame "and the Progress page" with a verb after it closes a sentence three times (ui:home.how.list.2, ui:home.after.body.1, ui:test.intro.2).
2. Bookish or British turns in a handbook meant to be American: 10. Hyphenated compounds, 6 (ui:making.rhyme.note, ui:final.intro.2). "Stand" for a word in a text, 3 (ui:words.intro.1, ui:making.emend.how). "A cross", 1 (ui:making.notyet). "The whole of it" in ui:heart.how.1 and ui:heart.write.intro.1 is of the same family and I have not counted it.
3. A statement closed with a colon and then filled in: 10 in running prose, six of them on the opening page (ui:home.how.1, ui:enrol.intro.1). The colons before true lists are fine. Those that explain or define can be two sentences, or one sentence without the colon.
4. A pronoun with no clear owner: 5. "Do it" three times (ui:unit.words.what, ui:test.intro.1), "this" in ui:question.intro.1, "It" in ui:unit.omit.
5. One statement made two or three times on one page: 5 sentences in 2 clusters, both on the opening page. What is documented and what is the site's (ui:home.how.after.2, ui:colophon.1, and the lede). Where the work is kept (ui:enrol.intro.2, ui:colophon.2).
6. The unnamed witness, "one student remembered": 3 (ui:home.what.1, ui:question.intro.1, and the lede), for at least two different people.

Not found in this part: em-dashes, exclamation marks, rhetorical questions, hedges, hype words, or runs of three short sentences of one shape. The confirmation prompts are real questions.
