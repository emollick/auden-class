# Part J (week 13). Lens: writing that reads as machine-made

### week13.intro.1
- where: each is asked the same question, whether she
- problem: This clause, thirteen words long, stands again word for word in week13.making.1.brief.1, where the exercise needs it. Here it only delays the one thing the student wants from the paragraph, which is where the chase comes.
- fix: Replace the second and third sentences with: "The Pequod meets five more whaling ships on the way, and the chase fills the last three chapters."

### week13.intro.3

### week13.questions.1.gloss.1

- weight: should

### week13.questions.1.auden.title
- where: about the Rachel, and about the end
- problem: "The end" has no owner (the book's, the voyage's, Ahab's), and the comma sets it off as an afterthought. The same comma stands in week13.questions.3.auden.title.

- weight: could

### week13.questions.1.auden.paras.1
- where: against the kind of tragedy Ahab belongs to
- problem: The article's own name for the second sort of tragedy is withheld and a roundabout phrase put in its place, though the article's title has just given the word. Two sentences on, "was never unlimited" is a double negative for a plain statement.
- fix: Replace the second sentence with: "The article sets Greek tragedy against Christian tragedy, the kind to which Ahab belongs. In the first the spectator pities what had to be, and in the second the pity is that it could all have gone otherwise." In the third sentence change "was never unlimited" to "had limits".
- weight: should

### week13.questions.1.auden.paras.2
- where: Auden calls the refusal of the Rachel
- problem: Five of the six sentences carry a verb of saying, and "Auden calls" closes one sentence and opens the next. An act cannot be a counterfeit "of the saying"; it counterfeits what the saying asks. "The saying" comes twice and is sourced only the second time, "shall" is mock-antique, and the last sentence runs to 46 words with two more verbs of saying.
- fix: In the pipe sentence change "shall" to "will". End the Pip sentence at "himself." and go on: "Auden calls that act an inversion of the Gospel saying that whoever is greatest must be as the least. By the same reading the refusal of the Rachel is a counterfeit of what is asked of a disciple, who must hate father and mother, wife and children." End the last sentence at its quotation and start a new one, "As a result pleasure and pain change places," running on as now, without "and says that".
- weight: should

### week13.questions.1.auden.paras.3
- where: is no chorus of average men
- problem: House rule 12. This is paraphrase, and the chorus stands for people in general. "Is no chorus" is also a literary turn where the handbook's plain negative would do.
- fix: "But Ishmael, he says, is not a chorus of average people."
- weight: could

### week13.questions.1.auden.paras.4
- where: and although Starbuck’s plea in Chapter 132 comes
- problem: Two "although" clauses are hung on one main clause, the mark of two sentences joined in revision, and "four chapters later" works out a sum the two chapter numbers already show.
- fix: "Auden sets the last chance for Ahab at the Rachel, in Chapter 128. Yet by his own account the past can always be redeemed, and Starbuck's plea comes later, in Chapter 132." The sentence about the reply stands.
- weight: should

### week13.questions.2.auden.paras.1
- where: was best reflected in this movement
- problem: The second sentence is 49 words long and puts 25 words, among them a quotation and two asides, between its subject phrase and its verb; the reader has to go back. "Castle" comes twice in seven words, "a thing of that kind" is vague, and in the first sentence the tacked-on clause about Pearce leaves "what he said" open to mean Pearce.
- fix: "Donald Pearce, a student in the class, reports that Auden talked as he worked the record onto the turntable of his portable phonograph, which he often brought with him. This movement, Auden said, best reflected the relation of an individual to fate, as far as music can express such a relation. He named two cases, the relation [quotation kept] and the relation between K. and the Castle in the novel of that name by Franz Kafka, which you read next."
- weight: must

### week13.questions.2.auden.paras.2
- where: Then Auden announced a recess of ten minutes.
- problem: House rule 8. An eight-word sentence is set after the quoted close of the music so that the paragraph ends on a deadpan beat.
- fix: Open the paragraph: "Auden gave no answer to his own question. He started the record, and when it was over he announced a recess of ten minutes." Then give Pearce's description as it stands, so that the paragraph ends on his words about the close.
- weight: could

### week13.questions.2.auden.paras.3
- where: it had all been done very simply, since
- problem: House rule 10. "It" has no owner anywhere in the block. "Since" offers as a cause what is the manner, "and that was all" is a tag kept for the cadence, and "he is certain real understanding" misreads for a moment as "a certain real understanding".
- fix: "Looking back, Pearce says that Auden had done the whole thing very simply, by putting two novels and a piece of music next to each other in the students' minds." In the second sentence add "that" after "certain".
- weight: should

### week13.questions.2.auden.paras.4
- where: Because Auden gave no answer to his question
- problem: The clause repeats the opening of week13.questions.2.auden.paras.2, two paragraphs back, almost word for word. The first sentence tells what happened before the scene in the block's first paragraph, so it arrives out of order and after Pearce has already spoken of "two novels".
- fix: Move the first sentence to the head of the block as "Auden had just assigned *The Castle*, the novel by Franz Kafka that you read next, for the following Tuesday." (The Kafka clause in the fix for the first paragraph then shortens to "in that novel".) Begin this paragraph "The questions Auden gave out with *The Castle* are in weeks 14 and 15." Cut the "Because" clause and start the last sentence at "Compare your paper".
- weight: should

### week13.questions.3.gloss.1
- where: which you see after you hand in
- problem: The second sentence carries two asides and six commas. One aside explains a routine the student knows well by week 13, which is the site talking about itself.
- fix: Replace the second sentence with: "Any three will do, but Auden's own remarks are on the mates (Starbuck, Stubb and Flask) and on Pip." The first and last sentences stand.
- weight: should

### week13.questions.3.auden.paras.1
- where: which he calls a kind of cowardice
- problem: The second sentence attributes twice ("he says," "which he calls") directly after a sentence built on "Auden says," and "a kind of" is the hedge that house rule 3 bars. In the last sentence nothing marks the voyage as a figure of speech, so it reads as the Pequod's.
- fix: In the second sentence cut "he says," and change "a kind of" to "a form of". Last sentence: flag only. The editor should add, from the page cited, a few words that say what voyage Auden means.
- weight: should

### week13.questions.3.auden.paras.2
- where: Of the three, only he suspects that
- problem: House rule 6 is broken in the third sentence, which gives a judgment with no verb of saying in it or in the sentence before. The last two sentences then attribute back to back, where one would cover both.
- fix: In the third sentence write "Of the three, he says, only Starbuck suspects". In the last sentence cut ", Auden says,".
- weight: could

### week13.questions.3.auden.paras.3
- where: Auden says that Stubb reads in the coin
- problem: All four sentences attribute, and "Auden says" stands in two sentences running, though the first already covers the second. "Who wrote the novel you read next" is coy about a title the student is about to need, and "need not have had" is a stiff way to say what the plain verb says.
- fix: Cut "Auden says that" and begin the second sentence at "Stubb reads". In the last sentence change "who wrote the novel you read next" to "the author of *The Castle*, which you read next"; cut "kind of" before "suffering"; for "need not have had" put "could have avoided".
- weight: should

### week13.questions.3.auden.paras.5
- where: Pip the strength of Ahab and Ahab the
- problem: The last sentence makes its point twice, once in general terms and once crossed over name by name, so the block closes on a chiasmus. Three sentences running attribute ("he says," "Auden says," "In Auden's view"). The clause that introduces Kierkegaard also lets the student think the two despairs come from the book read in week 6; they are from *The Sickness unto Death*.
- fix: Third sentence: "Each has met a catastrophe, but Ahab's madness is turned on the whale and Pip's on himself." Last sentence: "Both would have been saved, in Auden's view, if Pip had possessed Ahab's strength and Ahab Pip's humility." Kierkegaard: flag only; the editor should decide whether to name the source of the two despairs.
- weight: should

### week13.questions.4.auden.paras.1

- fix: Replace the first two sentences with: "Auden took from Chapter 25 of the *Education* the title of [title kept], an essay in his 1962 collection *The Dyer's Hand*. In it he turns Adams's two figures to his own use."
- weight: should

### week13.questions.4.auden.paras.3
- where: Auden then corrects Adams, for whom the Virgin
- problem: "Corrects" decides in the site's own voice that Auden is right and Adams wrong. In the last sentence six words part "puts" from "down to," and "nostalgia for Chartres over Chicago" is not idiom: a person prefers one thing over another but is nostalgic for one.
- fix: First sentence: "Auden then disagrees with Adams," and on as now. Last sentence: "He says that Adams's nostalgic preference for Chartres over Chicago was aestheticism: Adams thought the disguise prettier than the thing, [quotation kept]"
- weight: should
