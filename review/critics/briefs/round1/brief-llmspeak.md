# Your lens: writing that reads as machine-made

Read every sentence of your part. For each one ask two things. Would a careful person writing a course handbook have written this sentence? Does it do work for the student? You are looking for:

1. Stock phrases and signposts. Sentences that announce, frame, sum up or reassure and do not say a thing.
2. Reflex symmetry. "Not X but Y," "less X than Y," "X, and not Y," used for the rhythm when nobody holds X. Sentences built in mirrored pairs. Lists of exactly three by habit.
3. Endings that pose. An entry or paragraph that closes on an aphorism, a neat reversal, a moral, or a short sentence added for effect.
4. Inflated or vague diction. Abstract nouns that could be cut. Verbs such as explore, navigate, underscore, highlight, capture, embody. Words such as rich, deep, powerful, crucial, journey, lens, landscape. "A kind of," "a sense of."
5. Padding. A sentence that repeats the one before in other words. A clause that tells the student what he will feel or find. The obvious explained.
6. Mannered plainness. The writer was told to be plain and brief and may have overdone it. Look for runs of clipped sentences all of one shape, a staccato that calls attention to itself, a mock-antique or gnomic tone, coyness. Say where plainness has become a pose, and where two or three short sentences ought simply to be one.
7. Sameness across entries. Every paragraph opening the same way. One sentence frame used again and again. The same closing move in entry after entry. Count them.
8. Hedging, throat-clearing, apology, and the site talking about itself more than the student needs.
9. Unearned authority. Confident claims with nothing behind them. Evaluative adjectives offered as fact.
10. Anything else that made you think a machine wrote it. Trust the reaction and say what set it off.

Do not flag plain statements of fact, instructions that are simply clear, the set questions, or quotations.

For each finding write the replacement, and make sure the replacement does not commit a different fault from the list. Where the right fix is to cut, say cut.

Read the whole part once before you write anything, so that you can see what recurs. Then go through it entry by entry. Do not skip the short entries.
