## What to do

- Read every sentence on your list. Report on a sentence that is not on the list only when a listed sentence beside it has broken it: a pronoun that now points at the wrong owner, a word now repeated, two sentences that now say the same thing, an instruction now contradicted.
- Report only "must" and "should". Leave out anything that is only taste. The editor will act on what you report with no further reading, so a finding that is only taste does harm, and so does a fix that is worse than what it replaces.
- Look hardest for the tracks that a repair leaves: grammar broken where a phrase was swapped in, a pronoun cut loose from its noun, a verb of saying missing or wedged in twice, a name with no introduction, a pointer to another page that does not say where to look, a sentence that now disagrees with its neighbor, and any phrase that reads as written by a machine.
- This is a read of the prose and nothing else. Do not check the sentences against sources, scans or the program. Other checkers have done that, and the facts are not yours to reopen. Do not open any file under `research/`.
- Entries whose label contains `.glossary.` are lines of a word list for a translation exercise (a word of the poem, then its dictionary form and meanings). Read them only for sense and for a consistent form.
- Words in braces, such as {pages}, are filled in by the program with a number or a date. A bar | separates the singular form from the plural.
- Do not quote any source. Do not write out more of the site's text than the sentence you report on.
- Decisions that are closed, which you must not reopen: the formula "a student in the class who wrote down after lectures what he could remember" for Donald Pearce; the form “Title,” an essay in *The Dyer’s Hand* (1962); the words "As this site reads" and "this site's" for the site's own readings; American spelling.

## The report

Write the report to the file named in your task, and append each finding to it as soon as you have it, so that nothing is lost if you are stopped. For each finding give: the label; the first words of the sentence; the problem in a sentence or two; the sentence as it should read, in full, so that the editor can paste it; and the weight, "must" or "should".

If every sentence on your list is sound, say so and stop. A short honest report is what is wanted. End with one line: how many sentences you read, and how many findings.
