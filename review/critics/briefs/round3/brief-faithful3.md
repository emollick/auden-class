# The last check against the sources

This is the third and last round. In the second round checkers compared every report of Auden and of Donald Pearce with the scans, and editors revised the text to their findings and to the findings of two other kinds of critic. Rewording is where a report drifts from its source, and the reworded sentences have not been checked by anyone but the editor who wrote them. You check them now.

Read first, from the folder `review/r2/` of the scratchpad: `brief-common.md` (what the site is, the labels, how to report, the rules for your own writing) and then `brief-faithful.md` (how to check a statement against a source, where the sources are and how to read page numbers off them, and the extra rules for your own writing). All of it applies to you, with the changes below.

## What is different in this round

- Your part prints whole weeks so that you can read each changed entry in its place. An entry whose label is followed by the word NEW was written or changed in the last revision. Inside a NEW entry the sentences that are new stand between the marks ⟦ and ⟧. The marks are not on the site.
- Check every marked sentence that says what Auden, Pearce or any other writer wrote, said or did, and every marked source line. Do not recheck unmarked sentences: they were checked in the second round. Where a marked sentence only makes sense with the unmarked one before it, read both and report on the marked one.
- The second-round reports are in `review/r2/out/` (`V1--faithful.md` to `V6--faithful.md`). Many marked sentences are the editors' answers to findings there. Where a marked sentence answers a finding, look at the finding: it gives the scan file and line. Confirm that the new sentence now agrees with the source, and that the editor has not overcorrected.
- Each editor recorded, in a file `notes.md` under `edit2/w<week>/` in the scratchpad, the scan file and line against which each changed sentence was checked. Use those as leads, not as proof.
- The editors also added plain facts that no scan under `research/` covers: a date, a first name, a title, the year of a first performance, who translated what. Check every such fact in a marked sentence too. Use the site's own files, the research notes, and where those are silent a reliable public source on the web (a library catalogue, the Internet Archive's record of a book, a standard reference work). Give the source you used. Report a fact as a finding only if it is wrong, or if you could find nothing that supports it.
- Editors' notes for weeks 1 and 2 are in `edit2/w01/notes.md`, and for weeks 3 and 4 in `edit2/w03/notes.md`. Weeks 5 and 6 were revised by the chief editor and have no notes file; for those two weeks the second-round reports are your only leads.
- Report only "must" and "should". The editor will act on what you report without a further round.
- If every marked sentence in your part agrees with its source, say so and stop.

End the report with the section "Checked and right" as the second-round brief describes, covering the marked sentences only, and one last line: how many marked sentences you checked, and how many findings.
