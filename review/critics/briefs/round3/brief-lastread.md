# The last read

This is the third and last round. In the second round three kinds of critic read every word of the site, and editors revised it to their findings. Every revision makes sentences that no critic has seen. Those sentences are what you read now.

Read these first, in this order, from the folder `review/r2/` of the scratchpad:
1. `brief-common.md`: what the site is, the voice it is meant to have, the house rules, how to read the labels, how to report, and the rules for your own writing. All of it applies to you, with the changes below.
2. `brief-llmspeak.md`: the first of your two lenses.
3. `brief-student.md`: the second of your two lenses.

You read with both lenses at once.

## What is different in this round

- Your part prints a whole section of the site so that you can read each changed entry in its place. An entry whose label is followed by the word NEW was written or changed in the last revision. Inside a NEW entry the sentences that are new stand between the marks ⟦ and ⟧. (The marks are not on the site. Where two marked sentences are neighbors, each has its own pair of marks.)
- Report on the marked sentences. Read every one. Report on an unmarked sentence only when a marked sentence beside it has broken it: a pronoun that now points at the wrong owner, a word now repeated, two sentences that now say the same thing, an instruction now contradicted. An entry marked "NEW (reordered)" has old sentences in a new order or joined differently; read it whole.
- Report only "must" and "should". Leave out anything you would have marked "could". The editor will act on what you report without a further round, so a finding that is only taste does harm.
- Look hardest for the tracks that a revision leaves: a sentence whose grammar broke when a phrase was swapped in; a pronoun cut loose from its noun; a verb of saying wedged into sentence after sentence; a name introduced twice, or not at all because its introduction was cut elsewhere; the same repair made the same way many times; a sentence that now disagrees with its neighbor; two instructions for one thing; a fact stated in one entry and contradicted in another.
- The page itself supplies some things that your part does not show. Do not report their absence: under each opera the page tells the student to read the libretto and then listen to a complete recording, and says the site has no recordings; under a book marked "Omit" it says so; the test prints author and title before the `where` line of a passage; the `where` line of the missing-words passage is not shown to the student at all.
- If every marked sentence in your part is sound, say so and stop. A short honest report is what is wanted.

End the report with one line: how many marked sentences you read, and how many findings.
