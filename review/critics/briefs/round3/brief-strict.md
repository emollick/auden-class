# Read this before anything else

A checker who had this task before you was stopped by the system in the middle of the work, and everything it had not yet saved was lost. It was stopped because it began to write out the words of a work that is in copyright. To finish the task you must work as follows.

1. Never write out a line, a sentence or a long phrase of any poem, essay, review, lecture note, memoir or book. This holds in your thinking, in a message, in a shell command, in a search pattern, in a script and in your report. It covers everything by Auden, Donald Pearce's essay, Charles Miller's memoir, the modern critics and biographers, every modern translation, and the poems and libretti the site prints.
2. Search the files with one or two plain words at a time: a name, a rare word, a page number. Do not use a quotation from the site's text as a search pattern.
3. When you read a scan, print at most three lines at a time, and do not repeat them back in your own words at length. Decide whether the site's sentence agrees with the place, and write down only the file name, the line numbers and your verdict.
4. In the report never quote a source. Point to a place in a source by file and line number. Point to the site's sentence by its label and its first three or four words.
5. Do not compare texts by printing both side by side. If you want to test whether the site repeats a run of a source's words, write a script that prints only the count of shared words in a row and the position, never the words.
6. Save your report as you go. After each entry you check, append its result to the report file at once, so that nothing is lost if you are stopped.
7. If a step would need you to break one of these rules, skip the step and say in the report which sentence you could not check and why.
