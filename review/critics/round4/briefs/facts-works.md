# Your lens: is it true of the poems, the music, the pictures and the site?

Your business is every statement of fact in the prose file that is not a report of Auden or of a witness to his teaching (another checker has those). Go through the file string by string. Do not sample. In particular:

1. The song at the door. Everything said about *The Tempest* and Ariel's song: the date given for the play, who sings to whom and when, what Ferdinand believes and what is the case, what he says just before the song, what happens by the end of the scene, and each note on a word (the sense of a word in Shakespeare's English, what the *Oxford English Dictionary* shows for the compound in the song, which word rhymes with which, who rings the bell and how often). The song as the site prints it can be seen at http://localhost:8136/#/door , and the play is on the site as the text `tempest` (see item 2 for the tool that prints and searches a text). For the history of a word, use a reliable dictionary or edition on the web.
2. Dante. Every statement about the *Comedy*: which canto and which circle or terrace, who is where and what is done to them, what Virgil explains in *Inferno* XI and *Purgatorio* XVII and how the three kinds of wrong love map to the seven terraces, what is said on the shore in *Purgatorio* I, where Dante looks down in *Paradiso* XXVII, the Italian word the site names and the two places it is used, counts of lines ("fifteen lines earlier", "five months", "three lines that begin…"), and every phrase the site quotes or italicizes from Longfellow's translation, which must match the site's copy letter for letter. Check in the site's own texts: from /home/user/auden-course run `node build/tools/show.mjs dante-longfellow` to list the parts, `node build/tools/show.mjs dante-longfellow inf-26` to print one, and `node build/tools/show.mjs <textId> --grep '<regex>'` to search (text ids are the folder names under site/data/texts; the prose version is `dante-temple`, the Italian is there too if listed). The nine gap notes of the first meeting each describe a word in its line: open http://localhost:8136/#/unit/1/meet/1 , fill the gaps, show the writer's words, and check each note against the word and line it stands beside.

Every statement the site makes about its own contents and behavior (counts of cantos, weeks, meetings, operas, books; "sixty-seven cantos"; "a canto takes about ten minutes"; what is kept where; what a button does; the rule for counting a piece as known). Check counts against /home/user/auden-course/site/data/course.json and the index.json files under site/data/texts, and behavior in the running site.

How to check: the site's own texts and data first; then the web (WebSearch, WebFetch; load them with ToolSearch if they are not in your tool list). Prefer library catalogues, museum pages, standard reference works, scholarly editions and university sites. If the network refuses a site, try another source; do not try to get round the refusal.

Report each statement that is wrong or doubtful in the form the common brief gives. Under "problem" give the evidence: a URL, or a file and line. Under "fix" give the corrected wording. Weight "must" for anything false, "should" for anything you could not confirm after a real attempt, "could" for imprecision. Do not report style. Where the site states as a fact something that is a reading of the poem (an interpretation a careful reader could dispute), report it and say whether the site marks it as its own.

Modern editions, commentaries and reference works are in copyright: describe what they say in your own words and give the URL. Longfellow's translation and the plays are in the public domain, but still do not copy out more than a line of them.

End the file with a section "Checked and right": a plain list of labels with the number of statements checked under each.
