## Figures that stand on one tab (13)

- **allegory**: horace plates[0] ("ship") lenses[1] (tab "Allegory")
  - plain: one thing presented in the words and another in the meaning, usually by a run of metaphors (from the definition of Quintilian, a Roman teacher of rhetoric)

- **antilabe**: traviata plates[2] ("end") lenses[1] (tab "Shared")
  - plain: one line of verse divided between two or more speakers; the pieces together keep the measure

- **climax**: rilke plates[0] ("count") lenses[1] (tab "Figure")
  - plain: Greek for ladder: words or phrases set in rising order, each step greater than the one before

- **correctio**: adams plates[2] ("born") lenses[1] (tab "Tense")
  - plain: a correction made in the open: the writer amends a word as soon as he has used it

- **enumeration**: augustine plates[2] ("late") lenses[2] (tab "Senses")
  - plain: a counting out: one thing told part by part; here, sense by sense

- **epistrophe**: fidelio plates[2] ("name") lenses[0] (tab "Figure")
  - plain: ending one phrase or sentence after another on the same word

- **extended metaphor**: peer-gynt plates[1] ("onion") lenses[0] (tab "Figure")
  - plain: one thing is called another, and the likeness is carried on part by part

- **hypophora**: karamazov plates[0] ("first") lenses[0] (tab "Questions")
  - plain: a speaker asks a question and at once answers it himself

- **isocolon**: carmen plates[1] ("la-bas") lenses[2] (tab "Figure")
  - plain: parts of the same length and the same build, set side by side

- **parody**: faust plates[0] ("heaven") lenses[2] (tab "Parody")
  - plain: the form and wording of a dignified piece closely imitated and made ridiculous by an unfitting subject

- **personification**: adams plates[1] ("sister") lenses[2] (tab "Nature")
  - plain: an idea or a thing spoken of in the words used for a person

- **synchysis**: horace plates[1] ("crossing") lenses[0] (tab "Order")
  - plain: the interlocked order: the adjective of one pair stands between the two words of another pair

- **zeugma**: dante-purgatorio plates[2] ("garden") lenses[1] (tab "Figure")
  - plain: one verb is made to serve two clauses: it is said in the first and left out of the second
