## 6. On a phone: a pleasure or a slog

**A pleasure, down to the foot of the last plate.** The site's first
window is Auden's question and two lines with the beat drawn under
them (`shots/home-top-0`), and a card opens a piece at the plate
itself. On a plate the eye now has one thing to do at a time, in the
order it wants them: the lines, set large, with the marks on them;
straight under them what a stroke, a dot or a color is; under a rule
the reason, in ink; then the same lines another way, one press off,
with no trip back up. `eye2b/v2-hamlet-tobe-press-0` is the picture of
it: ten marked lines, the measure, the key and the button in one
window. He asked to be shown things, and the first window of every
plate is a drawing made on words. Nothing on a plate reads like a
course page.

It is long. Of the 158 tabs photographed at 390 the middle one is
1,701 px tall, two windows; 81 are over two windows and 10 over
three. A piece is twelve windows at the middle (10,036 px, measured on
all 35): a little over one before the first plate, about five of
plates, about five after. The windows of plates do not drag, because
something is drawn in nearly every one.

**Still a slog, in three places.**

1. *The lower half of an opera's Word tab* (fault A): 950 to 1,600 px
   of caption, notes and libretto before the tabs. Worst:
   `plates/traviata-end-0-word-390`, from 1,265 px to 2,871.
2. *The ten plates over three windows.* Eight are tabs of four operas
   (Traviata `end` and `refrain`, Don Giovanni `duet`, The Magic Flute
   `trial`, Carmen `cartes`); the others are Rilke `count` German and
   Baudelaire `evening` French. On the tabs that number two passages
   alike the reader carries the numbers across two windows
   (`plates/don-giovanni-duet-3-english-390`: nine marks from 117 px
   on, the caption and nine remarks after 1,981).
3. *After the last plate.* At the middle a piece runs on for 4,383 px,
   and "Read on" stands 2,501 px under the last plate (Orpheus 686,
   Tristan 3,791): "More from Auden", paragraph and source, paragraph
   and source (`eye2b/v10-purgatorio-garden-beat-2`). This is not the
   phone's doing and not this update's: on a desk Hamlet has 4.3
   windows after its last plate and on a phone 5.3. But it is where a
   phone reader passes three windows of words to reach the link that
   sends them to the book. If he asks again what to change: on a
   narrow window, "Read on" straight under the last plate. That
   changes the order of a page he has approved, so it needs his word.

**Before this goes live: nothing must be mended.** All fourteen
faults of the first round are mended, items 22 to 26 are right, and
the lead's tools for the keys, for Back and for the named words find
no fault. What can wait, in this order: A (fold the libretto on the
ten Word tabs), C (Agamemnon's table at 360), D (a prose version in
the quiet ink). B I would leave.
